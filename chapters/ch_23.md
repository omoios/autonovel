The city of Ouroboros did not rise. It was vomited upward.

Cassian stood on the rusted grating of the Mid-Levels, his boots welded to the floor by the sheer velocity of the ascent. Gravity was a suggestion, ignored by the screaming iron around him. The great ribs of the city hull groaned, a sound like a whale dying in a pressurized tank.

He looked at his hands.

They were gone.

He tried to clench a fist, but the nerve impulse hit a void. No pain. No pressure. Just the terrifying absence of sensation.

"Cassian?"

He drifted downward, passing through the solid rust of the deck plating as if it were smoke.

He saw the girl, Kait, kneeling on the grating below. She was gripping a heavy canvas jacket. *His* jacket. The fabric was greasy, the cuffs frayed.

"Cass," she whispered. She shook the empty sleeves. "Stop it. Wake up."

"Kait," he tried to say.

The air rippled. The sound was a frequency, not a voice. The girl flinched as the wave hit her face. She looked up, her eyes red-rimmed, searching the empty air.

*The past does not get to keep the witness.*

He felt the words more than heard them, a sudden, sharp knowledge blooming behind the eyes he no longer possessed.

He looked up.

Above them, the Spire had shattered the cavern roof.

For three thousand years, the city had lived in the dark, buried under the weight of the world. Now, the stone gave way.

And there was light.

It poured through the ragged hole in the cavern roof. It hit the upper decks of the Spire, blinding and harsh. It was not the warm, yellow light of the fairy tales. It was the cold, blue-white fire of a star that had moved on.

Cassian felt the vibration of the structural failure. He felt it in his essence. The city was waking up, and it was in pain.

He drifted upward, phasing through the iron floors of the Spire.

He emerged in the Command Center.

The rich were panicking.

 Vex did not panic. He audited.

"Report," Vex said. His voice was calm. Surgical. "Why has the ascent rate exceeded city charter parameters?"

A technician—face gray with terror—stammered. "The thrust, sir. It’s... it’s infinite. The Chronite reaction isn't slowing down. It's drawing power from the Mantle itself."

Vex tapped a finger against his porcelain cheek. *Clack. Clack. Clack.*

"Divert the flow," Vex said. "Open the secondary vents."

"The secondary vents are melting, sir!"

"We are breaking free," Vex whispered. He turned to the viewport, staring up at the hole in the world. "The budget is irrelevant. Full output. Burn the reserve tanks."

"But the reserve tanks are full of unstable isotopes," the technician cried. "Year Zero. The Founding Era. If we burn that—"

"Burn it," Vex said. "I will not be dragged back into the dark."

Cassian drifted toward the main Chronite Conduit. A massive pipe, thick as a subway tunnel, ran through the center of the Spire. Inside the pipe, the sludge of compressed time churned.

He felt the resonance of it. It screamed.

He recognized the scream.

He hovered closer. He pressed his faceless presence against the cold metal of the pipe.

Inside the flow, trapped in the sludge, was a shape.

A hand. Fingers outstretched.

*Joren.*

Cassian’s brother hadn't just died in the collapse ten years ago. He had been mined. His timeline had been excavated, compressed into Chronite oil, and piped up here to fuel the lights of the Spire.

Cassian’s rage was a physical force. The air pressure in the room dropped. Glass screens cracked.

"Divert the flow," Vex was shouting. "Stabilize the core!"

Cassian focused. He didn't have hands, but he had frequency.

He hummed.

The pipe shuddered.

"What is that noise?" Vex spun around, his mask snapping toward the conduit. "Seismic dampeners! Online!"

The dampeners did nothing.

Cassian pushed harder. He felt the Chronite inside the pipe responding to him. The oil began to separate. The sludge lost its viscosity.

The pipe groaned.

"Pressure spike!" a technician screamed. "The main line is destabilizing! It’s... it’s separating!"

Cassian saw the hand in the sludge clench. The fingers moved. They weren't just a fossil. They were a moment. A second of time preserved in amber, fighting to get out.

The metal of the conduit tore.

It wasn't an explosion. It was an unzipping. The steel ripped open along a vertical seam, peeling back like the skin of a banana.

The Chronite didn't spray out. It floated.

A stream of black oil rose into the air, defying gravity, and spiraled toward Cassian. It swirled around him, a tornado of lost time.

He saw faces in the oil. Miners. Dusters. A thousand forgotten lives erased by the Sanitation Directorate. They swirled around him, whispering.

*Thank you.*
*Remember.*
*The light.*

"Containment breach!" Vex drew a pistol. He fired blindly into the cloud of oil.

The bullets passed through Cassian. They passed through the oil. They hit the bank of servers on the far wall, sparking a shower of blue sparks.

"Stop it!" Vex screamed. He abandoned his composure. He ran to the emergency valve wheel, intent on manually shutting the flow.

Cassian sent a tendril of himself forward.

Cassian resonated the residue.

Vex froze. He grabbed his head. The porcelain mask cracked down the center.

"No," Vex gasped. "I am... I am the Auditor. I am..."

He fell to his knees. The memories trapped in the residue on his skin were flooding him.

"The audit," Vex whispered, clawing at his own face. "The audit... is incomplete."

He collapsed. The porcelain mask shattered completely, revealing the ruin beneath. But Cassian didn't look. He didn't care about the monster's face.

He turned his attention to the oil.

He gathered the stream. He pushed it. Not into the engines. Not into the furnaces.

He pushed it *up*.

The oil shot toward the hole in the ceiling. Toward the sun.

The Chronite hit the direct, unfiltered sunlight.

It ignited.

It didn't burn like fire. It burned like time. A pillar of violet fire roared up from the Spire, piercing the smoke, a beacon visible for miles.

The city shuddered. The gears ground. The ascent slowed.

The Queen.

Cassian felt her presence above him. She was the pilot. She was the will steering the city upward. But she was fading. The transition from stone to flesh to pure energy was consuming her. She had given her substance to the machine.

Cassian looked down.

He saw the Mid-Levels below. He saw the Rust Belt. He saw the hole where the Pit used to be.

He saw Kait. She was standing on the edge of the platform, holding his empty jacket, staring up at the violet pillar of fire.

She looked so small.

A memory surfaced in Cassian's mind. Not a memory of the city. A memory he didn't own.

*Green.*

The color of the world before the Scorch. The smell of rain that didn't taste like sulfur. The feeling of wind that wasn't generated by a fan.

The Queen was sharing it with him. Her final payment.

*Take it,* she whispered in his mind. *The future is heavy. Someone must carry it.*

He focused on Kait. He focused on the girl.

He couldn't speak. He couldn't leave a ghost.

But he could leave a vibration.

He pushed his frequency into the iron of the city. He pushed it into the deck plates, into the handrails, into the very air of the Mid-Levels. 

*Cassian was here.*

The vibration hit Kait.

She staggered. She gripped the railing. She looked around, wide-eyed.

"Cass?" she whispered.

She felt it. The warmth in the metal. The hum in her bones. She knew she wasn't alone.

Cassian let go.

He stopped fighting the drift. He allowed himself to rise.

He passed through the shattered Obsidian Eye.

He saw the sky.

It was infinite. It was a terrifying, empty blue dome, bigger than the deepest cavern he had ever dared to imagine. Clouds drifted, white and soft, like dust bunnies on a marble floor.

The city of Ouroboros hovered below him, a jagged spike of iron rising from the scarred earth. It looked like a splinter pulled from the world's thumb.

The Queen hovered beside him. She was no longer a woman of light. She was the light itself. A silhouette against the sun.

She looked at the horizon.

*We are out,* she said. *The drill is done.*

She turned her attention to the vast, empty wasteland of the surface. The ruins of the old world lay buried under dunes of black glass. Skeletons of skyscrapers jutted from the sand like broken teeth.

It was dead.

But the sun was shining.

Cassian felt a spark of himself ignite. Not a ghost. A recorder.

He saw everything.

He saw the dunes. He saw the distant, shimmering heat haze. He saw the birds circling, confused by the new metal mountain thrust into their domain.

He watched the city settle onto the crushed black glass. The landing gears—massive pylons of iron—crunched down. The city rested. It was no longer a cylinder digging down. It was a fortress sitting on the surface.

The engines died.

The violet fire faded.

Cassian hovered there, a consciousness suspended in the bright air.

He waited.

He watched the hatch open on the Mid-Levels.

A figure climbed out. Small. Fast. Wearing oversized goggles and a coat made of rags.

Kait stood on the roof of the world.

She looked up. She shielded her eyes.

She saw the sun. She saw the dunes.

Then she looked down. She looked at the metal of the roof beneath her boots.

She crouched. She touched the iron.

The metal hummed back at her. A low, steady thrum. A heartbeat.

Cassian poured his memory into the touch.

He showed her the way to the Spire. He showed her the location of the water filters. He showed her how to bypass the security locks Vex had engaged. He gave her the map of the city, drawn in vibration.

Kait smiled. A gap-toothed, fierce smile.

"I hear you," she said to the empty air. "Loud and clear."

She stood up. She put her goggles on her forehead.

"Right," she said. "Let's get to work."

Cassian Varr, the Second Son of the House of Bells, the Excavator, the Witness, let the wind take him.

He dissolved into the atmosphere, a scattering of gold dust in the blue.

The city sat on the edge of the world. The past was below. The sun was above.

And the work remained.
