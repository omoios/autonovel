The air in the maintenance shaft tasted of ozone. Cassian crouched behind the rusted housing of the ventilation unit, his chest heaving. Every breath dragged a jagged piece of air down his throat.

He looked at his shoulder.

The fabric of his coat was burnt away. The skin underneath was black. Not charred, but *voided*. A circle of flesh had been erased by the temporal round, leaving a crater in the muscle. Black ichor wept from the wound, thick and viscous like crude oil. It mixed with the red blood running down his arm, dripping onto the grating.

*Plink. Plink.*

The sound was lost in the groan of the city.

He needed to move. The searchlights from the Spire were sweeping the darkness above, cutting through the smog like scalpels. If they found him on the catwalk, he was a target in a shooting gallery. If they didn't find him, the Chronite poisoning would finish the job.

He reached the service hatch for Level 40. It was locked, sealed with a lead tamper-evident wax. He levered his weight. The metal groaned.

*Snap.*

The bolt gave. He pushed the hatch open and slipped inside.

The floor was a grate, and beneath it, the darkness of the drop-shafts waited. He ran. His boots struck the grate. *Clack. Clack. Clack.* The sound echoed, bouncing off the curved walls of the cylinder. He tried to soften his footfalls, landing on the balls of his feet, but the Nausea made him clumsy. His depth perception was skewed. The floor seemed to tilt up to meet him.

He passed a junction box. A warning stenciled on the side: *AUDIT ZONE. LISTENING ACTIVE.*

A drone drifted out of the gloom. A silver sphere, silent as a shark. It scanned the tunnel, its lens focusing with a wet click.

Cassian held his breath. *Match the rock. Be the stone.*

The drone drifted past, humming a high-pitched whine that set his teeth on edge. Once it was gone, he moved again.

The tunnel opened up onto the underside of the Grand Lift platform. The massive gears of the lift mechanism loomed above him, teeth the size of a man, grinding against the stone. Oil dripped from the axles, pooling on the walkway.

He found the maintenance ladder. It was slick with oil. He climbed. His muscles burned. His lungs felt like they were filling with fluid. Every rung was a battle.

He reached the service platform for Level 80. The Sanitation District. He hauled himself over the railing, collapsing onto the polished white stone.

*Not yet. Sleep later.*

He forced himself up. The corridor here was different. No rust. No grime. White walls. Sterile lighting. The smell of antiseptic and lavender. He was in the belly of the beast.

He pulled a small glass vial from his pocket. Year 900. The Great Sanitation. He uncorked it.

The moment the air hit the Chronite, the hallway shifted. The pressure of a thousand erased lives pressing against the present. The air grew heavy, viscous. He was swimming through history.

He crept forward, hugging the wall. The sensors on the ceiling swept the hallway with red lasers. They did not see a miner. They saw a blank spot in the timeline, a ghost from a sanitized year.

He reached the heavy bronze doors of the Vault. They were embossed with the seal of the Censors—a sun crushing a mountain.

Cassian smiled grimly. He wasn't an Auditor. But he knew how the stone worked.

He pulled the chisel from his belt. Not a weapon. A tool.

He placed the tip of the chisel against the bronze frame, right at the stress point. He tapped it gently with the hammer.

*Ping.*

A single, clear note. The sound didn't travel through the air; it traveled through the metal, finding the harmonic frequency of the lock. The bronze groaned. A hairline fracture appeared in the casing.

*Ping. Ping.*

The locking mechanism spun. The heavy bolts slid back with a sound like a grinding jaw.

The door swung open.

Cassian stumbled inside, sealing it behind him.

The Vault of Resonance was a circular room. In the center, on a pedestal of black glass, sat the Key. It hummed with a low, subsonic drone that vibrated in Cassian's marrow.

He approached it. The air around the pedestal shimmered with heat.

"It's beautiful," he whispered.

He reached out.

"Stop."

The voice was soft. Polite. Devoid of fear.

He turned slowly.

High Auditor Vex stood in the doorway. The door was closed behind him. His pale blue eyes stared out from the porcelain mask.

"Mr. Varr," Vex said. He smoothed the front of his tunic. "You are far from your strata."

"The ventilation is better up here."

"A jest." Vex stepped forward. His boots made no sound on the glass floor. "I'm just here to collect a debt," Cassian said. His heart hammered against his ribs.

Vex tilted his head. "The Key is not a debt. It is an instrument of order. You wish to introduce chaos. You wish to dig into the Basalt Threshold."

"The Basalt Threshold is a grave. You're digging up a Queen."

"I am excavating fuel." Vex lifted a hand. He made a small gesture, a flick of the wrist. "The city requires energy. The past is a battery. You, of all people, should understand the chemistry of consumption. You eat the years, do you not?"

"I don't eat people."

Cassian gripped the hammer. "I'm taking the Key."

Vex sighed. "You are desynchronized. Your temporal signature is... fragmented. I can see the decay on you. The white hair. The shaking hands. You are fading, Mr. Varr. You are becoming a ghost."

"I said," Cassian snarled, "I'm taking the Key."

The air between them thickened. Solidified. The hammer struck an invisible wall. It stopped dead.

Vex lowered his hand. Cassian shook his wrist. Pain shot up his arm. "You're an Auditor. You manipulate time."

"I manipulate the *physics* of time." Vex walked toward the pedestal. He reached out and touched the Key with a gloved finger.

Cassian looked at the Key. Then at Vex.

He pulled the tuning fork from his belt. The small one. His own.

"You talk a lot," Cassian said.

He struck his tuning fork against the bronze wall.

*CLANG.*

A high-pitched shriek erupted. Vex staggered back, clutching his head. "Stop that!"

Cassian struck the wall again. Harder.

*CLANG.*

"The resonance!" Vex shouted, his composure cracking. "You will shatter the containment!"

Cassian didn't stop. He struck it a third time. The bronze casing of the Vault split. The pedestal cracked. The invisible barrier holding the hammer snapped.

Cassian dropped the hammer. He threw himself at the pedestal.

He grabbed the Key.

It was cold. Freezing. It felt like holding a block of ice made of tears. He yanked it from the pedestal.

The room plunged into darkness. The hum stopped. The silence was deafening.

Vex screamed. It wasn't a scream of pain, but of outrage. "Kill him!"

The door burst open. Two Sanitizer soldiers rushed in, their gas masks distorting their faces into insectoid snarls. They raised their sonic rifles.

Cassian didn't fight. He ran.

He didn't run for the door. He ran for the window. The Vault had a viewport. Thick, reinforced glass.

He raised the heavy Key like a battering ram.

*Crack.*

The glass spiderwebbed.

*Crack.*

A hole appeared. Behind him, the rifles fired. Beams of focused sound ripped through the air. One clipped his shoulder. He felt the skin blister, the muscle tear. He grunted, ignoring the burn.

He threw himself through the hole.

Glass rained down around him like diamonds.

He fell.

The wind roared. The city spun. The Spire was a blur of white light above. The Pit was a black maw below.

He had a grapple. He fired it.

The cable lashed out, snagging on a gargoyle statue three levels down. He jerked to a halt. His shoulder screamed. The socket felt like it had been pulled out of the joint. He swung, dangling over the abyss.

Searchlights swept the smog below him. They cut through the fog like knives, looking for the thief.

He looked down.

Far below, in the shadows of the Rust Belt, he saw a signal light. Three flashes. A lantern.

Elara.

He had to go deeper.

He climbed down the rope, hand over hand, gritting his teeth against the pain. He reached the catwalk of the Rust Belt. He dropped onto the metal, rolling to absorb the impact.

He crawled into the shadows of an old ventilation unit. He sat up, pressing his back against the cold metal. He looked at the Key. Up close, it was terrifying. The metal seemed to shift, to flow like liquid smoke. It whispered. Not words, but *intent*.

*Open me,* it seemed to say. *Break the stone.*

He wrapped it in his coat, hiding the light.

"Got you," he whispered.

He pushed himself to his feet. He couldn't stop. If he stopped, the cold would catch him. The darkness would take him.

He moved toward the signal light. Toward Elara.

The city groaned around him, a massive beast disturbed from its sleep.

He had the Key.

Now he just had to survive long enough to use it.
