The service tunnel smelled of ozone and burnt rubber. The air here was recycled too many times, thin and hot against the back of the throat.

Cassian kept his back pressed against the metal door. Through the plating, he felt the vibration of the Sanitizers’ boots. Heavy. Rhythmic. They were sweeping the terminal. If they found the override open, they would flood the shaft with gas.

"Hey," Kait whispered. She kicked his heel. "You’re shaking."

Cassian looked at his hands. The leather gloves were trembling. It wasn’t fear. It was the frequency. The deep drills were turning down in the Pit, churning through the bedrock, and the hum was riding up the steel girders of the city. It resonated in the marrow of his bones.

"It’s the bore," he said. He pushed off the door and checked the dial on his wrist. The needle was pinned in the red. "We’re too deep. The gravity is pulling the timeline down."

"Good," Kait said. She adjusted her oversized goggles, making them sit straight on her nose. "Let it pull. We’re going to the Rust Belt, aren’t we? That’s lower."

"Lower is dangerous."

"I like dangerous." She grinned. Her teeth were white against the grime on her face. "Dangerous means nobody’s looking."

Cassian unclipped the heavy flashlight from his belt. He thumbed the switch. A beam of green light cut the dust. It didn't illuminate far. The darkness ahead of them was solid, like a wall of black velvet.

"Stay close," he said. "If you see a patch of rust that looks wet, don’t touch it. That’s not water. It’s concentrated Chronite leakage. It’ll age your skin off the bone in seconds."

Kait made a face. "Gross. Does it hurt?"

"Only for the first second," Cassian said. "After that, you don’t feel anything. You just turn to dust."

She shuffled closer to him, her boots scuffing the grate. "I don’t want to turn to dust. I want to see the Spire. I want to eat the white bread they have in the stories."

"There is no white bread," Cassian said. He started walking. The metal grid of the floor clanged under his boots. "That’s a lie they tell the Dusters so they’ll keep digging."

"My ma said it was real."

"Your ma also said the sun was a giant diamond."

"It is."

"It’s a star," Cassian corrected. "And it’s trying to kill us."

They moved down the shaft in silence. The only sound was the distant groan of the city settling—the grinding of massive gears, the screech of metal under stress, and the constant, low-frequency thrum of the Bore. It was a sound you felt in your teeth more than heard with your ears.

Cassian’s head began to throb. A sharp, piercing needle of pain behind his left eye. He flinched and rubbed the socket.

"What?" Kait stopped. "What is it?"

"Nothing," he lied. "Just a headache."

"You look sick. Your face is all pale."

"I’m always pale."

"Yeah, but now you look like a dead person. Like a fossil."

Cassian ignored her. He focused on the rhythm of his boots. Left. Right. Left. Right. He needed to anchor himself in the present. The headache was a warning. His internal clock was drifting. If he spent too much time in these deep strata, his body would forget what year it was. He might start seeing things that weren't there.

He saw a flicker of movement in the corner of his eye. A shadow against the wall.

He spun around, raising the flashlight.

Nothing. Just a rusted pipe dripping condensation.

"You okay?" Kait asked. She looked more concerned than annoyed.

"Fine," Cassian snapped. "Watch the rear."

They reached a junction in the tunnel. A rusted ladder led down into the gloom. Next to it, a hatch was bolted shut. Stenciled on the hatch in faded yellow paint were the words: *SECTOR 7 - MAINTENANCE ONLY.*

"We go down," Cassian said. He pointed the light at the ladder. "The Rust Belt is below the service line."

Kait peered over the edge. "It’s dark."

"That’s why we have the light."

"It’s really dark."

"Then don't fall."

She snorted. "I won’t fall. I’m a Duster. I climb better than I walk."

She swung onto the ladder and shimmied down, moving with the effortless agility of a rat. Cassian followed, his joints stiff. The rungs were cold and slick with oil. The air grew colder as they descended, and the smell changed. It lost the ozone tang and took on a heavier, metallic scent—the smell of old blood and wet iron.

The Rust Belt.

They dropped onto a catwalk suspended over an abyss. Cassian swept the light across the space.

It was a cathedral of scrap. Massive, derelict machines hung from the ceiling like sleeping bats, suspended by chains thick as tree trunks. They were the excavators of the previous era—shovel-headed behemoths, steam-pumps the size of houses, conveyor belts that hadn't turned in centuries. Below them, the darkness was absolute. If you fell off the catwalk, you didn’t hit the ground. You just fell until you starved.

"Whoa," Kait breathed. She gripped the railing with both hands. "Look at the teeth on that one."

She pointed at a rusted drill-bit hanging nearby. It was the size of a carriage, caked in dried slurry.

"Bit-Number 7," Cassian said. "They retired it during the Sanitation Era. It cracked the foundation of the Spire."

"It’s beautiful," she said.

"It’s junk."

"It’s *history*."

Cassian looked at her. She was vibrating with excitement, bouncing on the balls of her feet. She didn't understand. She saw the romance of the machines. He only saw the waste.

"Come on," he said. "We need the transceiver. It should be in the Foreman’s booth. Over there."

He pointed to a structure clinging to the side of a hanging gantry. A small, corrugated metal box with a window facing the abyss.

Getting there required crossing a suspension bridge made of woven steel cables. The gaps between the cables were wide enough to see through. Kait stepped onto it without hesitation. Cassian stepped carefully. The bridge swayed, creaking under his weight.

The headache behind his eye worsened. A sharp, white-hot spike.

*Focus.*

He stared at his boots. *Step. Shift. Step.*

He heard a sound. Not the creak of the bridge. A voice.

*Cassian.*

He stopped. He gripped the cable until his knuckles turned white.

"Cassian?"

It was soft. Distant. Like an echo in a pipe.

"Cassian, are you there?"

He squeezed his eyes shut. "Not real," he muttered.

"Who are you talking to?" Kait stood in the middle of the bridge, looking back at him.

"Nobody," Cassian said. He forced his eyes open. "Keep moving."

"It sounded like you were talking to Joren."

Cassian flinched. "I didn't say anything."

"You say his name when you sleep," Kait said. Her voice was flat. She wasn't teasing. "You say 'I'm sorry, Joren. I can't find the frequency.'"

Cassian didn't answer. He pushed past her, his boots heavy on the swaying steel. The headache pulsed in time with his heart.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

They reached the Foreman’s booth. The door was jammed. Cassian put his shoulder against it and shoved. The metal groaned, screeched, and gave way.

Inside, the air was stagnant. Dust motes danced in the beam of his flashlight. There was a desk, a chair, and a wall of controls. Everything was coated in a thick layer of gray grime.

Kait jumped onto the chair and spun around. "Is this where the boss man sat?"

"Yes."

"Did he have coffee?"

"Probably."

She hopped down and started opening drawers. "Empty. Empty. Empty. Just papers."

Cassian went to the control panel. He wiped the dust off a gauge. The needle was dead. He looked for the comms unit. It was a bulky rectangular box bolted to the wall, the handset curled like a dead snake. He ripped it off the mounting.

"Is that it?" Kait asked.

"It’s a hardline," Cassian said. "It runs through the city bedrock. It bypasses the Sanitizer grid. If we can splice into the main line, we can talk to Otho."

"Can we order pizza?"

Cassian paused. He looked at her. "Pizza?"

"Ma said they used to have it. In the old times. Flat bread with cheese."

"There is no pizza," Cassian said. "There is slurry. And if we’re lucky, hardtack."

"You’re boring."

"I’m realistic."

He knelt by the panel and pried off the back with his chisel. Sparks showered his gloves. Inside, a mess of copper wires greeted him. Red, blue, green. They were brittle with age. He needed the primary lead. The black one.

He reached in with his pliers.

"Don't touch the green one," Kait said suddenly.

Cassian froze. "Why?"

"The rats," she said. "They won't go near the green wire. They say it screams."

Cassian looked at her. "Rats don't talk, Kait."

"Mine do."

He sighed and turned back to the wires. He selected the black wire and the ground wire. He stripped the insulation with the edge of his chisel.

*Ping.*

The sound came again. Clearer this time.

*Cassian. Help me. The dark is cold.*

Cassian dropped the pliers. They clattered on the metal floor. He grabbed his head, his hands tangling in his hair. The pain behind his eye exploded—a starburst of white agony.

"Cassian!" Kait jumped off the chair.

"Get back," he gritted out. "Stay back."

"Are you sick? Do you need a doctor?"

"I need quiet!" he shouted.

He wasn't in the booth anymore. He was back in the tunnel. Year 2990. The Day of the White Dust.

The memory overlaid the room. The gray dust of the booth became the white powder of the breach. He saw Joren standing in front of him, holding the resonance chisel. Joren was young. He had a full head of hair. He was smiling.

*We found the vein, Cass,* Joren said. *We found the Primary Vein.*

Then the wall cracked. The sound like a thunderclap. The white dust poured out, not gray, but blinding white. It covered Joren. It ate him.

Cassian watched his brother’s face dissolve. The skin flaked away like ash. The bone turned to slurry. Joren opened his mouth to scream, but no sound came out. Just the hiss of escaping time.

*Dig,* Joren’s voice echoed in Cassian’s mind. *Dig me out. I’m still in the wall. I’m not dead. I’m just... compressed.*

Cassian gasped. He doubled over, retching dryly. He slammed his fist against the metal floor. The pain in his eye was blinding.

"Cassian!" Kait grabbed his shoulder. She shook him. "Stop it! You’re scaring me!"

He looked up. The hallucination faded. The booth was just a booth. The wires were just wires. Kait was staring at him, her eyes wide with fear.

He took a shuddering breath. He wiped sweat from his lip. The salt stung his cracked skin.

"It’s the Nausea," he rasped. "I... I drifted."

"You were screaming," she said. "You were screaming for Joren."

He pushed himself up. His hands were still shaking. He grabbed the pliers.

"I’m fine," he said. "Help me with this."

He held the black wire out to her. "Hold this."

She hesitated. She looked at the wire, then at his face. She slowly reached out and took the end of the wire between her thumb and forefinger. She held it away from the metal.

"Hold it steady," Cassian said.

He took the stripped end of the comms line and touched it to the black wire.

A spark. A hiss of static.

Then, a voice.

"—Otho. Come in, Otho. This is Cassian. Over."

Static crackled. Then, a burst of noise. A cough. The sound of heavy breathing.

"Cassian?" The voice was weak. Crackling. But it was her.

"Mother," Cassian said. "I hear you."

"Cassian!" The relief was palpable even through the distortion. "Where are you? We lost your signal an hour ago. The Sanitizers swept the terminal."

"We’re in the Rust Belt," Cassian said. "Sector 7. Below the line."

"The Rust Belt?" Otho’s voice sharpened. "That’s unstable, Cass. The supports are rotting. One tremor and you fall into the Mantle."

"We have no choice. We’re pinned."

"I know," Otho said. "I can hear the Bore from here. They’re accelerating. They breached the Threshold an hour ago."

Cassian felt a cold pit open in his stomach. "They breached the Queen’s layer?"

"They’re through the concrete. They’re in the crypt."

Cassian gripped the handset until the plastic creaked. "Is she...?"

"Still intact," Otho said. "For now. But Vex has the sonic drills on site. He’s going to liquefy the remains within the hour. He wants the Chronite underneath her."

"We can’t let him," Cassian said. "That much temporal displacement... it’ll trigger a collapse. The whole Spire comes down."

"I know," Otho said. "That’s why you have to wake her."

Cassian froze. "What?"

"You’re the closest, Cass. You have to wake the Queen."

"How? I’m not a Tuner. I don’t have the frequency."

"You have the chisel," Otho said. "And you have the blood. You’re a Varr. Your family worked the deep strata for generations. The resonance is in your DNA. Strike the fossil. Use the pain."

Cassian looked at his hands. The tremors were getting worse. He could feel the timeline slipping.

"I’m... drifting, Mother," he said. "The Nausea is bad."

"I know," she said softly. "I can hear it in your voice. You’re phasing."

"If I wake her... I might not come back."

"Then you die for a reason," Otho said. Her voice broke. "Better than fading away in the dark, bird. Better than becoming a shadow on the wall."

Cassian closed his eyes. The needle behind his eye throbbed.

"Okay," he whispered.

"Go," Otho said. "Before the Sanitizers triangulate the signal. I’ll keep the line open as long as I can. Good luck, Cassian."

"Wait," Kait said. She leaned into the mic. "Tell Otho I want extra rations. If we live."

Otho chuckled, a wet, rattling sound. "I’ll bake you a cake, Kait. A mud-cake with real dirt."

"Deal."

Cassian hung up the handset. The connection died.

He stood up. He felt heavy. His limbs were lead weights. The headache was a constant scream now.

"We have to go back," Cassian said.

"To the Spire?"

"To the Pit."

Kait looked at him. She saw the resolve in his face, and she saw the death waiting behind his eyes. She didn’t argue. She didn’t complain about the pizza.

"Okay," she said. "Let’s go wake the lady."

They left the booth. They crossed the swaying bridge back to the main catwalk.

The air pressure dropped suddenly. Cassian’s ears popped.

Then, the sound started.

It began as a rumble. Deep. Infrasonic. It vibrated the floor plates under their boots. The rust on the chains above them began to flake off like snow.

"What is that?" Kait shouted over the noise.

"The Bore," Cassian yelled back. "They’ve engaged the main drill."

The catwalk lurched. A massive piece of machinery above them—a shovel the size of a house—swung on its rusted chain. The groan of metal was deafening.

"Run!" Cassian shoved Kait forward.

They sprinted down the catwalk. Behind them, the chains snapped. The massive shovel plummeted. It smashed into the catwalk ten yards behind them, shearing through the steel like it was paper.

The walkway tilted. Cassian slid. He grabbed a railing, his boots skidding on the metal grating. He dangled over the abyss.

"Cassian!" Kait grabbed his wrist. She was anchored to the remaining ladder.

He looked down. Below him, the darkness yawned. Miles of empty air, ending in the crushing dark of the Mantle.

"Hold on!" she grunted.

He looked up at her. Her face was strained, her small frame trembling with the effort.

"Don't let go," he said.

"I got you," she said. "I got you."

He swung his leg up, hooking it over the rung. He hauled himself up, his muscles screaming. They collapsed onto the solid platform of the junction, panting.

Above them, the city screamed. The sound of grinding metal, of collapsing strata. The Sanitizers were tearing the world apart to get to the fuel.

Cassian stood up. He checked his chronometer. The glass was cracked. The needle was spinning wildly.

"We have to move," he said. "Now."

He didn't look at the abyss. He didn't look at the wreckage of the machine. He looked at the ladder leading down into the deepest dark.

"Are you scared?" Kait asked. She was holding his hand again.

Cassian looked at her. He thought of the white dust. He thought of Joren’s face dissolving. He thought of the Queen, buried in the stone, waiting for a hammer.

"Yes," he said.

He squeezed her hand.

"So am I."

They climbed down into the dark.
