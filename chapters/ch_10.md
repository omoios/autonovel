The glass in the street crunched under Cassian’s boots. It was not the sharp, clean shard of a fresh break, but the ground-down powder of a thousand trampled failures.

"Move," he said, shoving Elara into the shadow of a rusted support pillar.

She stumbled. Her bare feet caught on the uneven iron grating. She looked less like a monarch and more like a drowned rat pulled from the sump. Her hair hung in heavy ropes, stiff with compressed time.

The market was a chaotic press of bodies and steam. Vendors hawked slurry-cakes from iron carts. Dusters in rags picked through piles of refuse that fell from the upper chutes. No one looked at them. Down here, eyes were a liability.

Cassian gripped Elara’s arm. Her skin was fever-hot.

"Where are we going?" she asked. She stared at a neon sign flickering above a bodega. *FRESH WATER - 0.02 YEARS.*

"The Rust Belt," Cassian said. "Derelict sector near the Pit. Structural integrity is garbage, but the Sweepers don't patrol there. Too dangerous for their tin suits."

He pulled her forward. They moved through the crowd like two pebbles in a river.

A man pushing a cart of recycled bolts bumped into Cassian’s shoulder.

"Watch it," the man spat.

Cassian didn't have the breath for apologies. He kept moving, checking the sky. The white uniforms would be swarming the Mid-Levels by now.

They reached the edge of the market plaza. The massive circular void of the city’s central shaft yawned before them.

"The bridge," Elara said. She pointed a trembling finger at the narrow span of iron that crossed the chasm to the Rust Belt. "It wasn't there."

"Added in Year 800," Cassian said. "After the Collapse."

"Run," he said. "Don't stop. Don't look down."

They hit the iron mesh.

The wind howled up the shaft. It caught Elara’s tattered robes, flapping them like the sails of a shipwreck. She staggered, fighting the gale.

Cassian grabbed her hand. Her palm was rough, callused from gripping a sword that hadn't existed for three millennia.

*Crack.*

A sound like a thunderclap, but compressed. High-pitched.

A sonic blast.

"Down!"

He dragged Elara over the side of the bridge. They slammed onto the maintenance catwalk hanging underneath the main span. The iron grating bit into Cassian’s knees. He rolled, pulling Elara tight against his chest.

The sound hit them a second later—a teeth-rattling hum.

"Vex," Cassian hissed.

He looked up through the gaps in the grating. White uniforms. Three of them. They moved with the synchronized precision of clockwork toys.

"Thermal sweep negative," one said. The voice was tinny, filtered through a gas mask.

"The resonance trace stops here," another said. "The thief is close."

Cassian pressed his face into the grime. The smell of rust and ozone filled his nose. Elara was pressed against him, her breathing ragged. She was vibrating. Literally vibrating. Her frequency was bleeding out.

He needed to dampen it.

Cassian pressed his hand against the back of her neck. A counter-frequency.

Elara gasped. Her shivering slowed.

"Quiet," Cassian breathed into her ear. "Be a stone."

Above them, the boots clanked.

"Check the lower levels," the leader said. "He can't have jumped the chasm. He's in the vents."

The boots moved away.

Cassian waited. His heart hammered against his ribs. He looked at Elara. Her eyes were wide, pupils dilated to the edge of the iris. She was terrified.

"They wear the white," she whispered. "Like the surgeons."

"They are butchers," Cassian said. "Come on. We have to climb."

He pointed to the service ladder bolted to the bridge suspension cable. It led down into the darkness of the Rust Belt.

Elara looked at the ladder. She looked at the endless drop.

"I cannot," she said.

"You must," Cassian said. "Unless you want to go back in the box."

He swung over the edge and grabbed the ladder. The iron was slick with oil. He slid down a few rungs. Elara followed. Her movements were stiff, jerky. She hated this.

***

They descended.

The Rust Belt was a warren of tunnels and hanging walkways. Cassian dropped onto a corroded landing and helped Elara down.

"Water," she said.

Cassian checked his canteen. Half-full. He handed it to her.

"Where are we?" Her voice was stronger.

"The underbelly," Cassian said. "Year 2800 architecture. Cheap steel. Bad welding."

He paced the small landing. The Nausea was a spike in his brain now. A white-hot needle. He rubbed his temple. He checked his watch. The hands were frozen at 11:58.

"What is that device?" Elara pointed at the watch.

"Tells me when I'm going to die," Cassian said.

"It is broken," she said.

"Probably."

He needed to get to Mother Otho. But the way to Otho’s was blocked. Vex would have surveillance on all transit hubs.

"We have to go through the Slurry Chutes," Cassian said.

Elara wrinkled her nose. "The waste tunnels?"

"It's the only blind spot," Cassian said. "The sensors don't work down there. The interference from the organic sludge scrambles the signal."

He led her deeper into the belt. The air grew hotter. The smell shifted from rust to sulfur.

They reached a massive pipe that angled down into the dark. It was made of transparent aluminum, now yellowed with age. Inside, a thick, gray sludge oozed slowly downward.

"The river of dead things," Elara said.

"Just recycled protein," Cassian said. "And old bricks."

He pried open the maintenance hatch.

Elara looked at the opening. She looked at Cassian. There was no accusation in her eyes, only a profound sadness. A recognition of how far her city had fallen. She had built a sanctuary. They had built a toilet.

She stepped inside.

Cassian followed, sealing the hatch behind them.

Inside the pipe, the sound was different. A wet, sucking sound. They walked on the narrow maintenance walkway that ran along the inner curve. 

"You spoke of the Interregnum," Elara said. Her voice echoed in the confined space.

"I know what I dug up," Cassian said. "I know you stopped the mining. I know they buried you for it."

"I stopped the mining because we dug too deep," she said. "We woke something."

Cassian stopped. "What?"

Elara touched the wall of the pipe. Her fingers left trails in the grime.

"The world is not dead, Cassian," she said. "It is sleeping. And we... we are the ants biting the paw of the bear."

"What did you find?" he asked.

"Silence," she said. "A place where time does not flow. A stillness. If the drills break that seal... the bear wakes."

Cassian thought of the Bore. The massive drill Vex was constructing. The one aimed at the Basalt Threshold.

"We have to stop Vex," Cassian said.

"We have to kill the machine," Elara corrected.

They moved in silence. The sludge grew thicker, darker. The heat rose. Cassian’s strength was fading. The Nausea was feeding on him. He felt heavy. His limbs were lead.

He stumbled.

Elara caught him.

"You are fading," she said.

"I'm fine." He tried to stand, but his legs wouldn't hold him. The gravity in the deep levels was stronger. It pulled at his bones.

"You lie." She pulled his arm over her shoulder. "Lean on me."

They limped forward together. The thief and the Queen.

They reached the end of the pipe. It opened into a vast, cavernous space—the Sump. This was where the city's waste was collected before being processed into fuel. The smell was overpowering. The scent of a billion bodies compressed into a single room.

"There," Cassian pointed. "The hatch."

He fell to his knees. He couldn't go on. The pain in his head was blinding. He was seeing double.

"Go," he gasped. "Open it."

Elara let him down gently. She crawled to the hatch. She gripped the wheel. She threw her weight against it.

It resisted. The metal was fused with age.

She groaned.

*Snap.*

The hatch flew open.

A rush of cool, clean air hit them. It smelled of lavender and ozone.

"Mother," Cassian whispered.

He passed out.

***

He woke up to the taste of broth.

Hot and salty. He choked.

"Easy," a voice said.

Cassian opened his eyes.

The room was dim. Lit by a single lamp made of a glowing crystal. The walls were lined with shelves. Jars of buttons. Spools of copper wire. Pieces of colored glass.

Mother Otho sat by the bed. She looked ancient. Her skin was parchment, her hair a wispy gray cloud. She stirred a bowl with a wooden spoon.

"You were gone a long time, Cassian," she said. "I was starting to think you found a nice rock and decided to stay under it."

Cassian tried to sit up. His head spun, but the needle-pain was gone.

"Vex," he croaked.

"Vex isn't here," Otho said. "Nobody is here. Just the rats and me."

"Elara?"

Otho’s eyes crinkled. She gestured with her chin.

Cassian turned his head.

Elara sat in the corner of the room. She was wrapped in a blanket of woven electric fibers. She was staring at her hands, flexing her fingers.

"She is quiet," Otho said. "Hasn't said a word since we hauled you out of the chute."

Cassian sat up. The room swayed. He grabbed the edge of the cot.

"She's the Queen," Cassian said.

"I figured," Otho said. "She has that look. Like she owns the air."

Otho set the broth down. She sighed.

"You brought a fossil into my house, Cassian," she said. "A fossil that Vex is tearing the city apart to find."

"He wants to burn her," Cassian said. "To get to the Primary Vein."

Otho nodded slowly. She picked up a piece of blue glass from the table and held it up to the light.

Cassian swung his legs off the bed. He stood. His knees were weak, but they held.

"She says there's something down there," Cassian said. "Something sleeping."

Otho lowered the glass. She looked at him. Her eyes were sharp, ancient, and tired.

"The world is full of sleeping things," she said. "We don't poke them."

"We have to warn the Excavators," Cassian said. "Vex is going to break through. He's going to collapse the strata."

Otho placed the glass back on the shelf. She stood up. She went to a chest in the corner and threw it open.

She pulled out a heavy coat. Leather and canvas. And a belt. A belt with pouches and loops for tools.

"You aren't going back out there," she said.

"I have to."

Otho tossed the coat to him. He caught it.

"You're a fool," she said. "But you're my fool."

She reached into the chest again. She pulled out a small, heavy object. A sonic detonator.

"Take this," she said.

Cassian hefted the device. "What for?"

"If you find the drill," Otho said. "If you find the heart of the machine... you break it."

Cassian looked at Elara. Elara looked up. Her eyes met his. They were black, endless, and terrified.

"We go to the Spire," Cassian said.

"The Spire?" Otho laughed. "You'll be shot before you clear the perimeter."

"No," Cassian said. He pulled the coat on. "We go to the Spire because that's where Vex is. And that's where the kill switch is."

He looked at his watch. 11:59.

"We have to hurry," he said.

Elara stood up. She let the electric blanket fall. She wore the rags she had arrived in, but she wore them with a sudden, terrifying dignity.

"Lead," she said to Cassian. "And I will follow."

Cassian nodded. He looked at Otho.

"Keep the lights off," he said.

"I always do," Otho said.

Cassian opened the door.

The hallway outside was dark. The distant sound of the city—a low, grinding thrum—waited for them.

He stepped out.

Elara followed.

Behind them, the door clicked shut.

The hallway stretched out into the gloom, a throat of rough-hewn stone swallowing the light from Otho’s room. The air tasted of ozone and old wet soot.

Cassian moved forward. The leather coat creaked. He kept his left hand against the wall. The stone was cold, damp, slick with the condensation of the city’s breath.

Elara followed. Bare feet on stone. She moved like a shadow cast by a light that no longer existed.

They reached the intersection of the corridors. A junction box on the wall sparked, throwing erratic blue shadows against the rock.

"Wait," Elara said.

Cassian stopped. He didn't turn. He watched the dark ahead.

"The resonance," she said. "It is louder here."

"It's the mains," Cassian said. "The ventilation pumps."

"It is not pumps," she said. "It is a scream. Wrapped in iron."

Cassian closed his eyes for a second. The needle behind his left eye pricked sharp. He rubbed the spot with his thumb. "It's just the city settling. The Spire is heavy. The weight compresses the gas in the lines."

He started moving again. Faster.

Elara kept pace. She didn't look at the flickering lights. She looked at the darkness between the lights.

The tunnel widened. Wooden supports, rotting and swollen with dampness, shored up the ceiling. Through the gaps in the wood, Cassian could see the faint, green glow of Lamp-Vines growing inside. Wild. Unchecked.

"Faces," Elara said.

Cassian stopped. He turned to her.

"What?"

"In the fog," she said. "When we crossed the bridge. I saw them."

Cassian’s stomach turned over. He remembered the Smog. The gray swirl of memory-dust.

"They're just echoes," he said. "Residue. When the Sanitizers burn the Chronite, the smoke is full of... fragments. Images."

"They were screaming," she said.

"It's what they do," Cassian said. "It's the sound of history being erased."

He started to turn back to the path.

"No," she said. Her voice was hard. "They were screaming *at* me."

Cassian looked at her. Her face was pale in the blue light of the sparking junction box.

"I know that scream," she said. "I heard it when they buried me. When they poured the concrete."

She stepped closer to the wall. She pressed her hand against the stone.

"The city is haunted, Cassian," she whispered. "Not by ghosts. By the future."

Cassian felt a cold drop of sweat slide down his spine. He checked his watch.

11:59.

"The future," Cassian said. "You mean the past."

"I mean what comes after," she said. "The bear is waking. And the ghosts... they are the warnings."

She looked at him. Her eyes were two black holes in the dim light.

"You see them too," she said. "Don't lie to a Queen, Cassian."

Cassian looked away. He looked at the dark mouth of the tunnel ahead.

"I see shapes," he admitted. His voice was rough. "In the Smog. Sometimes when I dig too deep."

"What do they look like?"

"Faces," he said. "Hundreds of them. Tiny. Compressed."

Elara reached out. She took his hand. Her grip was like iron.

"Then we are already too late," she said. "The bear is awake."

Cassian pulled his hand away. He adjusted the collar of the coat.

"We aren't dead yet," he said.

He started walking.

Elara stood there for a moment. Then she followed.

The tunnel sloped upward. The air grew thinner. The smell of sulfur faded, replaced by the sharp, metallic tang of ozone.

They were climbing out of the deep levels. Heading for the Spire.

The Smog swirled around Cassian’s legs. He looked down.

He blinked. It was gone.

He kept walking.
