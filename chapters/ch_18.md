The steel bit into his palms. Cassian lowered himself, boots scraping against the rusted girder. The metal groaned, a high-pitched whine that vibrated in his molars. The air down here didn't move. It hung, heavy and wet, tasting of sulfur and the copper tang of old blood.

He looked up. Otho’s silhouette blocked the circle of light from the floor above. Kait was a smaller shadow beside her. They were descending. Good.

Cassian dropped the last few feet to the platform below. His knees buckled. He caught himself on a pile of shale, the sharp rocks digging into his gloves. He waited for the spike of nausea. It didn't come. The deeper he went, the quieter the noise in his head became. The constant static of the city—the drills, the presses, the screams of the lifted gears—faded into the earth. The silence was a relief. It felt like pressing a bruised thumb against cold iron.

Otho landed beside him with a grunt. Kait hopped down, light as a rat, clutching the cracked porcelain mask to her chest.

"Quiet," Cassian said.

He pointed to the tunnel ahead. It wasn't a tunnel anymore. It was a throat. The circular bore of the Sanitizer drill had chewed through the basalt, opening a jagged wound in the city's foundation. The walls here were smooth, glass-like, melted by the friction of the bit. But the surface wasn't black.

It was blue.

A vein of Chronite, pure and uncut, pulsed in the rock face. It hummed. A low, resonant frequency that made Cassian’s bones itch. He checked his chronometer. The needle spun wildly, the friction heating the metal against his wrist.

"The resonance is rising," he whispered. "Stay close to the wall. Don't touch the blue."

"Is that... time?" Kait asked. She reached out a hand.

"Stop," Cassian snapped. "It's Year 50. The Interregnum. It’s hot enough to burn your shadow off."

He pulled a lead-lined cloth from his pack and wrapped it around his left arm. The Chronite was reacting to him. He could feel the drag, the gravity of the past trying to pull him in. If he touched the vein with bare skin, he’d flash back to the day the Queen fell. He’d feel the concrete pour over his head. He’d die of suffocation, three thousand years late.

They moved into the throat. The heat intensified. It wasn't the dry heat of the furnaces; it was a wet, suffocating blanket. Cassian’s shirt clung to his back. Sweat stung the corners of his eyes.

Ahead, the drill-bit had snapped. The massive, toothed head of the machine lay twisted on the floor, shorn off by something harder than the rock. The floor beyond was smooth. Polished.

And in the center of the room, a throne of solidified Chronite.

She sat there. Elara.

She wasn't a fossil. She wasn't bones. She was a woman, frozen in the act of screaming. Her mouth was open, her hands raised as if to push away the ceiling. Her skin was black stone, her hair a cascade of gold veins that merged with the rock behind her. She was terrifying. She was magnificent.

Cassian fell to his knees. The pressure in his head vanished, replaced by a singular, blinding clarity. He had found her. The ghost in the geology.

"The Queen," Otho breathed. She didn't kneel. She gripped her side, where the medical tape was stained dark. "Birds, she looks... heavy."

Kait stepped forward. She wasn't awed. She was counting. "She's wearing rings. Three of them. Gold."

"Get back," Cassian said. He pulled the tuning fork from his belt. It was a heavy thing, forged from steel scavenged from the Spire elevators. He struck it against the rock.

*Clang.*

The sound died instantly. The rock swallowed it.

"Strike it," Cassian said to Otho. "Harder. Match the pitch."

Otho hit her wrench against the throne. *Clang.* Better. The blue vein in the wall shuddered. Dust puffed from the Queen's open mouth.

"We have to wake her," Cassian said. "Before Vex gets the secondary drill online. If he drills through the floor, he hits the Mantle. He boils the city."

"Why do we care?" Kait asked. She poked at the pile of debris. "Let the rich people boil."

Cassian stood up. The needle behind his eye flared, sharp and hot. "Because we live in the basement, Kait. When the basement floods, the rats drown first."

He struck the fork again. *Clang.*

The sound built. It overlapped with itself, echoing off the glass walls. The air began to warp. The smell of ozone hit Cassian, so strong he could taste it on the back of his tongue.

The rock cracked.

It started at the Queen’s throat. A hairline fracture of white light. Then her shoulder slumped. The stone skin flaked away, falling like ash.

"Is she moving?" Kite asked. She backed away, hiding behind Otho.

"Stand your ground," Cassian said. He didn't know if he was talking to the girl or himself.

The Queen’s mouth closed. Her head snapped forward. The sound was like a pistol shot. Her eyes opened. They were black pits, devoid of whites, filled with the swirling dust of the mine.

She inhaled. A gasp that sucked the air out of the room. The blue vein in the wall dimmed, its energy flowing into her lungs.

She stood.

The stone throne crumbled to dust. She wore a dress of rusted iron, the chains hanging heavy from her wrists. She looked at them.

"Who disturbs the rest of the grave?" Her voice wasn't a voice. It was the vibration of the drill-bit, slowed down to a human growl. It bypassed Cassian's ears and rattled his sternum.

Cassian stepped forward. He ignored the pain in his legs, the heat, the fear. He saluted. The Excavator’s salute. Fist over heart.

"Your Majesty. I am Cassian Varr, Chronicler of the Strata. We are here to extract you."

Elara turned her black eyes on him. She didn't blink. She didn't seem to have eyelids, just pits of darkness.

"Extract," she repeated. The word tasted like bile. "Like a tooth? Like a tumor?"

"Like a memory," Cassian said. "The city is dying. The sky is gone. We are digging too deep."

Elara walked toward him. She didn't walk on the floor; she hovered an inch above it, repelled by the magnetic field of the Chronite. She stopped inches from his face. She smelled of dry ice and ancient perfume.

"The sky," she whispered. "Tell me of the sun."

"The sun is a myth," Cassian said. "A story we tell the children so they don't fear the dark."

Elara’s face twitched. The stone cracked on her cheek. "Liar."

"It's true," Kait piped up from behind Otho. "It's just a big light in the ceiling. The Spire controls it. I saw the wires once."

Elara looked at the child. "A small thing. Loud. Like a rat."

"I'm a Duster," Kait said, bristling. "And I have a knife."

"Silence," Otho hissed. She pushed Kait back. "Your Grace. We need you. The Sanitizers are breaking the Threshold. They mean to burn the Year 50 for fuel."

"Fuel," Elara said. She turned her back on them. She ran a hand along the smooth blue wall. "My Silence. They burned it?"

"They are burning it now," Cassian said. "We have minutes before the thermal spike kills us all."

Elara stopped. She looked at the floor. "The Mantle stirs. I can hear it. The heart of the world. It beats slow. *Thump. Thump.*"

She looked back at Cassian. "You brought me back to stop the noise?"

"To stop the fire," Cassian corrected.

Elara smiled. It was a terrible expression, a widening of a fissure in a stone face. "I did not stop the digging to save the rock, little miner. I stopped the digging because the rock is the shell. And inside the shell..."

She pointed down.

"...is the beast."

A rumble shook the chamber. Not a drill. A tectonic shift. The floor tilted. Cassian grabbed Otho to keep her from falling. Kait scrambled up the debris pile, clutching her mask.

"What was that?" Otho asked. She coughed, a wet, hacking sound that brought up black spittle.

"The Mantle," Cassian said. He checked his chronometer. It had stopped. The glass was cracked. "The heat transfer is accelerating."

"We need to move," Cassian said. "Your Grace. Can you walk?"

"I can float," she said. "I can scream." She looked at the ceiling, where the hole they had descended through was now glowing with orange light. "And I can burn."

"Up," Cassian ordered. "Now. Before the Lag sets in."

The Lag. The time dilation. Down here, minutes were hours. If they stayed too long, they would age to dust before they reached the ladder.

Kait scrambled up the rocks first. She was fast, agile. "Come on! The heat is coming!"

Cassian helped Otho. Her skin was hot, fever-hot. The Nausea was taking her. The deep time was rewriting her cells. "Lean on me," he said.

"I'm not a child," she snapped, but she leaned.

Elara didn't move. She stood in the center of the room, arms outstretched.

"Come!" Cassian shouted.

"The machine," Elara said. She pointed to the broken drill-bit. "It is a key."

"Forget the key!" Cassian grabbed her arm. Her skin was cold. Colder than the rock. "Move!"

She looked at his hand on her arm. "You touch me. You do not burn."

"I'm already burning," Cassian said. He pulled.

She followed. They moved up the slope of the debris. The heat behind them was becoming a physical force. It pushed against their backs. The air shimmered. Rocks began to melt.

They reached the girder. Kait was already halfway up the climb to the upper level. Otho was struggling, her breath rattling in her chest.

"Go," Cassian said to Elara. He boosted her toward the steel. "Climb."

She grabbed the metal. Her fingers punctured the steel like it was soft clay. She hauled herself up, moving with an inhuman, jerky speed.

Cassian turned back. The floor of the chamber was glowing red. A pool of molten Chronite was spreading. And in the center of the pool, something was rising.

Not rock.

A shape. Massive. Ribbed like a shell.

"The beast," Elara whispered from above.

Cassian scrambled up the girder. He didn't look down. He didn't want to see what the city had been built on top of.

He hauled himself over the edge of the upper level, gasping. The air here was cool, comparatively. He rolled onto his back.

"We have to collapse the hole," he said. "We have to bury it again."

Otho shook her head. She was sitting against the wall, her eyes closed. "No dynamite, Cass. We used it all on the door."

"We don't need dynamite," Elara said. She stood over them. The porcelain mask Kait had found was in her hand. She looked at it, then crushed it to powder.

"I am the keystone," she said. "I was the lock. I was the wall."

She walked to the edge of the hole. She raised her hands. The air pressure dropped. Cassian’s ears popped.

"What is she doing?" Kait asked. She was hiding behind a pile of crates.

"Sealing the breach," Cassian said. He crawled forward. "Your Grace! Wait!"

"If I seal it," Elara said, her voice echoing with the power of a thousand years, "I remain here. I cannot leave. The Threshold requires a Guardian."

Cassian stopped. The needle in his eye throbbed. If she stayed, she died. If she left, the city burned. The Sanitizers would drill through the floor in an hour. They would hit the Mantle. The Spire would fall. The Slums would boil.

"You can't stay," Cassian said. "You're the only proof the truth ever existed."

"I am the truth," she said. "And the truth is heavy."

She looked at Cassian. "Do you have the strength to carry me, Chronicler?"

Cassian stood up. His legs were trembling. His lungs were full of dust. But he stood. "I can carry a memory," he said. "I can't carry a mountain."

Elara lowered her hands. The heat from the hole lessened. She stepped back from the edge.

"Then we run," she said.

Cassian turned to Otho. "Can you run?"

Otho opened one eye. The white was gone, replaced by a swirling black iris. She was seeing the past. "I can fly," she whispered.

"Good enough," Cassian said. He grabbed Otho’s arm and hauled her up. "Kait! Lead the way. The maintenance tunnels. The ones the rats use."

"The rats are scared," Kait said. She was looking at the vent. "Something is blocking the way."

Cassian looked. The vent grill was bulging outward. Something was pushing from the other side.

"Vex," Cassian said. "He's here."

The grill popped. Clumps of white gas rolled into the room. Sanitation Foam. It ate everything. Organic, inorganic. It dissolved the crates. It hissed as it hit the floor.

Through the mist, white shapes moved. Soldiers. Sanitizers. They wore porcelain masks, blank and smooth. They carried sonic cannons.

"Audit," a voice crackled through a synthesizer. "Unauthorized excavation. Sentence: Termination."

Cassian pushed Kait behind him. He drew his pickaxe. It was a pathetic weapon against sonic cannons.

"Get the Queen to the lift," Cassian said to Otho. "Go."

Otho looked at him. She saw the determination. She saw the end. "Cassian..."

"Go!" he shouted.

He charged.

He didn't aim for the soldiers. He aimed for the steam pipe on the ceiling. The auxiliary line. It was old, rusted. One good strike.

A soldier fired. The sonic wave hit Cassian in the shoulder. He felt the bone turn to jelly. He screamed, but he didn't stop. He swung the pick.

*Clang.*

The pipe burst. Scalding steam erupted, blinding white. It filled the room. The soldiers shouted. The sonic waves went wild, bouncing off the walls.

"Run!" Cassian yelled.

He couldn't use his left arm. It hung limp. He grabbed Otho with his right hand. "Move!"

They stumbled into the corridor. Kait was already there, prying open the grate of the secondary shaft. "In! In!"

They tumbled into the darkness of the maintenance shaft. The metal ladder was slick with condensation. They slid down, fast and uncontrolled.

They hit the bottom. A tunnel. Dark. Lit only by the faint blue glow of Kait's stolen lamp.

Cassian leaned against the wall. His shoulder was on fire. He looked at his arm. The flesh was translucent. He could see the bone, glowing faintly. The Nausea. He was fading.

"You're hurt," Kait said. She touched his arm. Her fingers went *through* his skin.

"It's nothing," Cassian lied. He looked at Elara. The Queen was fading too. She was becoming translucent, losing her solidity.

"The Lag," Elara said. "We are too deep. We are too old."

"The lift," Cassian said. "We get to the Mid-Levels. The gravity is lighter. We stabilize."

"And Vex?" Otho asked. She was coughing again. Her skin looked like parchment paper.

"Vex can have the hole," Cassian said. "Let him deal with the beast."

They ran. The tunnel was narrow, lined with pipes. The sound of the Sanitizer boots echoed behind them. Heavy. Rhythmic.

Cassian felt his timeline slipping. He saw Joren. Not a ghost. A memory. Joren standing by the drill, laughing, wiping grease from his nose.

*Cassian.*

He shook his head. "Not yet."

He focused on the rhythm of his boots. *Left. Right. Left.* He was a miner. He was solid. He was here.

They reached the junction. The Grand Lift.

It was huge. A platform the size of a house, suspended by cables thicker than tree trunks. But it was locked down. The power was cut.

"The override," Cassian said. He pointed to the control box. "Kait. You're small. You can crawl inside the panel."

Kait scrambled up the box. She ripped the panel open with a crowbar. "Wires. Lots of wires."

"Cut the blue one," Cassian said. "No, the red one. The one that looks like dried blood."

"Which one?" Kait yelled. The Sanitizers were at the tunnel mouth now. They were filling the corridor with white foam. It was eating the walls.

"Any wire!" Cassian shouted. "Just touch them together!"

Kait grabbed a handful of copper and yanked.

*Sparks.*

The lift groaned. The gears above them screamed. The platform jerked, then began to rise.

The Sanitizers fired. A sonic blast hit the counter-weight. The cable snapped.

The lift tilted.

They slid. Otho and Kait tumbled toward the edge. Cassian grabbed Kait’s belt. He dug his boots into the metal grate. He caught Otho’s hand with his bad arm. The pain blinded him.

"Hold on!" he screamed.

The lift listed at a forty-degree angle. The remaining cable sang under the tension. It was hauling them up, but unevenly. They were scraping against the tunnel wall.

Elara stood at the top of the tilt. She wasn't holding on. She was looking up. Through the cracks in the lift shaft, the light of the Spire was visible.

"The sun," she whispered. "The false sun."

"Help me!" Cassian gritted his teeth. His arm was pulling out of its socket. Otho was heavy. She was made of stone and time.

Elara looked down at them. She extended a hand.

"Rise," she said.

The Chronite in the walls responded. The blue veins flared. The lift shuddered. It righted itself. Not mechanically, but magnetically. The metal bent to her will.

Cassian pulled Otho and Kait up to the center of the platform. They collapsed, gasping.

The lift rose faster now. Accelerating. The G-force pinned them to the floor.

The Sanitizers were left behind, tiny white specks in the deep dark.

They passed the Mid-Levels. The Market. The air grew cooler. The smell of frying grease and sweat replaced the sulfur.

Cassian closed his eyes. The needle behind his eye was gone. The silence was gone, replaced by the roar of the city.

They were alive. They were in the Present.

The lift slowed. It docked at the Spire platform. The highest level. The domain of the Censors.

The doors opened.

Light. Blinding, artificial, white light. It hurt Cassian’s eyes after the dark of the Pit.

Standing there, waiting for them, was a row of Sanitizers. And in the center, a man in a white uniform.

Vex.

He wore no mask. His face was gone. Just a smooth, porcelain plate with eye holes and a speaker grill. He held a white cane.

"Welcome home," Vex said. His voice was smooth, polite. "You have something of mine."

Elara stepped off the lift first. She walked toward Vex. The air around her rippled with heat.

"I am not yours," she said. "I am the city."

Vex tilted his head. *Clack.*

"The city is a machine," Vex said. "And you are a rusted cog."

He raised his cane. The Sanitizers leveled their cannons.

Cassian stepped off the lift. He stood between Elara and the guns. He was covered in dust. His arm was translucent. He was bleeding black ichor.

"She's the Queen," Cassian said. "And I'm the Chronicler."

He looked at Vex. He looked at the porcelain face.

"And we're here to audit you."
