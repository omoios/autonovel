The air in the Mid-Levels tasted of wet iron and recycled breath.

Cassian walked with his head down, the collar of his coat turned up against the falling dust. The gray snow coated his shoulders, dusted his hair. It settled in the creases of his neck, cold and gritty.

Behind him, the gap in the wall smoked. The Sanitizers were sealed inside the vent, trapped with the burning Chronite, but the shouting had stopped. Now there was only the crackle of fire and the high, thin whine of cooling metal.

He kept his hands in his pockets. His left hand wrapped around the silver ink on his forearm. It was hot. It felt less like ink and more like a second circulation system, a network of hot wires laid under his skin.

Kait walked ahead, skipping over a puddle of oily wastewater. She turned, her goggles reflecting the red warning lights of the sector.

"Did you see his face?" she asked. Her voice was high, breathless. "The meat of it. I thought he was wearing a mask, but that was just his head peeled back."

"Keep moving," Cassian said.

"Elara shot him," Kait said. She turned to Elara, walking beside her. "You put a hole in his knee. He shattered. Like a dropped mug."

Elara didn't look at the girl. She walked with a stiff, military gait, her eyes scanning the catwalks above them. "He was already dead. The porcelain was just holding him together."

"Sick," Kait said. She grinned, showing a missing canine tooth. "Brilliant, but sick."

Cassian focused on the vibration of the deck plates beneath his boots. The city was waking up. The graveyard shift was ending. The forges were roaring to life, burning the excavated past to keep the present warm.

He felt the needle behind his left eye. A sharp, piercing prick.

The King. The Echo.

Vex’s final scream echoed in Cassian’s memory, but now the sound was warping. It deepened, stretching out, becoming something else. A voice that wasn't a voice.

*Return.*

Cassian stumbled. He caught himself on a rusted railing.

"You okay?" Elara asked. She didn't stop, but she slowed.

"Fine," Cassian said. He rubbed his eye. The pressure was building, pushing against the back of the socket. "The resonance is shifting. The city is settling."

"You're bleeding," Kait said. She pointed.

Black ichor dripped from Cassian’s nose. It hit the metal grate with a heavy *plink*.

He wiped it away with the back of his glove. The smear was thick, viscous, shimmering with motes of silver. "It’s the Nausea. The exposure down there was... high."

"You look like a Rock-Rat," Kait said. "You know, the ones that eat the bad veins and their eyes turn blue."

"Shut up, Kait," Cassian said.

He checked the pocket watch. The hands were spinning lazily, caught in a magnetic eddy. They weren't ticking; they were drifting. He snapped the case shut.

"We need to get off the main deck," Elara said. She pointed to a ventilation shaft jutting out from the wall like a rusted rib. "The Censors will be swarming the sector in ten minutes. The sonic blast from the collapse will have tripped every alarm in the district."

"Mother Otho is at the orphanage," Cassian said. "She needs to know we failed to stop the drill. We only delayed it."

"We killed Vex," Kait said.

"We killed a body," Elara corrected. "The Directorate is a machine. You cut off a hand, the machine grows a new one."

They moved toward the vent shaft. The crowd of workers was thickening—men and women in gray canvas coveralls, carrying lunch pails and tool kits. They moved with the shuffling gait of the exhausted, heads down, eyes on the ground.

Cassian pulled his scarf up over his nose. The smell of the crowd was suffocating. Sweat, grease, and the sour tang of unwashed wool.

"Move it," a foreman shouted, cracking a cane against a metal stanchion. "The lifts don't wait for stragglers."

Cassian pressed himself against the wall as a line of haulers trundled past, carrying massive drums of waste slurry. The drums rumbled and sloshed, the gray liquid inside churning.

He watched the liquid. For a second, the gray slurry looked like faces. Dissolved features. Mouths opening in silent screams.

He blinked. The faces were gone. Just sludge.

"You seeing things?" Elara asked. She stood close to him, her shoulder brushing his.

"No," Cassian lied. "Just tired."

"Your arm," she said. She looked at the silver ink. It was spreading. The sharp geometric lines were blurring, becoming organic, vein-like. "It’s moving up your forearm."

"It’s stabilizing," Cassian said.

"It looks like it's eating you," Kait said. She poked at his arm.

Cassian pulled away. "Don't touch it."

They reached the vent shaft. Elara grabbed the lever. It was rusted tight. She grunted, straining, the muscles in her neck corded.

"Help me," she said.

Cassian stepped in. He wrapped his hands around the cold iron. He pulled. The metal groaned.

The needle behind his eye spiked.

*Let me in.*

The voice was clearer now. Not a sound, but a thought that wasn't his. It felt like a cold hand sliding down his spine.

Cassian gritted his teeth. He pictured the Queen. He pictured the Basalt Layer. He focused on the memory of the cold, hard rock. *I am the rock. I am the strata.*

He jerked the lever.

With a screech of torn metal, the vent door popped open. A blast of stale, filtered air hit them.

"In," Elara said.

Kait scrambled up the cables like a spider. She vanished into the darkness of the duct. Elara followed, her armor clanking against the sheet metal.

Cassian paused. He looked back at the main deck.

The crowd was parting. A squad of Censors in white enameled armor marched through, their sonic cannons lowered. They wore masks that looked like smooth, featureless eggs. They scanned the crowd with red sweep-lights.

One of the Censors stopped. It turned its head toward Cassian.

Cassian froze. The silver ink on his arm seared him. It felt like it was trying to pull him toward the Censor. Like a magnet.

The Censor tilted its head. It raised a hand.

Cassian didn't wait. He grabbed the cable and hauled himself up, swinging his legs into the shaft. He kicked the vent door shut.

Darkness swallowed him.

He climbed. The cable was greasy, digging into his palms. Above him, he could hear Kait’s breathing—quick, light. Below him, the metal rang with the impact of his boots.

"Where are we going?" Kait whispered, her voice echoing down the tube.

"The Rust Belt," Elara said. "We cut through the derelict machinery. It’s a blind spot for their sensors."

"My arm," Cassian said. He spoke to the darkness. "The silver. It reacted to the Censor."

"He knows you're there," Elara said. "Vex. Or whatever is left of him."

"He’s in my head," Cassian said. "He’s... whispering."

"Don't listen," Elara said. "If you listen, you invite him in. That’s how the Nausea takes you. You accept the hallucination as reality, and your brain rewrites itself to accommodate it."

"He knows about the Queen," Cassian said. "He wants her."

Cassian climbed higher. The vertical tunnel stretched up for fifty feet. His arms burned. The sweat on his back turned cold.

He thought about the day Joren died. The Day of the White Dust.

They had been digging a vein of Year 50 Chronite. The resonance had been perfect. Then the drill had hit a pocket of gas. The White Dust—compressed time from the Pre-Scorch era. It had poured out of the rock like a waterfall of milk.

Joren had screamed. He had held up his hands, watching them dissolve into sand. His skin, his muscles, his bones—turning to white powder and blowing away.

Cassian had tried to grab him. He had tried to hold his brother together, but there was nothing to hold. Joren had become a cloud of history, a memory of a man, drifting into the ventilation system.

Cassian had inhaled him.

He still felt the tickle of that dust in his lungs sometimes.

*We are the same,* the voice in his head whispered. *We are the displaced.*

"Shut up," Cassian growled.

"What?" Kait asked from above.

"Nothing," Cassian said. "Keep climbing."

They reached the junction box. A rusted hatch led out onto the Rust Belt.

Elara kicked the hatch open.

They emerged onto a platform suspended from the ceiling of the Mid-Level. Below them, the city floor was a miles-distant patchwork of lights and industry. Above them, the Spire hung like a sword of Damocles, its lights gleaming through the smog.

But here, in the Rust Belt, it was quiet. Massive gears, the size of houses, sat dormant. Great chains hung slack, swaying gently in the draft.

It was the graveyard of industry. The machines that had dug the city were left here to rust when they became obsolete.

"Careful," Elara said. "The metal is fatigued."

They walked along a narrow catwalk suspended between two giant flywheels. The wind whistled through the holes in the metal.

Kait ran ahead, jumping over the gaps in the grating. "Oi! Look at this!"

She pointed to a massive piston, half-buried in the wall. It was covered in Iron-Moss, the black crusty fungus that clicked when it grew.

"It's eating the machine," Kait said. She poked it. The moss clicked back.

"Don't touch it," Cassian said. "It’s corrosive."

"You're boring," Kait said.

Elara stopped. She held up a fist. "Stop."

The wind died. The silence of the Rust Belt was heavy, absolute.

Cassian heard it.

A ticking sound.

Not a clock. A biological tick. Like a giant heart.

It came from the piston Kait was standing next to.

"Kait," Cassian said. "Step away."

Kait looked down. The black moss was pulsing. It was swelling, bulging outward. It cracked the metal casing of the piston.

"It’s waking up," Elara said. She drew her pistol. "It’s a Strata-Serpent. A big one."

The piston exploded.

Black slime sprayed across the catwalk. A pale, segmented body uncoiled from the machine like a snake made of wet marble. It was twenty feet long, its head shaped like a pickaxe, its eyes glowing with a faint blue Chronite light.

It hissed. The sound was steam escaping a valve.

"Run," Elara said.

Kait screamed. She scrambled back, slipping on the black slime.

The serpent struck. Its head smashed into the catwalk where Kait had been standing a second before. The metal groaned and tore. Rivets popped like gunshots.

Cassian moved. He didn't think. He lunged forward, grabbing Kait by the back of her coat, and hauled her toward the safety of the gear housing.

The serpent reared up. It sensed them. It tasted the air with a forked tongue that flickered like a candle flame.

"It smells the Chronite," Cassian said. "It smells the silver ink."

He pushed Kait behind him. He stood at the edge of the platform, his feet spread wide. He raised his left arm, the one with the silver ink.

The ink was boiling. It was glowing through his skin, casting a bright, harsh light.

"Hey," Cassian shouted. "Over here."

The serpent turned its head. It focused on him. The blue eyes flared.

Cassian felt the pull. The silver ink in his arm was screaming. It wanted to go to the serpent. It wanted to join the predator.

*Release me,* the King whispered. *Let me hunt.*

"No," Cassian said. He gritted his teeth. He visualized a wall. A dam. He forced the silver ink down, compressing it, holding it in place with his will.

The pain was blinding. It felt like he was holding a lightning bolt in his veins.

The serpent lunged.

It moved faster than anything that big had a right to. It was a blur of pale muscle.

Elara fired.

*Bang. Bang.*

The bullets sparked against the serpent's scales, ricocheting into the dark.

The snake's jaws snapped shut inches from Cassian’s face. He smelled the creature's breath—ozone and rot.

He didn't flinch. He locked eyes with the beast. He felt the resonance of the creature. It was old. It was a creature of the deep strata, displaced and starving.

Cassian hummed.

He found the frequency. It was low, subsonic. A vibration in the floor plates.

He matched it.

The serpent froze. Its head jerked. It looked at him, confused.

Cassian stepped closer. He hummed louder, forcing the sound through his clenched teeth. The vibration rattled his skull.

The serpent shuddered. It pulled back, its eyes losing focus. The resonance was disrupting its nervous system.

With a final, angry hiss, the serpent turned. It coiled its massive body and slithered back into the hole in the piston, dragging its bulk into the darkness of the machine.

Silence returned.

Cassian dropped to his knees. He vomited black bile onto the grate.

He coughed, spitting the taste of copper and old time from his mouth.

"Cassian?" Elara ran to him. She holstered her gun. "What did you do?"

"I... I confused it," Cassian wheezed. He wiped his mouth. "I sang to it."

"You sang?" Kait peeked out from behind the gear housing. Her eyes were wide. "I didn't hear anything."

"It was below the range of hearing," Cassian said. He leaned against the railing, his whole body trembling. "Geological frequency."

He looked at his arm. The silver ink was dim now, dull gray. But it had moved. It was now wrapped around his wrist like a bracelet. A permanent shackle.

"We need to keep moving," Cassian said. He pushed himself up. His legs felt weak. "The noise will attract other things."

Elara looked at him, then at the hole where the serpent had vanished. "You're changing," she said.

"I'm adapting," Cassian said.

They walked the rest of the way in silence. The Rust Belt gave way to the shanties of the Slums. The air grew warmer, thicker. The smell of sewage and frying grease replaced the metallic tang of the upper levels.

They reached the Ironwood District. The buildings here were made of scavenged hull plates and bolted together with rusted bolts. It was a maze of alleys and dead-ends.

Mother Otho’s orphanage was a converted warehouse in the center of the district. It was the only building with a fresh coat of paint—bright yellow, peeling in the humidity.

They slipped in through the side entrance.

The interior was warm. The smell of baking bread—real yeast, not the fungal cakes of the deep levels—filled the air.

Children sat on mats on the floor, reading from tattered books. They looked up as the trio entered, their faces dirty but bright.

Mother Otho sat in a rocking chair by the furnace. She was knitting something grey and lumpy. She looked up, her eyes clouded with cataracts but sharp as flint.

"You're late," she said. Her voice crackled like dry leaves. "And you smell of trouble."

She sniffed the air. "And sulfur. And blood."

"We had a complication," Elara said. She unbuckled her armor, letting it drop to the floor with a clang. "Vex is dead."

Otho stopped knitting. She looked at Cassian. "Dead?"

"He burned," Cassian said. He walked toward the old woman. "The Chronite consumed him."

Otho set her knitting down. She leaned forward. "And the Queen? The fossil?"

"We didn't get her," Cassian said. "The Sanitizers breached the chamber before we could extract her."

Otho closed her eyes. She let out a long, slow breath. "Then the city is doomed."

"Not necessarily," Elara said. She pulled a map from her pack and spread it on the table. "Vex is dead, but the drill is still operational. The Directorate will send a new Auditor. But the drill is damaged. It will take them a week to repair the bore head."

"A week," Otho said. "Then we have a week to find the Key."

"The Key?" Kait asked. She sat on the floor, pulling off her boots. "What key?"

"The Queen’s Seal," Cassian said. He sat heavily on a crate. He rubbed his left eye. The needle was still there, a dull ache. "When she was buried, they didn't just seal her in concrete. They locked her timeline with a Chrono-Lock. A specific frequency that keeps her in stasis."

"To dig her out," Otho said, "we need the counter-frequency. The Key."

"Where is it?" Elara asked.

Cassian looked at his arm. The silver ink pulsed once, a slow, heavy beat.

"It’s in the Spire," Cassian said. "Vex had it. He was wearing it around his neck, beneath the porcelain."

Elara stared at him. "You saw it?"

"I felt it," Cassian said. "When the Chronite burned him... I saw the echo of it. A silver fob watch. It matched the vibration in my arm."

Otho stood up. Her joints popped. She walked to Cassian. She took his face in her hands. Her skin was paper-thin, dry and warm.

"You have a piece of him inside you," she said. It wasn't a question.

"Yes," Cassian said.

"Then you have a map," Otho said. She tapped his chest. "The King knows where the Key is. He knows the Spire."

"He wants me to go there," Cassian said. "He’s pushing me."

"Then let him push," Otho said. She turned to Elara. "We need to infiltrate the Spire. While the city mourns Vex, while the power transfer is happening. That is our only window."

"It's suicide," Elara said. "The Spire is a fortress. The gravity alone will crush us."

"We have no choice," Cassian said. He stood up. The Nausea washed over him in a wave—dizziness, nausea, the feeling of his organs shifting. He rode it out, waiting for it to pass.

"If we don't get the Key," Cassian said, "the drill finishes its repairs. They break the Threshold. The Queen dies. And the city... the city collapses."

He looked at the children. They were watching him. A little boy with a shaved head and a bandage on his arm.

"What happens then?" Kait asked. "If the city collapses?"

"We fall," Cassian said. "Into the Pit. Or we fade."

He looked at his hands. The skin was translucent. He could see the blue veins beneath. For a second, they looked like the roots of a plant.

He needed to sleep. But he knew if he slept, he would dream of the silver mask. He would dream of the throne room.

"I'll go tonight," Cassian said.

"Not alone," Elara said.

"No," Cassian said. "Alone. If I take you, the King will sense it. He'll know I'm coming for the Key. He'll destroy it before I can get close."

"You can't control him," Otho said. "He's an Auditor. He's a ghost of high compression."

"I can," Cassian said. He lied. He didn't know if he could. But he knew he had to try.

He walked to the window. He looked out at the city.

The smog was swirling. Below, the lights of the Mid-Levels flickered.

Above, the Obsidian Eye stared down like a Cyclops.

He felt the pull. The gravity of the Spire. It was calling him.

He touched the glass.

"It wants me to come up," he whispered.

"Then go," Otho said. "And come back. Don't make me bury another son."

Cassian turned from the window.

"I'll be back before dawn," he said.

He grabbed his pack. He checked his tools. Chisel. Hammer. Tuning fork.

He didn't look at Kait. He didn't look at Elara. If he looked at them, he would hesitate.

He opened the door.

The wind howled outside, carrying the dust of the dying city.

He stepped out into the dark.
