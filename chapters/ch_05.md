The fissure in the floor was wide enough to swallow a leg, but the steam hissing out of it was thin, smelling of sulfur and wet iron. Cassian stood over the crack, his boots slipping on the damp metal grating. The pickaxe in his hand felt light, too light, like holding a bird bone instead of steel.

He looked at the steam. The Nausea spiked—a needle driving into the left temple, sharp and hot. He squeezed his eyes shut.

"Don't stare," Kait said. She was already moving, dragging a heavy canvas bag from the pile of gear near the tunnel wall. "It’s rude to stare at the abyss. Gets you pushed."

Cassian opened his eyes. The vision of the silhouette—Joren—was gone. Only the black steam remained. "Get the shovel," he said.

Kait hefted the bag. "We’re going in? After the ghost-thing?"

"After the King," Elara said. She stood at the edge of the fissure, looking down. Her white armor was filthy, stained with soot and the gray slurry of the sewers. She didn't look like a queen. She looked like a miner who had been underground too long. ""

Cassian stepped to the edge. 

"The Vein," Cassian said. "It’s not just Chronite. It’s a compression chamber."

"I’m lighter," Kait said, grabbing his arm. "I go first. I can fit in the cracks."

"No," Cassian said. He pulled her back gently. "The pressure down there... it’ll crush a child’s ribs like paper. I go first. Elara, you’re rear guard."

Elara nodded. She drew a pistol from her thigh holster. It was an antique, heavy caliber. "If the Sanitizers find the entrance, I will delay them."

"If they find the entrance, we’re already dead," Cassian said.

He stepped off the edge.

 He dropped through the first layer of fog—cool, wet, smelling of ozone. Then the second layer—hot, dry, smelling of burnt hair.

He landed on a narrow ledge carved into the side of the shaft. 

Kait dropped down beside him, landing like a cat. Elara followed, heavier, her boots ringing on the basalt.

"Quiet," Cassian whispered.

The sound was deafening. The *thump-thump* wasn't just a sound; it was a physical wave. The wall vibrated against Cassian’s back. He could feel the teeth in his skull buzzing.

"Where is he?" Kait whispered. "The King?"

Cassian pressed his hand to the black wall. He closed his eyes. 

*Year 50. The Silence.*

He felt the resonance. A low, mournful frequency. It felt like Joren.

"There," Cassian pointed to a vein of silver running through the black basalt. ""

He raised the pickaxe.

*Strike.*

The basalt rang like a bell. A high, clear note that hung in the air.

*Strike.*

A chip of black rock flew off. Behind it, the silver vein pulsed.

"He’s in there," Cassian said. He struck again. "I can hear him."

"Who?" Kait asked, peering into the dust.

"My brother," Cassian said. "He didn't disappear. He was just... filed away."

Elara moved to the tunnel mouth, her pistol raised. "Haste, Cassian. The tremors are getting stronger."

Cassian swung the pickaxe. *Clink. Clink. Clink.* The rhythm was hypnotic. He forgot the pain in his head. He forgot the heat. He just dug. The black chips piled up around his boots.

Then the wall gave way.

 

The mercury swirled. It formed a shape. A man. Tall, broad-shouldered.

Cassian dropped the pickaxe. "Joren?"

The figure turned. It had no face. Just a smooth, oval surface of silver.

"That is not the King," Elara said.

The silver figure lunged.

It moved faster than a human—a blur of liquid metal. It slammed into Cassian, knocking him backward. He hit the basalt wall hard. The air left his lungs.

The thing pinned him. Its silver hands were cold, burning cold. It pressed against his throat.

"Get off!" Kait screamed. She swung her shovel, hitting the figure in the back.

 It squeezed. Cassian gagged. His vision blurred.

"Chronite echo," Elara said. She stepped forward. She didn't shoot. She reached out and grabbed the figure’s arm.

"Release him," she commanded. Her voice cracked with authority.

The silver figure froze. It turned its blank face toward Elara.

"You," it said. Its voice sounded like grinding stones. "The Burier."

Elara’s face paled. "Joren?"

The silver face rippled. Features formed. A nose. A mouth. Eyes. It was Joren’s face, but twisted, melted.

"You buried us," the thing said. "You left us in the dark."

"I saved you," Elara said. "I stopped the drill. I put you in the deep stasis to save you from the Scorch."

"We were awake," the thing screamed. The sound shattered the lenses of Kait’s goggles. "We were awake in the dark. For three thousand years."

The silver figure exploded. Not into pieces, but into mist. It rushed into Cassian’s nose, his mouth, his ears.

He couldn't breathe. He couldn't scream. He was drowning in cold mercury.

Then, silence.

Cassian gasped. He sat up. The tunnel was empty. No silver figure. Just the steam.

"He’s gone," Kait said, shaking him. "Cass, he went into you."

Cassian grabbed his left arm. The tattooed bars—the Yield marks—were moving. The ink was writhing under his skin, reshaping itself. The black bars turned silver.

"He didn't go into me," Cassian wheezed. "He’s resonating with me."

"The city remembers," Elara said. She looked grim. "And it is angry."

She checked the load on her pistol. "We need to move. The Sanitizers will have heard that resonance."

"Move where?" Kait asked. "Up? Down?"

"Through," Cassian said. He stood up.  "The wall opened for a reason. It was a door."

He walked to the spot where the figure had vanished. The basalt was smooth again, except for a small, hand-sized indentation. A keyhole.

"Elara," Cassian said. "The key."

Elara reached into her armor. She pulled out a sliver of crystal. It glowed with a faint, blue light—the only light left in the city that wasn't burning Chronite.

She pressed the crystal into the hole.

The tunnel groaned. The floor dropped out from under them.

They fell.

***

They slid down a chute of polished obsidian. It was smooth, frictionless. The darkness was absolute.

Cassian tried to control the fall, to slow himself with his gloves, but the rock was too slick. He tumbled, head over heels, colliding with Kait and Elara in the dark.

They hit bottom.

It wasn't a hard impact. They landed in something soft. Wet.

Cassian pushed himself up. His hand sank into the ground.

It wasn't ground. It was hair. Thick, matted black hair covering the floor.

"Where are we?" Kait’s voice trembled.

Cassian clicked his lamp on. The beam cut through the gloom.

They were in a cavern, massive as the Spire above. But the walls weren't rock. They were flesh. Massive, pulsating walls of pale, veined stone. The ceiling was a ribcage of calcified ivory beams.

"The Basalt Threshold," Elara whispered. "My prison."

She stood up. She touched the fleshy wall. It twitched under her fingers. "I didn't bury the city here. I buried *myself*."

"Why?" Cassian asked. He scanned the room with the lamp. In the center of the fleshy cavern was a lake. Black oil. Chronite.

"To stop the feeding," Elara said. "I realized... the city wasn't digging for energy. It was digging for *me*."

She walked toward the lake. " My blood became the oil. My bones became the pillars."

Kait gagged. "That’s gross. That’s... really gross."

"It is efficient," Elara said. "Flesh makes the best fuel. It burns with the memory of pain."

Cassian followed her. The Nausea was gone. In its place was a strange euphoria. The air here was sweet. Like rotting fruit.

"What do we do?" Cassian asked. "Dig you out?"

"No," Elara said. She stopped at the edge of the black lake. "We wake it up."

She raised her pistol. She pointed it at the reflection of the moon in the oil.

"If this body is the fuel source," she said. "Then we must cut the supply line."

She pulled the trigger.

The bullet hit the oil. It didn't splash. It detonated.

The black liquid exploded upward. A geyser of Chronite shot toward the fleshy ceiling. But it wasn't just liquid. Shapes formed in the spray.

Soldiers.

Hundreds of them. Men and women in white porcelain armor, their faces hidden behind gas masks.

"The Queen's Guard," Cassian said. "You buried them with you."

"I buried them *in* me," Elara corrected. "They are my antibodies."

The geyser collapsed. The soldiers landed in the flesh around them. They didn't move like men. They moved like insects—jerky, synchronized. They drew swords that looked like bones.

"The Sanitizers are here," Elara said. "I can feel their drills boring into my skin."

She pointed to the far wall of the cavern. The flesh was ripping. Beams of white light cut through the darkness. The smell of ozone hit Cassian.

"We are breached," Cassian said.

He grabbed Kait. "Stay behind me."

The wall burst open.

Three figures stepped through the tear. They wore white, pristine suits. They were tall, thin.

The leader wore a porcelain mask.

**High Auditor Vex.**

"Finally," Vex said. His voice was amplified by a synthesizer in his throat, metallic and smooth. "We have been chasing this signal for so long."

He stepped into the cavern, his white boots sinking slightly into the fleshy floor. He looked around, his watery blue eyes scanning the room.

"And here she is," Vex said. "The Mother Lode."

He looked at Elara.

"Your Majesty," Vex said, bowing slightly. "You are looking... unwell."

Elara sneered. "You look like a doll that was left in the fire, Vex. Does that mask hide your face, or your shame?"

"It hides the rot," Vex said casually. "Just as you hide yours."

He snapped his fingers.

The two Sanitizers behind him raised their weapons—sonic cannons.

"Hold them," Vex ordered. "I begin the extraction."

Vex walked to the edge of the Chronite lake. He reached into his coat and pulled out a device. It looked like a syringe, but the needle was the size of a spear.

"I am going to drain this chamber," Vex said conversationally. "The energy density of this room... it could power the Spire for a millennium. We could finally stop digging. We could finally... ascend."

"If you drain this," Elara said, "the city collapses. I am the foundation."

"You are the past," Vex said. "And the past is fuel."

He plunged the spear into the black lake.

The reaction was instant.

The lake screamed.

It wasn't a metaphor. The oil let out a sound—a high-pitched, shrieking wail that vibrated in Cassian’s bones.

Cassian fell to his knees, clutching his ears. Blood dripped from his nose.

Kait was screaming, but he couldn't hear her.

Vex laughed. He spoke into a comms unit on his wrist. "Flow rate is optimal. Divert to the main grid. Initiate the burn."

The black oil began to churn. It flowed up the spear, into Vex’s machine. The machine glowed red-hot.

The walls of the cavern began to shrink. The flesh was compressing.

"He’s killing the city," Elara shouted. "Cassian! Stop him!"

Cassian looked up. His vision was swimming. The Nausea was back, worse than ever. The silver bars on his arm were burning.

He couldn't stand. He couldn't fight.

*Kait.*

He looked for her. She was crouched behind a rock, covering her ears.

He had to do something. He had to dig.

But there was no rock here. Only flesh.

*Wait.*

Cassian looked at Vex. The man was standing in the open, absorbed in his machine.

*The frequency.*

Cassian remembered the silver figure. The resonance.

He focused on the silver bars on his arm. He tried to push the Nausea outward. To project it.

He focused on the sound of Vex’s breathing. The mechanical hiss of the respirator.

*Match it.*

Cassian took a breath. He hummed. A low, guttural sound.

The sound hit the Chronite lake.

The oil stopped flowing up the spear. It reversed.

The black oil shot back down the spear, cracking the glass casing of Vex’s machine.

"What?" Vex stepped back. "No!"

The oil exploded. It coated Vex. It covered his white suit, his pristine mask.

He clawed at his face. "Get it off! It’s hot! It’s—"

Vex screamed. The Chronite burned through the porcelain mask. Cassian saw the face beneath.

It was gone. Just raw, red muscle and teeth.

The Sanitizers turned. They raised their sonic cannons at Cassian.

"Run!" Elara shouted.

She fired her pistol. The bullet struck one Sanitizer in the knee. He didn't bleed; he shattered. Porcelain shards flew.

"Go!" Elara grabbed Kait. "To the bore hole!"

Cassian scrambled to his feet. He ran. He didn't look back at Vex. He didn't look at the burning oil.

He ran toward the tear in the wall where the Sanitizers had come in.

He squeezed through the jagged gap in the flesh.

He emerged onto a metal catwalk. The air was cool here. Industrial.

He was back in the **Mid-Levels**.

Below him, the great drills of the Sanitizers were silent. The workers were looking up, pointing at the smoke rising from the vents.

"We did it," Kait whispered. She was shaking. "We killed him."

"We didn't kill him," Cassian said.  "We just... relocated him."

Elara leaned against the railing. She looked tired. Her armor was cracked.

"The city is safe for now," she said. "But the wound is open."

She looked at Cassian. "You have a piece of him in you. The King. The Echo."

Cassian rubbed his arm. He felt cold. "I know."

"It will make you sick," she said. "Eventually, it will overwrite you."

"I know," Cassian said.

He looked up. Above the smog, the lights of the Spire flickered. For a second, he thought he saw a face in the Obsidian Eye. A face with a silver mask.

"Come on," Cassian said. He adjusted his pack. "We need to get back to the orphans before the Censors lock down the sector."

He started walking. The Nausea was a dull throb now, a background rhythm. Like a heartbeat.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

He walked into the crowd of workers, disappearing into the gray snow of the falling dust.
