 Dust billowed, choking and white, rolling over the polished surface of the Gallery like a dry fog.

Cassian didn't wait for Vex to finish his order. He yanked Elara’s arm. Her skin was cold, hard as river stone, but she moved. She stumbled, her legs stiff, knees locking with the grinding noise of tectonic plates.

"The door," Cassian said. He pointed to the service arch behind the dais.

Vex was shouting, his voice tinny and distorted through the synthesizer in his throat. "Seal it! Don't let them reach the lift!"

 They looked like ghosts fighting in a cloud of milk.

"Go," Elara said. Her voice cracked. A sound like a glacier calving. "The resonance is wrong here. The pitch is... it hurts."

Cassian shoved her toward the archway. He glanced at the table. Vex was gone. The High Auditor had backed away into the shadows of the pillars, his white uniform disappearing into the gloom. The guards were fumbling with the breach-seals on the main doors, their movements jerky with panic.

Cassian ran.

The service corridor was dark. The emergency lights spun, casting long, bloody shadows against the walls. The air here tasted of ozone and sulfur. The smell of the deep.

He kept a hand on Elara’s shoulder. "Faster. The tremors aren't stopping. They’re harmonics."

"I know," she said. She gasped, a sharp intake of breath that sounded like wind in a tunnel. "I can hear the stone screaming. You dig too deep, little miner, and the stone screams back."

They hit the intersection. The left tunnel led to the Central Lift. The right led to the Slums. The Slums were below. The Slums would be crushed first when the Spire settled.

"Left," Cassian said.

Elara planted her feet. The stone floor cracked beneath her boots. "No. The Up. We must go to the Roof. To the Light."

"The Lift is the only way up," Cassian said.  "And it’s probably on lockdown. Vex isn't stupid."

"I am the Queen," Elara said. She straightened her spine. The dust on her shoulders shimmered, turning into a gown of white powder. "The machine obeys me. It remembers."

She marched toward the heavy blast doors of the Central Lift.

Cassian followed.  The past was leaking out of her, warping the present around her.

They reached the doors. The console was dark.

"Open," Elara said.

Nothing happened. The steel remained seamless and cold.

Cassian stepped forward. He pulled the Sonic Wrench from his belt. "It’s not voice-activated, Your Highness. It’s mechanical. It needs a frequency."

He struck the wrench against the console housing. A hollow clang. He listened to the ring. The metal was vibrating. A low C-sharp. The note of the settling foundation.

"Stand back," he said.

He adjusted the wrench. He set the dial to match the resonance of the door’s locking bolt. 

He pressed the emitter pad against the steel.

A hum.

The steel shivered. Dust fell from the frame.

The door groaned. A sound of protest.

"Break," Cassian whispered.

The bolt snapped. The doors slid apart a foot, then jammed.

"Enough," Elara said.

She stepped forward. She didn't squeeze through the gap. She pushed. She placed her hands on the cold steel and simply pushed.

The metal screamed. It didn't bend; it lost its structural integrity. It aged. Centuries of corrosion passed in a second. The steel flaked away into red rust, crumbling under her touch like dry bread.

She stepped through the hole she had made.

Cassian stared at the rust. It coated his gloves. He sniffed it. It smelled of blood and time.

He scrambled after her.

The Lift Car was huge. A glass and brass cage suspended on a cable as thick as a man's torso. It hung in the center of the shaft, a vertical tunnel stretching down into the blackness and up toward the distant circle of the Spire's roof.

Elara stood in the center of the car. She looked up.

"The Obsidian Eye," she said. "It is still there. My father built it to watch the stars. Now it watches us burn."

 "Vex has snipers. If he looks up the shaft, we’re silhouettes."

"He fears the dark," Elara said. She knelt, but her eyes stayed on the ceiling. "He always did. Even as a boy, he slept with a candle."

"Vex is old," Cassian said. "He’s been alive a long time."

"Not him," Elara said. "His blood. The Line of Vex. They are stewards of nothing."

Cassian crawled to the control panel. The main lever was locked. A Sanitizer Override clamp.

"He's sealed the lift," Cassian said. "He's locked out the ascent. He’s trapping everyone down here to crush with the Pit."

"Can you override it?"

"I need a key. A physical key. Or a blowtorch."

Elara reached into the folds of her dust-gown. She pulled out a hand. It wasn't hers. It was a skeletal hand, made of black iron, clutching a jagged key made of bone.

"A relic," she said. "From the First War."

She jammed the bone key into the console.

The machine didn't spark. It hummed. A deep, guttural vibration that Cassian felt in his marrow.

"Rise," Elara commanded.

The cable above them went taut. The glass floor of the car shuddered.

They ascended.

The acceleration pressed Cassian into the floor. He watched the walls of the shaft slide by. Level 50. Level 40.

They passed the Mid-Levels. Through the glass, he saw the streets. People were running. Steam pipes were bursting, filling the avenues with scalding fog. The alarms were blaring, a rhythmic *whoop-whoop* that vibrated in his teeth.

"Look at them," Elara said. She stood, unconcerned with the speed. "Ants in a farm that is burning."

"They don't know," Cassian said. "They think it's a quake. They don't know the Threshold is broken."

"Then tell them."

"We can't."

"Then you are complicit."

Cassian looked away. He watched the numbers tick by on the altitude dial. 100. 150. The Slums were far below now.

"The pressure," he said. "It’s stabilizing."

"For now," Elara said. "The Sleeper breathes. When it inhales, the city falls. When it exhales, the city rises. We are riding its breath."

Cassian rubbed his left eye. The needle was there, piercing the vision. A migraine blooming behind the socket. The Nausea. He was too close to her. She was a concentrated dose of the Past. Being near her was like standing next to an open nuclear reactor.

"You're fading," she said. She looked at him. Her eyes were black, no whites, just endless obsidian pools.

"I'm fine," Cassian said. He gripped the brass railing of the car. His knuckles were white.

"Your timeline is fraying," she said. "You dig too much. You eat the time like a glutton."

"It's my job."

"It is your death."

The car slowed. They were approaching the Spire Level. The roof of the world.

The glass ceiling of the shaft split open. The car emerged into the Gallery of the Obsidian Eye.

It was beautiful. Terrible.

 It focused the light from the surface world into a single, blinding beam that shot down into the city below.

But the beam was flickering.

The surface storm was raging. The Scorch winds were howling outside the mountain, shaking the foundations.

And standing on the balcony, bathed in that flickering light, was Vex.

He wore a mask of white porcelain. A sleek, expressionless face. He held a sonic cannon in one hand, resting it on the railing.

"Welcome back," Vex said. His voice was smooth. Calm. "I see you brought the fossil."

Cassian stepped out of the car. He kept his body between Vex and Elara.

"She's not a fossil," Cassian said. "She's the Queen."

"She is biomass," Vex said. "And she is obsolete."

Vex raised the cannon. He didn't aim at Elara. He aimed at the Obsidian Eye.

"The Eye must fall," Vex said. "If the city is to survive, we must sever the connection to the surface. We must become a closed system."

"The surface is dead," Vex continued. "There is nothing but fire and wind. We look at it for nostalgia. It is a weakness. We must look inward. Down. Into the deep."

"If you break the Eye," Elara said, stepping forward, "you blind the city."

"We will have our own lights," Vex said. "Powered by the Core. The Vein you tried to hide from us, Elara. The Primary Vein. It is ours now."

He squeezed the trigger.

The cannon didn't fire a bullet. It fired a sound.

A focused beam of pure resonance. It hit the magnetic housing of the Obsidian Eye.

The lens shivered.

Cassian lunged. He didn't have a weapon. He had his wrench. He threw it.

It spun through the air, a heavy disc of steel. It hit Vex’s arm.

The shot went wide. The beam struck the ceiling, shattering a chrome panel. Sparks showered down.

Vex stumbled. He dropped the cannon. It clattered on the marble floor.

He looked at Cassian. The porcelain mask was chipped. A crack ran down the left cheek.

"You dirty little miner," Vex said.

Cassian ran. He didn't run at Vex. He ran for the cannon.

He slid across the floor, his boots squealing on the polished stone. He grabbed the handle of the weapon. It was heavy. Vibrating.

He rolled to his knees. He leveled the cannon at Vex.

"Don't," Cassian said. He was breathing hard. His chest burned. The Nausea was a roar in his ears now. "Don't touch the Eye."

Vex stood up. He brushed dust from his white sleeve. He looked at the cannon, then at Cassian.

"You would shoot me?" Vex asked. "With my own weapon?"

"I'd bury you," Cassian said.

"Then you die with the rest of us," Vex said. He tapped a button on his wrist gauntlet.

The floor groaned.

A loud *clunk* echoed through the dome.

"The supports," Vex said. "I have detached the Spire’s anchorage. We are free. We are falling."

"The city is sinking," Elara said. She walked to the railing. She looked down into the shaft. "He is killing us to feed the beast."

"He is saving us!" Vex shouted. "The pressure is the only way to crack the Threshold! We must crash into the Mantle! We must reach the fuel!"

"You're insane," Cassian said. He tightened his finger on the trigger.

"I am efficient," Vex said. "And you are too late."

Vex pointed to the ceiling.

The Obsidian Eye was coming loose. The magnetic field was failing. The massive black stone was swaying.

"It is too heavy," Vex said. "It will crush the Gallery. It will crush you."

He stepped back toward the exit. "I leave you to your history, Queen. It weighs a lot, doesn't it?"

Vex turned and ran. He moved fast, his white uniform vanishing into the service corridor.

Cassian stood up. He lowered the cannon.

"He's gone," Cassian said.

"He is not the threat," Elara said. "Look."

The Eye.

The massive lens was swinging. The cable holding it up had snapped. It was hanging by a single bolt.

The bolt was groaning. Metal shrieking against metal.

"It’s going to fall," Cassian said.

"If it falls here," Elara said, "it destroys the lift shaft. It collapses the Spire. It seals the roof forever."

"We have to catch it," Cassian said. He looked around. There were winches. There were manual overrides. But they were on the other side of the room.

And the bolt snapped.

The Eye dropped.

It fell in slow motion. A massive monolith of black glass, plummeting toward the glass floor of the gallery.

Cassian didn't think. He moved.

He ran toward the drop zone. He couldn't catch it. It weighed ten tons.

But he could change the frequency.

He skidded to a halt. He raised the sonic cannon. He set the dial to *Maximum*. He set the frequency to *Null*.

"Brace!" he screamed at Elara.

He fired.

The beam hit the falling stone.

The sound was a physical blow. A wall of pressure that knocked Cassian backward.

He hit the floor hard. The air left his lungs.

The stone didn't shatter. It stopped.

 It was a standing wave. A cushion of noise.

"Hold it," Cassian gritted out. His finger was white on the trigger. The cannon was overheating. The barrel was glowing red.

Elara ran to the manual winch on the wall. She grabbed the crank.

"It is rusted," she shouted.

"Break it," Cassian yelled. "Age it! Do what you did to the door!"

She placed her hands on the wheel. She closed her eyes.

The metal turned gray. Then red. It flaked. She cranked. The wheel screamed, shedding layers of rust, turning into smooth, oiled steel beneath her touch.

The cable spooled out. It latched onto the housing of the Eye.

"Got it!" Elara shouted.

"Let go!" Cassian released the trigger.

The cannon died with a whine.

The Eye dropped six inches. The cable went taut.

The glass floor of the gallery cracked under the impact of the vibration, spiderwebbing out from the center. But it held.

Cassian lay on his back. He stared up at the ceiling. The chrome was swirling. His vision was blurring. The Nausea was winning. He could feel his timeline slipping. He was remembering things that hadn't happened yet.

He saw Joren. His brother. Standing over him.

"You're fading, Cass," Joren said. "Let go."

"Not yet," Cassian whispered.

He sat up. He coughed. Black ichor spattered the marble.

Elara was standing over the Eye. She was touching the black glass.

"It remembers," she said. "It remembers the sun."

Cassian crawled to her side. He looked at the reflection in the obsidian. He saw his own face. He looked old. His hair was white. His skin was sagging.

"How long?" he asked.

"I need to get to the Pit," Cassian said. "I need to stop Vex. He's bringing the Spire down on purpose."

"He is already gone," Elara said. "Can you not hear it? The sound of the city breaking?"

Cassian listened.

Below them, the city groaned. A long, deep, mournful sound. The sound of a million tons of stone settling into a new bedrock.

"We are falling," Elara said. "Slowly. But we are falling."

Cassian stood up. He swayed. He grabbed the railing of the balcony.

"Then we have to climb," he said.

"We cannot go up," Elara said. "The surface is closed. Vex saw to that."

"We don't go to the surface," Cassian said. "We go to the Foundation. We go to the Bore. If he wants to crash the city into the Mantle, we have to be there when he lands."

He looked at her. The Queen. The fossil. The woman made of time.

"Can you age stone?" he asked.

"I can return it to dust," she said.

"Good," Cassian said. "Because we're going to have to dig our way down."

He took the sonic cannon. He slung it over his shoulder. The weight was immense. It dragged him down.

He walked to the service door Vex had used.

"The Elevator," Cassian said. "We take it down. All the way to the bottom."

"And then?" Elara asked.

"Then," Cassian said. "We wake the Sleeper properly."

He opened the door.

The staircase spiraled down into darkness.

Cassian stepped onto the first step. His leg shook. The bone felt brittle.

"Come on, Your Highness," he said. "We have a revolution to finish."

They descended into the dark.
