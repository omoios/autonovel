The apple in his hand felt heavy. Heavy as a bearing block.

Cassian took a second bite. The skin snapped. A spray of juice hit his wrist, sticky and sharp. It smelled of rain. It smelled of dirt. It smelled like the world before the smoke.

He leaned back against the balcony railing. The metal was hot, radiating the heat of the beam cutting the sky above. The smog was gone. The gray roof of the world had been peeled back.

Below him, Ouroboros lay exposed.

The Slums were a jagged scar across the city’s flank. Rust and rot. But he could see them now. The individual rivets. The laundry lines strung between ventilation shafts. The thousands of tiny, clinging lives.

Then, the sound.

It started as a rumble. Not the deep geological grinding of the drills. This was higher. A frantic, scratching vibration coming from the balcony floor.

The planter box.

The dirt inside was boiling. The soil, gray and dead a moment ago, had turned black and rich. The green stalk of the Glass-Tree shuddered. Leaves slapped together. *Clap-clap-clap.*

The vibration traveled up the railing, into Cassian’s spine. His teeth ached.

He looked at the apple in his hand. The flesh was oxidizing, turning brown in the air. It was dying. It was decaying. It was doing what living things did.

He threw the core over the edge. It fell, a speck of red vanishing into the abyss.

The sound grew.

The tree’s roots—tangles of copper and lignin—burst through the wooden sides of the planter. They cracked the ceramic. They reached down, piercing the stone floor of the terrace, drilling into the masonry of the Spire.

The Spire groaned. The structural integrity alarm blared, a distant, rhythmic *whoop-whoop-whoop*.

Cassian turned to the glass doors of the Queen’s chamber.

The room was empty. The dust was settling. The throne was a pile of slag. Only the golden skull remained, buried in the dirt of the planter, half-consumed by the roots.

He walked back inside. His boots left footprints of juice and mud on the pristine white tile.

The air pressure changed. His ears popped.

A sharp pain spiked behind his left eye. The Nausea. The under-note. It wasn't a sound this time; it was a pressure gradient. The city was reacting. The past was pushing back.

He grabbed the edge of the doorframe as the Spire listed two degrees to the left. Gravity dragged at his gut.

*Clang.*

From the ventilation shafts. The distinct, hollow report of a mag-lock breaching a seal.

They were here.

Cassian sprinted for the service corridor. He couldn't use the lifts. The Sanitizers would control the verticals. He needed the stairs. The maintenance ladders bolted to the exterior of the Spire.

He hit the door at a sprint. His shoulder slammed the steel bar. The door flew open.

Wind roared in. The updraft from the Pit was fierce, tugging at his clothes, trying to pull him into the void. He stepped onto the grated catwalk.

Below, three hundred feet down, a white hover-barge was docking with the Spire’s mid-level terrace. Sanitizers in white haz-mat armor were spilling out, moving with the fluid precision of mercury. They carried sonic cutters and containment poles.

High Auditor Vex stood at the railing of the barge. Even from this height, the porcelain mask was a stark white circle against the gray city.

Cassian scrambled up the ladder. His hands shook. The Chronite vibration in his blood was interfering with his grip. He kept seeing double—rung and ghost-rung.

He climbed.

Level 90. Level 91.

The Queen’s voice echoed in his memory. *“Reclaim it.”*

He reached the roof access hatch. He spun the wheel. It was stiff. He planted his feet, gritted his teeth, and heaved. The threads groaned.

He shoved the hatch open and rolled onto the roof of the Spire.

The wind was deafening up here. The Obsidian Eye—the massive lens in the cavern ceiling—was focused directly above him. The column of raw sunlight it amplified was a pillar of fire, hotter than a forge.

And at the center of the pillar, standing in the beam, was a man.

He wore the tattered remnants of a Queen’s Guard uniform. The bronze plate was corroded green. The leather was rotted away. But the flesh underneath was gray and stone-like, fossilized.

The Guard was holding a spear. He stood at attention, facing the lens, facing the sun he hadn’t seen in three thousand years.

Cassian crawled forward, keeping low.

The Guard didn't move.

Cassian circled around, staying behind the cover of the ventilation fans.

The Guard’s skin was cracking. Great fissures ran from his neck to his temples. Inside the cracks, a faint blue light pulsed.

Chronite. He was running on residual time. He was a ghost running on fumes.

Cassian stepped on a loose grate. *Creak.*

The Guard snapped around. The movement was unnatural, jerky, like a puppet pulled by strings. The face was gone—worn smooth by erosion—but the eye sockets burned with that blue Chronite fire.

"Halt," the Guard said. The voice was grinding, like stones in a mixer. "State the Era."

Cassian froze. He raised his hands, palms open. "Year 3000," he said. "The Interregnum is over."

The Guard tilted his head. The blue fire flared. "Lies," the Guard rasped. "The Queen sleeps. The city burns. You are a Censor."

The Guard leveled the spear. The tip was a jagged shard of obsidian.

"I'm an Excavator," Cassian said. He took a step back. "I woke her."

The Guard lunged.

He was fast. Faster than a man should be, especially one made of stone. The spear tip sliced the air where Cassian’s neck had been a second before.

Cassian rolled. The roof gravel tore at his coat. He came up with his pick-axe in hand. He swung it, not to strike, but to deflect.

*Clang.*

The pick met the spear shaft. The shockwave traveled up Cassian’s arms, rattling his bones. The Nausea flared—a hot needle behind the eye.

"Traitor," the Guard said. He swung again, a wide arc that cleaved the air.

Cassian ducked under the blow. He drove his shoulder into the Guard’s midsection.

It was like hitting a brick wall. The Guard didn't budge. Cassian bounced off, stumbling back.

The Guard reversed his grip and brought the pommel down on Cassian’s skull.

Cassian saw it coming. He dropped to his knees.

*Crack.*

The pommel smashed the roof tile, sending shards of ceramic flying. A chip grazed Cassian’s cheek. Blood welled, hot and thin.

Cassian scrambled backward, panting. "She told me to plant her!" he shouted over the wind. "She’s a tree now. She’s on the balcony."

The Guard paused. The blue fire in his eyes dimmed, flickered. "A... tree?"

"She’s alive," Cassian said. "But the Sanitizers are coming. They’re below us. If you want to protect her, you have to let me pass."

The Guard straightened. The stone plates of his armor ground together. He looked toward the balcony edge, then back at Cassian.

"Proof," the Guard said.

Cassian reached into his pocket. He pulled out the core of the apple. The brown, oxidized flesh. He tossed it.

The Guard caught it. He sniffed it. He brought it to the slit where his nose should have been.

The Guard shuddered. The blue fire in his eyes turned from cold to warm. A sound escaped him—a wheezing, rattling gasp.

"Organic," the Guard whispered. "Residue."

He lowered the spear.

"The Censor approaches," the Guard said. "I will hold the line."

"You can't fight them all," Cassian said. "They have sonic cannons."

"I am the Queen's Memory," the Guard said. "I do not fade. I erode."

He turned his back on Cassian and walked toward the roof access hatch, planting himself firmly between the door and the lens.

"Go," the Guard said. "To the surface."

Cassian didn't argue. He scrambled to his feet and ran for the maintenance shed at the base of the Obsidian Eye.

Inside the shed, the great winch-motors for the lens were silent. But there was a ladder. A service ladder that led up the interior of the lens shaft, all the way to the ceiling. To the source of the light.

He started climbing.

The rungs were slick with condensation. The heat increased with every foot. The air grew thin. His lungs burned.

Below him, he heard the hatch crash open.

Shouting. The distinctive *hiss* of a sonic cannon discharging.

A roar. Inhuman. The sound of stone breaking.

Cassian climbed faster. He didn't look down.

The heat was becoming unbearable. It felt like the Nausea, but external. Like standing next to an open blast furnace.

His skin prickled. Blisters formed on his knuckles.

He reached the top of the shaft. A circular hatch marked with the symbol of the Founders: A serpent eating its own tail.

He pushed against it.

It didn't move.

He slammed his shoulder into it. Again. Again.

*Groan.*

The metal was expanding due to the heat. It was seized.

He reared back and kicked it with the heel of his boot. The steel-toe cap sparked against the metal.

*Crack.*

The hatch gave way.

Sunlight.

Real, unfiltered, brutal sunlight hit him like a physical blow.

He tumbled out onto the surface.

He lay there, gasping, covering his eyes. The light was too bright. It burned through his eyelids.

He crawled to the edge of the outcropping and looked down.

The world was yellow. Endless yellow dunes. A sea of sand rippling in the wind. No trees. No water. Just the scorched skeleton of the earth.

The Solar Scorch hadn't ended. The world was still dead.

The city of Ouroboros was just a speck in the distance, a black scar on the horizon where they had buried themselves.

He had brought the Queen to a grave.

He looked at his hands. They were red. Burning.

The Nausea hit him then. A wave so strong it knocked him flat.

He convulsed. The needle behind the eye became a drill.

He saw Joren.

His brother was standing in the sand, wearing his miner’s jumpsuit. He was translucent. He was flickering, like a bad lamp connection.

"Cass," Joren said. His voice was dry, like wind over sand. "You can't stay here."

Cassian clawed at the dirt. "I woke her," he rasped. "I did it."

"You brought the sun to a dead world," Joren said. He pointed back toward the hole in the ground. "The life isn't up here. It's down there."

Cassian looked at the hole. The shaft leading back into the dark.

"The Queen is wrong," Joren said. "We can't go back. We can't go forward. We have to stay."

Joren started to dissolve. His body turning into sand, blowing away in the wind.

"Cass," Joren said. "Don't let them drill the Threshold."

The vision shattered.

Cassian blinked. He was alone on the burning sand. The pain in his eyes was blinding.

He realized then.

The Surface wasn't a home. It was a warning.

He crawled back to the hatch. He lowered himself into the shaft, away from the killing light.

As he descended, the air cooled. The shadows embraced him.

He climbed down, rung by rung, his body shaking with exertion and temporal displacement.

He reached the maintenance shed.

The roof was quiet.

The Guard was gone.

A pile of shattered gravel and broken stone lay near the hatch. The white armor of the Sanitizers was scattered, dented, some cracked open.

But in the center of the debris, the Guard’s spear remained. Impaled into the roof deck. Vibrating.

Cassian approached it.

A figure in white stood by the spear. High Auditor Vex.

Vex was holding his porcelain mask in one hand, wiping sweat from his face with a silk handkerchief. His lower jaw was gone—just raw, pink scar tissue and a metal vocal synthesizer.

"A magnificent construct," Vex said. His voice was tinny, synthesized. "Year 50 alloy. Nearly indestructible."

He looked up at Cassian. His eyes were pale, watery blue.

"Did you enjoy the view, Mr. Varr?" Vex asked.

Cassian didn't speak. He gripped the pick-axe until his knuckles turned white.

Vex replaced his mask. *Click.* The porcelain face snapped back into place.

"It's a wasteland," Vex said. "I told you. The past is dead. The surface is dead. Only the city survives. Only the machine."

Vex walked to the edge of the roof and looked down at the Slums.

"The Queen is awake," Vex said. "Or what’s left of her. And she has brought... weeds."

Vex turned back to Cassian. He raised a hand. From the sky above, a massive drill-bit, suspended from a hovering crane, lowered itself toward the roof.

"The Basalt Threshold is compromised," Vex said. "The roots have cracked the foundation. We must accelerate the excavation. We must mine the Primary Vein before the tree poisons the city with nostalgia."

"You can't drill now," Cassian said. "The vibration will collapse the Spire."

"We will reinforce," Vex said. "We always do."

Vex signaled the operator.

The drill began to spin. A high-pitched whine that vibrated in Cassian’s teeth.

Cassian looked at the spear. Then at the drill.

He realized what the Queen meant by *reclaim*.

It wasn't about going up.

She was reclaiming the city from below.

The roots. They weren't just sitting in the planter.

Cassian ran to the edge of the roof and looked over the side of the Spire.

The Glass-Tree was gone.

But the green stalk... it hadn't stopped growing. It had plunged through the balcony floor. It was growing *down* the side of the Spire. A vertical river of green and gold, spiraling down the flank of the city.

It was heading for the Pit.

It was heading for the Basalt Threshold.

It was going to anchor the city before the drill could break it.

Cassian looked at Vex. "You're too late," Cassian said.

Vex tilted his head. "Explain."

"The Queen isn't on the balcony," Cassian said. "She's in the walls."

Below them, a massive tremor shook the city. Not a drill. A growth spurt.

The Spire groaned, metal shrieking as wood expanded within the mortar.

Vex stumbled. "What is that?"

Cassian smiled. Blood was running from his nose, black and thick. He wiped it away with the back of his hand.

"That," Cassian said, "is history taking root."
