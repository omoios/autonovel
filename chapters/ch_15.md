The Spire did not fall. It screamed.

Cassian threw himself flat against the concert floor as the roof split. A fissure wide enough to swallow a man opened near the parapet, vomiting clouds of gray dust and the smell of old, wet mortar. The drill-bit whined above, a toothless god trying to chew through granite, but the earth fought back.

Cassian scrambled backward, boots scrabbling for purchase on the trembling tile. His left eye throbbed—a sharp, hot needle driving itself into the socket. The under-note. The resonance of the deep strata vibrating in harmony with the Queen’s return.

"Structural integrity failing," Vex said. His voice was calm, amplified by the synthesizer in his throat. He stood amidst the chaos, white suit flapping, tapping a command into his wrist-gauntlet. "Reinforce the western buttresses. Deploy the gel-pack."

The crane operator swung the massive drill sideways, fighting the gale-force wind whipping around the Spire. The bit gouged the roof, sending sparks showering down like lethal fireflies.

"You have to stop it!" Cassian shouted. He grabbed the edge of a planter box, hauling himself up. "The resonance! You're matching her frequency!"

Vex turned. His porcelain mask was pristine, untouched by the dust that coated Cassian’s skin and hair. The High Auditor tilted his head. "Frequency analysis is nominal. We are drilling through basalt, not singing to it, Mr. Varr."

"Look at the wall!"

Cassian pointed to the green river cascading down the side of the Spire. The Glass-Tree was no longer a sapling in a box. It was a torrential growth, a vertical forest bursting from the balcony levels below, shattering the pristine white masonry.

Vex looked. The mask hid his expression, but the tapping of his index finger against his cheek stopped. A rare silence.

"Fascinating," Vex said. "Biomass expansion ratio of... incalculable."

"It’s not biomass," Cassian spat. He coughed, wet black flecks hitting his lips. The Nausea was rolling in his gut, a heavy ocean swell. "It’s Elara."

"The Queen is a fossil, Mr. Varr. A memory in the stone." Vex stepped over a fallen tile, his boots making a crisp *click* on the debris. He did not draw a weapon. He did not need to. Two Sanitizers in heavy haz-mat armor stepped out of the haze behind him, leveling sonic cannons at Cassian’s chest.

"Kill him," Vex said. "Then prune the weeds."

The cannons hummed. A high-pitched whine that made Cassian’s teeth ache.

Cassian didn't look at the guards. He looked at the air between him and Vex. The resonance was visible here, a shimmering heat haze. The frequency of the drill—the Sanitizer’s song of consumption—was clashing with the frequency of the Tree—the Queen’s song of growth.

*Dissonance.*

If Cassian could push it. If he could make the gap wider.

He closed his eyes. He ignored the sonic cannons. He ignored the burning in his lungs. He listened to the hum of the Chronite in his blood. He pictured the black vein in the Ossuary. The silence of the deep strata. The cold weight of the past.

He pushed the sound out of his throat. Not a scream. A chord. A low, infrasonic vibration that matched the bedrock.

*Thrum.*

The air rippled.

The Sanitizers stumbled. The sonic cannons wavered, their focus beams distorting as the air pressure dropped.

Vex staggered. He clapped a hand over his ear, the porcelain mask rattling. "What is this? Acoustic sabotage?"

"The past is pushing back," Cassian rasped.

He slammed his boot onto the tile. *Stomp.*

The vibration traveled through the floor, into the bedrock of the Spire, and found the roots.

The roof exploded.

Not from the outside. From within.

A massive root, thick as a subway tunnel, burst through the marble near Cassian’s feet. It was made of iron-wood and gold, glowing with a fierce, internal light. It whipped sideways, catching the lead Sanitizer.

The guard didn't scream. He was lifted off his feet and thrown into the abyss, his white suit a speck vanishing into the smog.

The second guard fired. The sonic bolt struck the root. Bark shattered. Gold dust sprayed.

The root recoiled, then struck again. This time, it slammed into the roof access hatch, crumpling the steel door like paper.

"Enough!" Vex shouted. He raised his hand. A command signal.

The drill-bit above them shrieked. The crane operator swung the massive column of steel down, aiming not for the roof, but for the source of the growth. The balcony where the Tree had begun.

"No!" Cassian scrambled forward. He couldn't reach the drill. He couldn't reach Vex.

But he could reach the Obsidian Eye.

The great lens was mounted on a gimbal at the center of the roof. It was focusing the sunlight, amplifying the heat. It was the engine of this growth.

Cassian sprinted. His legs were heavy, sluggish. The Nausea was pulling at him, trying to drag him into the gray.

Vex saw him move. "Stop him!"

The remaining Sanitizer fired.

Cassian dropped. The sonic bolt singed his hair, the ozone smell stinging his nose. He rolled, came up, and slammed his shoulder into the base of the Obsidian Eye.

The lens was hot. Scorching hot. But the metal housing was cold.

He gripped the manual override clutch. It was rusted shut.

"Give it up, Mr. Varr," Vex said. He walked through the chaos, unconcerned by the whipping roots or the wind. He was ten feet away. "The physics are settled. The city burns. We collect the ash."

Cassian’s hand slipped. The blood on his palms made the grip slick.

Vex raised a small, sleek pistol. Not a sonic cannon. A projectile weapon. Old school.

"Goodbye, Mr. Varr."

Cassian didn't let go. He pulled.

The clutch snapped.

The Obsidian Eye shuddered. The gears ground. The massive lens, fixed on the balcony below, suddenly jerked.

It tilted up.

It caught the beam of direct sunlight and angled it away from the Tree.

It aimed the beam at Vex.

The High Auditor had a moment to tilt his head. "Oh."

Then the wall of heat hit him.

It wasn't a laser. It was just sunlight. Raw, unfiltered, magnified sunlight. But for a man who lived in the dark, for a creature who feared the cold, it was a death sentence.

Vex’s white suit ignited.

He didn't scream. The synthesizer in his throat melted. He flailed, a burning torch stumbling back, crashing into the planter boxes.

The Sanitizer guard broke off, fleeing the heat.

Cassian let go of the clutch. He fell back, panting, shielding his eyes.

The drill-bit above groaned. The crane operator, blinded by the sudden shift in light, panicked.

The cable snapped.

The drill, a hundred tons of steel, dropped.

It didn't hit the roof. It smashed into the side of the Spire, tearing through the lower levels, shearing off the balcony where the Queen had been planted.

The shockwave knocked Cassian flat.

Silence followed. Heavy, ringing silence.

Cassian lay on the tiles. He waited for the pain. The Nausea. The end.

But he felt... cool.

He opened his eyes.

The green light was everywhere.

The roots hadn't just destroyed the roof. They had *invaded* it. They were wrapping around the Obsidian Eye, cradling the lens. Vines of glass and steel were weaving through the debris, sealing the fissures.

And the drill. The massive, fallen drill-bit was embedded in the city wall below. But it wasn't smoking. It was glowing.

Green moss was spreading over the hot steel. Rapidly. Eating the metal.

The city was stopping.

The machine was slowing down.

Cassian sat up. He looked at the spot where Vex had fallen.

There was a pile of ash. And the porcelain mask. Cracked down the center.

Cassian crawled to the edge of the roof.

He looked over the side.

The Slums were dark. The machinery had stopped. The great exhaust fans were silent.

But there was light.

Bioluminescence. The Lamp-Vines were waking up. The roots of the Glass-Tree had pierced the skin of the city, and they were bleeding light into the lower levels.

He looked at his hands. The shaking had stopped. The Nausea was gone.

He picked up a shard of the porcelain mask. It was cool to the touch.

"History," he said. His voice was a rasp. "Takes root."

A leaf drifted down from above. It landed on his knee. Not metal. Real leaf. Veined and fragile.

He crushed it in his fist. The juice stained his skin green.

He stood up. The wind was gone. The air pressure was normal.

He walked to the service hatch. It was bent, buckled by the roots, but open.

He didn't climb down. He looked at the tunnel leading to the Spire interior.

The Sanitizers would be regrouping. The Censors would be panicking.

The Queen wasn't on the balcony anymore. She was in the walls. She was in the water. She was the structure now.

Cassian Varr, the Chronicler, the last Excavator, put the porcelain shard in his pocket.

He had a new job now.

He had to audit the audit.

He descended into the green dark.
