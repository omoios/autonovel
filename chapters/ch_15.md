The Spire groaned, a high-frequency shriek of shearing steel.

Cassian threw himself flat as the roof split. A fissure opened near the parapet, vomiting gray dust and the smell of wet mortar. The drill-bit whined above, grinding uselessly against the granite.

Cassian scrambled backward, boots scrabbling for purchase on the trembling tile. His left eye throbbed—a hot needle driving into the socket. The under-note. The resonance of the deep strata vibrating in harmony with the Queen’s return.

"Structural integrity failing," Vex said. His voice was calm, amplified by the synthesizer in his throat. He stood amidst the chaos, white suit flapping, tapping a command into his wrist-gauntlet. "Reinforce the western buttresses. Deploy the gel-pack."

The crane operator swung the massive drill sideways, fighting the gale-force wind whipping around the Spire. The bit gouged the roof, sending sparks showering down.

"You have to stop it!" Cassian shouted. He grabbed the edge of a planter box, hauling himself up. "The resonance! You're matching her frequency!"

Vex turned. The porcelain mask was pristine, untouched by the dust coating Cassian’s skin. "Frequency analysis is nominal. We are drilling through basalt, Mr. Varr."

"Look at the wall!"

Cassian pointed to the green river cascading down the side of the Spire.

Vex looked. The tapping of his index finger against his cheek stopped.

"Inefficient biomass expansion," Vex said. "Pruning required."

The Nausea rolled in Cassian’s gut. "It’s Elara."

"The Queen is a fossil. A memory in the stone." Two Sanitizers in haz-mat armor stepped out of the haze, leveling sonic cannons at Cassian’s chest.

"Kill him. Then prune the weeds."

Cassian watched the air between him and Vex. The resonance was visible here: a shimmering heat haze where the drill’s frequency clashed with the Tree’s.

*Dissonance.*

If he could push it. If he could make the gap wider.

He listened to the hum of the Chronite in his blood. He pictured the black vein in the Ossuary. The silence of the deep strata. The cold weight of the past.

He pushed the sound out of his throat. Not a scream. A chord. A low, infrasonic vibration matching the bedrock.

*Thrum.*

The air rippled.

The Sanitizers stumbled. The sonic cannons wavered, their focus beams distorting as the air pressure dropped.

Vex staggered. He clapped a hand over his ear, the porcelain mask rattling. "Acoustic sabotage?"

"The past is pushing back," Cassian rasped.

He slammed his boot onto the tile. *Stomp.*

The roof exploded.

Not from the outside. From within.

A massive root, thick as a subway tunnel, burst through the marble near Cassian’s feet. Iron-wood and gold, glowing with internal light. It whipped sideways, catching the lead Sanitizer.

The guard didn't scream. He was lifted off his feet and thrown into the abyss, his white suit a speck vanishing into the smog.

The second guard fired. The sonic bolt struck the root. Bark shattered. Gold dust sprayed.

The root recoiled, then struck again. It slammed into the roof access hatch, crumpling the steel door like paper.

"Enough!" Vex raised his hand.

The drill-bit above them shrieked. The crane operator swung the massive column of steel down, aiming for the balcony where the Tree had begun.

Cassian sprinted, legs heavy.

He couldn't reach the drill. He couldn't reach Vex.

But he could reach the Obsidian Eye.

Vex saw him move. "Stop him!"

The remaining Sanitizer fired.

Cassian dropped. The bolt singed his hair. He rolled, came up, and slammed his shoulder into the base of the Obsidian Eye.

The lens was hot. The metal housing was cold.

He gripped the manual override clutch. It was rusted shut.

"The physics are settled," Vex said. He raised a sleek pistol. "Goodbye, Mr. Varr."

Cassian pulled.

The clutch snapped.

The Obsidian Eye shuddered. The gears ground. The massive lens, fixed on the balcony below, suddenly jerked.

It tilted up.

It caught the beam of direct sunlight and angled it away from the Tree.

It aimed the beam at Vex.

The High Auditor had a moment to tilt his head. "Oh."

Then the wall of heat hit him.

Vex’s white suit ignited. He flailed, a burning torch stumbling back, crashing into the planter boxes.

The Sanitizer guard broke off, fleeing the heat.

Cassian let go of the clutch. He fell back, shielding his eyes.

The drill-bit above groaned. The crane operator, blinded by the sudden shift in light, panicked.

The cable snapped.

The drill, a hundred tons of steel, dropped.

Cassian lay on the tiles. He waited for the impact. The end.

It didn't come.

He opened his eyes.

Green light washed over the roof.

The roots hadn't just destroyed the roof. They had invaded it. They were wrapping around the Obsidian Eye, cradling the lens. Vines of glass and steel wove through the debris, sealing the fissures.

Cassian sat up.

He looked at the spot where Vex had fallen.

A pile of ash. And the porcelain mask. Cracked down the center.

He looked over the edge of the roof.

The Slums were dark. The machinery had stopped. The great exhaust fans were silent.

But there was light.

Bioluminescence. The Lamp-Vines were waking up. The roots of the Glass-Tree had pierced the skin of the city, bleeding light into the lower levels.

He picked up a shard of the porcelain mask. It was cool.

"History," he said. "Takes root."

A leaf drifted down. It landed on his knee. Real leaf. Veined and fragile.

He crushed it in his fist. The juice stained his skin green.

He stood up. The wind was gone.

He walked to the service hatch. It was bent, buckled by the roots, but open.

He didn't climb down. He looked at the tunnel leading to the Spire interior.

The Sanitizers would be regrouping.

Cassian Varr, the Chronicler, put the porcelain shard in his pocket.

He had a new job now.

He had to audit the audit.

He descended into the green dark.
