The steel bit into his palms. Cassian lowered himself, boots scraping against the rusted girder. The metal groaned, a high-pitched whine that vibrated in his molars. The air down here didn't move. It hung, heavy and wet, tasting of sulfur and the copper tang of old blood.

He looked up. Otho’s silhouette blocked the circle of light from the floor above. Kait was a smaller shadow beside her. They were descending. Good.

Cassian dropped the last few feet to the platform below. His knees buckled. He caught himself on a pile of shale, the sharp rocks digging into his gloves. He waited for the spike of nausea. It didn't come. The deeper he went, the quieter the noise in his head became. The constant static of the city—the drills, the presses, the screams of the lifted gears—faded into the earth. The silence was a relief. It felt like pressing a bruised thumb against cold iron.

Otho landed beside him with a grunt. Kait hopped down, light as a rat, clutching the cracked porcelain mask to her chest.

"Quiet," Cassian said.

He pointed to the tunnel ahead. It wasn't a tunnel anymore. It was a throat. The circular bore of the Sanitizer drill had chewed through the basalt, opening a jagged wound in the city's foundation. The walls here were smooth, glass-like, melted by the friction of the bit. But the surface wasn't black.

It was blue.

A vein of Chronite, pure and uncut, pulsed in the rock face. It hummed. A low, resonant frequency that made Cassian’s bones itch. He checked his chronometer. The needle was pinned against the stop.

"Year 50," he whispered. "The Interregnum."

Otho crouched by the wall, running a gloved finger over the blue vein. "It’s active. The stone is... sweating."

"That’s not sweat," Cassian said. "It’s time. The Queen passed through here. She woke the layer."

He moved to the edge of the platform. The Bore did not close. It yawned. A circular wound in the factory floor, exposing the dark, slick throat of the shaft. The air rushing out of it smelled of sulfur and ancient, compressed ice.

Kait joined him at the lip. She kicked a loose cog into the hole. It fell for a long time before striking stone. The sound came back sharp and distant.

*Clack.*

"Deep," Kait said. She peered over the edge. "Like... really deep."

Cassian took a magnesium flare from his bag, struck it against the rough concrete of the floor. A blinding white hiss filled the chamber. He dropped it.

 It illuminated the shaft walls—smooth, glassy obsidian, bored by the machine. It fell past the level of the Excavator catwalks, past the flooded sub-levels, past the rusted remains of the Mid-Levels.

It hit a ledge of jagged, pale rock and died.

"That’s the Threshold," Cassian pointed. The smoke from the flare drifted upward, tasting of ozone. "That pale layer. That’s where they buried her."

"Looks sharp," Kait said.

"It is," Cassian said. "It’s compressed time. It’ll cut your rope if you rub it. It’ll cut your skin if you touch it. Don't touch anything."

"Right," Kait said. "No touching. Just falling."

Cassian clipped the carabiner to the line. "Otho, you go first. You’re the lightest."

Otho nodded. She didn't argue. She stepped to the lip. She didn't look down at Cassian. She looked at the darkness. She closed her eyes, muttered a prayer to the Geologists that sounded like a plea to the bedrock itself, and stepped back into nothing.

The line whined through the drum.

Cassian watched her descent. She was a speck of gray against the black. She slowed as she hit the first friction brake, then dropped again. She moved with the jerky, fluid grace of a spider—too many joints, not enough bone.

"She’s fast," Kait noted.

"She’s scared."

"Your turn," Kait shoved him. "Go."

Cassian went.

The descent was not silent. The rope sang in the housing—a high-pitched *keening* that vibrated in the metal of his harness. The air pressure changed instantly. The factory smell of ozone and burnt hair vanished, replaced by the smell of the deep. Dry dust. Old stone. The metallic tang of Chronite.

Cassian controlled the drop with the leather glove on his right hand. He let the rope slide, palm burning, then gripped to slow. The wall rushed past in a blur.

He passed the Industrial Zone. Abandoned drills hung like dead insects from the ceiling, their bits spinning lazily in the updraft.

He passed the Slums. He saw the tops of the tenement blocks, rusted through, lights flickering in the windows. People were looking up. He felt their eyes like insects on his skin. Then the darkness swallowed them.

The Nausea started as a hum in his gut.

It was the vibration. The deeper you went, the closer you got to the raw Chronite veins, the more the Earth tried to sync you with its rhythm. His heart beat a frantic, arrhythmic tap-dance against his ribs. He checked his Chronometer.

*Year 500.*

The air grew hot.

The rope jerked. Otho had landed.

Cassian slowed his descent. His boots crunched onto the ledge.

It was a shelf of white rock, jutting out into the shaft. The Basalt Threshold.  It was hard as diamond and cold as the void.

Otho was crouched by a fissure in the wall. She had a small lamp out, the light weak and yellow against the oppressive dark of the shaft.

"Cassian," she whispered.

He unclipped and moved to her. The gravity here was heavier. Every step felt like he was carrying a second person on his shoulders. His knees popped. The Nausea spiked—a sharp pain behind the left eye, the needle twisting.

"Look," Otho said.

She pointed the light into the fissure.

The stone was cracked. Not naturally. Split open from the inside. Green moss—Chronite residue—grew thick in the crack.

"She came through here," Cassian said. He touched the green moss. It hissed under his fingertip. A spark jumped to his hand. He pulled back, shaking his fingers. "Recently. The heat is still on it."

"Where did she go?" Kait’s voice crackled over the comms in Cassian’s ear. She was still descending, somewhere above them.

"Down," Cassian said. "There's a vein."

He looked at the strata. The white Basalt layer ended abruptly, sheared off by a violent force. Beneath it lay the Black Shale. The Pre-Scorch Era.

"That shouldn't be there," Otho said. Her voice trembled, not from fear, but from the sheer physical effort of speaking against the pressure. "The Shale is unstable. It’s gas pockets and old memories. If she’s digging through that..."

"She’s causing a collapse," Cassian finished. "Or a bleed."

A sound echoed from the fissure. *Clack. Scrape.*

Metal on stone.

"Kait," Cassian said into the comms. "Hurry. And bring the pry-bar."

"On my way," Kait’s voice was breathless. "This rope is burning my hands. I think I have blisters."

"Keep the grip loose. Let the leather take the weight."

"Easy for you to say, you have calluses."

Cassian ignored her. He pulled the tuning fork from his belt. It was a heavy piece of steel, calibrated to the frequency of the Basalt layer. He struck it against the wall.

It didn't ring. It gave a dull, wet thud.

"The resonance is dead," Cassian said. "The layer is desaturated."

"Because she took the time," Otho said. "She’s consuming the rock to manifest."

Kait dropped onto the ledge, landing in a crouch. She rolled, absorbing the impact, and popped up. "I’m here! I’m here! Did I miss the monster?"

"No monster," Cassian said. He pointed to the fissure. "Just a hole."

Kait peered in. "It smells like... burnt hair and peppermint."

"Decay," Cassian said. "Come on."

Cassian crawled into the fissure.

It was tight. The rock pressed against his back and chest. He had to exhale to inch forward. The dark was absolute, save for the narrow beam of Otho’s lamp ahead. The sound of his own breathing was deafening, a rasp in his ears. The Nausea roared now—a cacophony of whispers.

*...help... cold... dig...*

 The rock recorded everything. If you pressed your ear to the shale in the Pit, you could hear the screams of the city from a thousand years ago.

The tunnel opened up suddenly.

Cassian tumbled out onto a floor of cracked tile. He rolled, came up with the pry-bar raised.

They were in a hall. It was vast, ceiling lost in the gloom above. The walls were lined with pillars of obsidian. But the pillars were broken. Shattered.

In the center of the hall stood a machine.

It wasn't Sanitizer tech. It was too old. Too ornate. It looked like a ribcage made of brass and iron, enclosing a spinning core of green light. The green light pulsed, pushing against the walls, expanding and contracting.

And standing next to it, holding a human heart in her hand, was a man.

He wore white armor, stained with grime. He had a respirator mask pulled down around his neck. His skin was pale, almost translucent.

Vex.

Cassian froze. The Nausea spiked so hard his vision blurred. He staggered, catching himself on a pillar.

Vex looked up. He didn't look surprised. He looked bored.

"The Chronicler," Vex said. His voice was smooth, untroubled by the crushing gravity. "You are late for the execution."

He squeezed the heart.

It wasn't a heart. It was a sphere of black crystal. A Chronite Node.

The machine spun faster. The green light turned red.

"What are you doing?" Cassian shouted. He started forward.

Otho grabbed his harness. "Don't. It’s a trap."

"I know it’s a trap," Cassian said. He didn't stop. "Vex!"

Vex dropped the crystal into a slot on the machine. The machine roared. A shockwave of heat blasted outward, knocking Cassian off his feet. He skidded across the tile, scraping his elbows.

"I am optimizing," Vex said. He dusted off his gloves. "The Queen’s passage destabilized the vein. I am simply... extracting the excess before the sector collapses."

"You’re killing the layer," Cassian said. He scrambled up. "That’s the Foundation Era. You wipe that, the Mid-Levels fall into the Pit."

"The Mid-Levels are already falling," Vex said. "I am merely accelerating the inevitable to harvest the energy. Efficiency, Cassian. It is not a dirty word."

Kait scrambled out of the tunnel, saw Vex, and immediately scrambled back in. " Nope. Nope nope. That’s the bad man."

"Stay there, Kait," Cassian ordered.

Vex drew a pistol from his hip. It was a Sanitizer-issue sonic cannon. Small, handheld, but enough to turn a man’s internal organs to jelly.

"Where is she, Vex?" Cassian asked. He circled left, putting a pillar between him and the gun.

"The Queen?" Vex laughed. A dry, chuckling sound. "She is where she belongs. In the dirt. I tried to tell her. The past is fuel. She refused to burn. So I buried her."

"You didn't bury her," Cassian said. "You murdered her."

"I liberated her city," Vex corrected. "And now, I will liberate yours."

Vex raised the gun.

Cassian didn't think. He moved.

He threw the pry-bar.

It wasn't a weapon, but it was heavy iron. It spun through the air, end over end.

Vex shifted his arm to block it.

The iron slammed into Vex’s wrist. There was a crack of porcelain—the sound of a plate shattering. The gun went off.

The beam missed Cassian by an inch. It struck the pillar behind him. The stone didn't explode; it liquefied. Melted into glowing slag.

Cassian tackled Vex.

They hit the ground hard. The breath left Cassian’s lungs in a rush. Vex was strong—unnaturally strong. He felt like metal wrapped in silk.

"Fool," Vex hissed. He drove a knee into Cassian’s ribs. Cassian felt a bone snap.

Cassian didn't let go. He grabbed Vex’s porcelain mask. He hooked his fingers into the eyeholes and pulled.

Vex screamed. It wasn't a human scream. It was the squeal of a pig, the shriek of a train whistle.

The mask came away.

Underneath, there was nothing. No jaw. No tongue. Just a wet, raw cavity of muscle and bone, pulsating with green Chronite veins. A speaker box embedded in the throat clicked and buzzed.

Cassian recoiled, scrambling back. He dropped the mask.

Vex stood up. The hole of his face worked, the muscle bunching and spasming. The speaker box synthesized a voice, flat and cold.

"You see?" Vex said. "The cost of power. I am the future, Cassian. I am pure efficiency. I have removed the weakness of flesh."

Cassian wiped blood from his lip. He stood up. His side throbbed where the rib was cracked. The Nausea was a siren in his head, drowning out everything.

"You’re a monster," Cassian said.

"I am a survivor," Vex said. He raised the sonic cannon again. "And you are sediment."

The machine in the center of the room whined. The red light spiked. The floor shook.

"Vex," Otho shouted from the tunnel mouth. "Look at the gauge!"

Vex glanced at the machine. The pressure needle was in the red.

"The vein is collapsing," Vex said. He sounded annoyed. "Your interference has compromised the structural integrity."

He pointed the gun at the machine. "I must take the core and go."

"Don't touch it!" Cassian yelled. "It’s unstable!"

Vex ignored him. He reached for the green core.

The ceiling groaned. A massive crack split the obsidian dome. Dust rained down.

Cassian looked at Kait, peeking out of the tunnel. She was holding the tuning fork.

"Kait!" Cassian yelled. "Throw it!"

"What?"

"The fork! Throw it at the machine!"

Kait looked at the steel fork in her hand, then at the machine, then at Cassian. She shrugged.

"Okay!"

She wound up and threw like a pitcher. The tuning fork sailed across the room.

It struck the side of the machine, right where the Chronite core met the brass housing.

*Ping.*

The resonance matched perfectly.

The machine shrieked. The green core flared brighter, blindingly bright. The frequency shifted. The sonic waves that had been holding the rock together suddenly inverted.

The floor dropped out.

The machine, the core, and Vex were swallowed by the earth as the shale simply ceased to be solid. It turned to liquid sludge.

Vex didn't scream. He just sank, his white armor swallowed by the black muck. He reached up, his hand grasping at the air, then disappeared.

The liquid rock swirled, draining away into the deeper dark, leaving a jagged hole where the machine had been.

Silence returned to the hall. Heavy, dust-laden silence.

Cassian lay on his back, staring up at the cracked ceiling. He couldn't feel his legs. The Nausea was gone, replaced by a cold numbness.

"Cassian?" Otho crawled to him. She pressed a hand to his chest. "Heartbeat. Fast. But there."

"He’s gone," Cassian whispered.

"The machine?" Otho asked.

"Gone."

"Vex?"

"Gone."

"Good," Otho said. She coughed, a wet, hacking sound. She wiped her mouth. "Less paperwork."

Kait ran over. She looked at the hole where Vex had vanished. "Cool. Did you see his face? It was all... squishy."

"Yeah," Cassian said. "I saw."

He sat up. The pain in his side was sharp, hot. He pressed his hand to his ribs. Definitely broken.

"We have to keep moving," Cassian said. "That collapse... it would have opened the path to the Mantle."

"You’re insane," Kait said. "You’re actually crazy. We just died. We died and we’re ghosts now."

"The Queen went down there," Cassian said. He used the pillar to pull himself upright. "She’s heading for the fire. If she wakes the Mantle, the city burns. We have to stop her."

"You can’t walk," Otho said.

"I can limp."

Otho shook her head. She took a roll of medical tape from her pocket. "Turn around. I’ll strap you up."

She bound his ribs tight. The compression helped. He could breathe again, shallow breaths, but breathe.

"Thank you," Cassian said.

"Don't thank me," Otho said. "Thank the girl. She has good aim."

Kait beamed. She picked up something from the floor. It was the mask. Vex’s mask. It had cracked when Cassian dropped it, splitting the porcelain cheek.

"Can I keep this?" Kait asked. "It’s creepy."

"Yes," Cassian said. "You can keep it."

He looked at the hole in the floor. Heat was rising from it now. Not the dry heat of the drills, but the wet, sulfurous heat of the deep earth.

"The Mantle," Cassian said. He stepped toward the edge.

"Wait," Kait said. "Listen."

They all stopped.

From the depths of the hole, a sound drifted up.

It wasn't a drill. It wasn't a machine.

It was a voice. Singing.

The song was low, wordless. A lullaby for a dying world.

"The Queen," Cassian said.

"She’s calling us," Kait said. She didn't sound scared anymore. She sounded entranced.

"She’s calling the fire," Cassian corrected. "Come on."

He lowered himself over the edge, finding a handhold on the exposed girders of the level below.

"Cassian," Otho said.

He looked up.

"If we go down there," Otho said, "we might not come back up. The Lag... it’s too strong. We’ll fade."

Cassian looked at the dark below. He thought of Joren. He thought of the needle in his eye. He thought of the white dust falling on the city like snow.

"Then we fade," Cassian said. "But we do it on our feet."

He climbed down into the dark.
