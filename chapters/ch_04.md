The rungs of the service ladder were cold enough to burn skin. Cassian’s gloves were shredded, his palms sticky with half-dried blood, but he didn’t stop. He descended into the throat of the shaft.

Kait followed above him, her boots clanking rhythmically against the metal. She didn't complain. The shockwave had knocked the wind out of both of them, leaving a ringing silence that felt heavier than the dark.

"Slow down," Kait hissed. Her voice was a raw scrape.

Cassian didn't answer. He pushed off the wall with his feet, sliding down the ladder rails, skipping three rungs at a time. The friction heated the soles of his boots.

The smell hit him first. It wasn't the familiar coal-dust or the oily reek of machinery. It was old air. Air that had been sealed in a vacuum for three thousand years. It tasted like dry-rot and ancient spices.

"Cassian," Kait said. "I can't see the bottom."

It wasn't just dark. The dark in the Pit usually had texture—the flicker of a lamp-vine, the dull red glow of a heating coil. This was a void. A total absence of photons.

Cassian’s left eye throbbed. A sharp, piercing pressure, like a needle working its way into the optic nerve. The Nausea. It wasn't just the altitude; it was the temporal displacement. They were passing out of Year 3000, descending through the numbered layers of history, falling backward in time.

He checked the glowing dial on his wrist. The Chronometer needle spun wildly, clicking over the years as he dropped.

*2800. 2500. 2000.*

"You feel that?" Kait whispered.

"Yeah."

"It feels like... static. On my arms."

"Hairstatics," Cassian said, his voice flat. "The air is charged. Keep your mouth shut. Don't breathe it if you can help it."

He hit a landing platform. Year 1800. The Rust Belt. The metal here was pitted, orange with corrosion that looked like leprosy. He vaulted the railing and dropped into the next shaft.

The Queen’s layer was close. The Basalt Threshold.

The needle on the Chronometer stopped spinning. It jammed against the pin. Year 50.

Cassian landed hard on a surface that wasn't metal. It rang like stone. He clicked on his shoulder lamp. The beam cut through the darkness, illuminating a wall of smooth, black rock. No seams. No rivets. Just a single, flawless cylinder of basalt.

"We're here," Cassian said.

He touched the wall. It vibrated. A low, subsonic hum that made his teeth ache. Behind this rock lay the Primary Vein. And the Queen.

Kait dropped down beside him, hugging herself. "It’s cold. Colder than the Slums."

Cassian pulled the *Ledger of Years* from his belt. The book was trembling, the leather cover hot to the touch. He flipped it open. The redacted pages were no longer blank. Ink was bleeding onto the paper, forming jagged, frantic letters.

*THE PRISON IS BREACHED. THE INTERREGNUM ENDS.*

"She’s waking up," Cassian said.

He scanned the wall. The Sanitizers hadn't cracked this layer. They had only shattered the drill above. The Basalt was intact. But the vibration was getting stronger. The frequency was changing.

"We have to crack it," Cassian said. He unslung his pickaxe. It was a heavy piece of iron, the head tipped with diamond-hard diamond-saw.

"Crack it?" Kait backed away, her eyes wide. "You said the resonance would kill us. If we break the seal without a dampener field—"

"If we don't, she suffocates in there," Cassian snapped. "Or worse. The Sanitizers will bring down the big drills in an hour. They'll turn her into sludge." He positioned the pick against the black stone. "I need you to listen."

"Listen to what?"

"The rock. It sings. I need you to find the pitch." Cassian gripped the pick handle. His left eye felt like it was bleeding. The pain was blinding. "When I strike, I want you to hum. Match the tone. If we harmonize, we can split the seam without triggering the collapse."

"I don't know the song!"

"You will. Listen to the hum in your teeth. That's the Year 50 frequency." Cassian raised the pick. "Ready?"

Kait hesitated, then nodded. She pressed her ear to the cold stone.

Cassian swung.

The pick struck the basalt. *Clang.*

A spark flew, blinding white. The sound was dull, dead. Wrong.

"No!" Cassian shouted. "Higher! You were flat!"

He swung again. *Clang.*

The floor shuddered. Dust sifted down from the invisible ceiling miles above.

"Cassian, stop!" Kait screamed. "It's not working!"

"Closer," Cassian gritted out. He raised the pick a third time. He ignored the blood dripping from his nose. He ignored the Nausea twisting his gut into knots. He focused on the vibration in the stone. It was a scream, buried deep. He needed to let it out.

He swung.

This time, the sound didn't die. It rang. A pure, clear note that hung in the air like a bell.

Kait’s voice joined it. She hummed, a wavering, uncertain note that slid into place. She harmonized with the ring of the steel.

The black stone groaned.

A crack appeared. It ran from the impact point, zigzagging up the wall like a lightning bolt. Light leaked out of the crack. Not the green glow of Chronite. A harsh, magnesium-white light.

"Stand back!" Cassian yelled.

He shoved Kait away as the basalt exploded outward.

A chunk of rock the size of a cartwheel missed his head by an inch. The air rushed out of the chamber, replaced by a sudden, violent wind. The pressure change popped Cassian’s ears.

The dust cleared.

In the wall, there was a hole. Rough, jagged, leading into the dark.

Cassian pointed his lamp into the breach.

It wasn't a vein of oil. It was a chamber. A small, cylindrical room carved directly into the bedrock.

And there was a figure standing inside.

Cassian’s breath hitched. It wasn't a fossil. It wasn't bones.

It was a woman. She wore armor that looked like it was made of gold and glass, now covered in three millennia of gray dust. She stood upright, arms crossed over her chest, her hand resting on the hilt of a sword.

She was frozen. Not just still—she was a statue. Her skin had turned to black stone, her hair to spun iron.

"Is that her?" Kait breathed. She crept closer, peering over Cassian’s shoulder. "She looks... small."

"That’s the Queen," Cassian said. He stepped into the chamber. The air here was dry, perfectly preserved. No dust. No smell of decay. Just the faint scent of lavender and ozone.

He reached out to touch her shoulder.

"Don't," Kait warned. "The Nausea—"

"I have to." Cassian pressed his hand against the stone cheek.

The reaction was instant.

A shockwave slammed into Cassian’s chest. It wasn't physical; it was temporal. He gasped, his vision blurring. He wasn't in the cavern anymore. He was standing in a hall of marble. Sunlight—real, yellow sunlight—streamed through windows. People were screaming. The sound of drilling. The sound of stone cracking.

*The memory.*

He saw the Queen. She was young. Her face was pale, her eyes terrified but fierce. She was shouting orders. "Seal the door! Don't let them take the fuel!"

Then, the ceiling fell. Darkness. Compression. The weight of the city pressing down, crushing her, flattening her into the rock.

Cassian ripped his hand away, stumbling back. He hit the wall, sliding to the floor. His heart was racing so fast it felt like a single continuous hum.

"Cassian!" Kait grabbed his shoulders. "You’re bleeding. Your eyes!"

Cassian wiped his face. His hand came away black. Not ichor. Just shadow. He blinked, clearing his vision.

The Queen was moving.

It was subtle. A flake of stone falling from her cheek. A finger twitching against the sword hilt. The stone skin was cracking, shedding like a dry scab.

"Give me the Ledger," Cassian rasped.

Kait handed it over. Cassian flipped to the blank page at the back. He needed a quill. He used his own blood, dragging a finger across the paper.

*Subject: Elara. Status: Recovered.*

The chamber shook. A violent tremor. Rocks fell from the ceiling.

"We have to move," Cassian said. He stood up, swaying. "She's destabilizing the strata just by waking up."

He grabbed the Queen’s arm. It was heavy, impossibly heavy. "Kait, grab her legs. We have to haul her out."

"She weighs a ton!" Kait grunted, straining. "She’s still rock!"

"Pull!"

They dragged the statue out of the chamber, into the main shaft. As soon as her feet cleared the threshold, the chamber behind them imploded. The rock collapsed, sealing the tomb forever.

They lay on the floor of the shaft, panting.

The Queen lay between them. The stone shell was crumbling faster now. Large chunks fell away, shattering into dust. Underneath, there was flesh. Pale, cold flesh, but flesh.

Her chest rose. A shallow, rattling inhale.

Cassian scrambled to his knees. He shone his lamp in her face.

Her eyes snapped open.

They were black. No whites. Just solid, obsidian disks.

She sat up. The movement was jerky, unnatural, like a puppet being pulled by rough hands. She looked at her hands. She looked at the broken stone skin on the floor.

Then she looked at Cassian.

She didn't speak. She opened her mouth, and a sound came out. A grinding, mechanical sound, like tectonic plates shifting.

Cassian scrambled back, grabbing Kait, pulling her behind him.

The Queen’s eyes focused. The blackness receded, revealing pupils. Human pupils. She blinked. She took a breath. The grinding sound stopped. She coughed, a cloud of gray dust puffing from her lungs.

"Where," she croaked. Her voice was ruined by three thousand years of disuse. "Where is the sun?"

Cassian stared. "There is no sun," he said. "The surface is burned."

The Queen turned her head slowly. She looked up the shaft, toward the faint, distant lights of the city above. Then she looked down, into the Pit. She stood up. She was unsteady, but her presence filled the tunnel. The air around her seemed to warp, shimmering with heat.

"You," she said. She pointed a finger at Cassian. The finger was trembling. "You are the thief."

"I'm the Excavator," Cassian said. "I got you out."

"You broke the seal." She stepped closer. Her armor clinked—faint, musical sounds. "Do you know what you have done? The Silence was a cage. And you have opened the door."

She looked past him, into the dark of the Pit. Her expression changed. From confusion to horror.

"It's awake," she whispered.

"What?" Cassian followed her gaze. The dark hole below them seemed endless. "What's awake?"

"The thing we buried," the Queen said. She grabbed Cassian’s jacket. Her grip was iron. "We didn't drill for the oil. We stopped the drill because we hit the Mantle. And something... something heard us."

The ground lurched. Not a tremor. A step.

Something massive had moved beneath them.

Cassian’s Chronometer spun backward. The needle snapped off.

"We have to go," Kait yelled. "The whole shaft is coming down!"

"Run," the Queen commanded. Her voice regained some of its authority. "To the Spire. We must sever the connection."

"We can't go up!" Cassian shouted. "The Sanitizers will be waiting. They’ll kill us and take you."

"They will try," the Queen said. She drew her sword. The metal was no longer stone; it was gleaming steel, etched with runes that glowed with a faint blue light. "I am Elara, of the House of Lights. I do not hide from rats."

She started up the ladder. She moved with a terrifying speed, ignoring her exhaustion, climbing like a spider.

Cassian looked at Kait. "We stick with her. She's the only chance we have."

"I don't like her," Kait muttered. "She looks at us like we're dirt."

"She's a Queen," Cassian said. "That's what they do."

He grabbed the ladder and started to climb.

***

They climbed for an hour. The Nausea faded as they ascended, leaving Cassian with a splitting headache and a deep, hollow fatigue.

The Queen—Elara—didn't slow down. She didn't look back. She climbed with a singular, driving purpose.

They reached the Mid-Levels. The Rust Belt.

The industrial noise was deafening. Hissing steam, clanking gears, the shouts of foremen. The smog hung low, a yellow fog that smelled of sulfur and burnt grease.

Cassian pulled Kait and Elara into the shadow of a massive ventilation duct. "Stay low," he whispered. "The patrols are thick here."

Elara wrinkled her nose. "The air. It is... poisoned."

"It's recycled," Cassian said. "It keeps us alive."

"It kills you slowly," she countered. "I can hear the death in your lungs. A rattle. Like dry leaves."

She looked around at the rusted iron walkways, the dripping pipes, the piles of discarded slag. "This is my city?"

"It grew," Cassian said. "While you slept."

"It rotted," she said.

A whirring sound approached. A Sanitizer drone. A spherical metal device with a red scanning lens. It hovered near the walkway, sweeping back and forth.

"Down," Cassian hissed.

They pressed themselves flat against the grating. The drone buzzed overhead. The red light swept over them.

Cassian held his breath. The light passed over Kait, lingered on her colorful rags, then moved on.

Once it was gone, Cassian exhaled. "We need to move. The Grand Lift is two sectors over. If we can hijack a cargo hauler, we can ride it to the Spire."

"No," Elara said. She pointed toward a massive, cylindrical structure that dominated the center of the Mid-Level. A tower of black glass and steel. "We go there."

"That’s the Sanitation Directorate," Cassian said. "That's Vex's headquarters."

"Yes," Elara said. "The source of the rot."

"We can't just walk in the front door," Kait said. "They have sonic cannons. They’ll turn us to jelly."

"We don't walk in," Elara said. She looked at the massive pipes feeding into the building. "We go through the veins. The city is a body. We are the infection."

Cassian looked at the pipes. They were massive, carrying pressurized sludge and Chronite slurry up to the Spire.

"The pressure in those lines will crush us," Cassian said.

"Not if the flow is reversed," Elara said. She placed her hand on the pipe. She closed her eyes. She hummed. A low, resonant note.

The pipe shuddered. The metal groaned.

Cassian felt the vibration in his boots. She was using the resonance. She was tuning the metal.

"Stand back," she said.

She struck the pipe with the hilt of her sword.

*Boom.*

The valve blew. Slurry sprayed out, coating the walkway in gray muck. But the flow stopped. The pipe was empty.

"The pressure is equalized," Elara said. "We climb."

She pulled open the maintenance hatch and slipped inside.

Cassian looked at Kait. Kait looked back, her eyes wide with a mix of terror and awe.

"She's crazy," Kait whispered.

"She's the Queen," Cassian said again. He wiped the sludge from his goggles. "Come on."

They crawled into the pipe.

It was dark. It smelled of old oil and something sweet—fermentation. They moved on hands and knees. The metal was slick.

"Cassian," Kait whispered from behind him. "Your brother."

Cassian froze. "What about him?"

"When we were in the chamber... when you touched the Queen... I saw him."

Cassian’s heart skipped. "You saw Joren?"

"I saw a shadow. Standing behind you." Kait’s voice trembled. "He looked... young. He was holding a clock."

Cassian gripped the sides of the pipe. The Nausea spiked again. The needle behind his eye twisted. "It's a hallucination. The Chronite fumes."

"It wasn't a fume," Kait said. "He looked at me. He said, 'The clock is wrong.'"

Cassian didn't answer. He pushed forward, crawling faster. He didn't want to hear about Joren. He didn't want to think about the possibility that his brother was trapped in the timeline, a ghost in the machine.

They reached a junction. The pipe branched.

"Left or right?" Kait asked.

Cassian closed his eyes. He listened to the hum of the city. The vibration of the machinery. One path felt heavy, sluggish. The other felt sharp, fast, electric.

"Right," Cassian said.

They crawled. The pipe began to slope upward.

Then, the pipe ended.

It opened into a large room. Cassian poked his head out.

It was an office. High ceilings. Polished mahogany floors that looked ancient. The walls were lined with books—real paper books, not digital screens.

And there was a desk. A massive slab of black obsidian.

Sitting behind the desk was a man in a white uniform. He wore a porcelain mask.

High Auditor Vex.

He wasn't looking at them. He was looking at a screen on his desk. He was talking. His voice was smooth, synthesized.

"Increase the drill output. The Basalt has been breached. I don't care about the structural integrity. Bring me the Primary Vein."

Elara stepped out of the pipe.

She walked toward the desk.

Vex turned his head. The pale blue eyes behind the mask widened.

"Guards—" Vex started.

Elara moved. She was a blur. She leaped over the desk, her sword flashing.

Vex scrambled back, knocking over his chair. He raised a hand. A sonic emitter on his wrist activated. A wave of distorted air slammed into Elara, throwing her back.

She hit the bookshelf, books tumbling down around her. She shook her head, dazed.

Cassian scrambled out of the pipe. He grabbed a heavy brass lamp from the side table and hurled it at Vex.

Vex deflected it with a sonic blast, but it bought Elara a second.

She surged to her feet. She didn't attack Vex. She slashed at the machinery on his desk. The screens shattered. Sparks flew.

"The connection," she shouted at Cassian. "Sever it!"

Cassian ran to the wall panel. It was a mass of wires and glowing crystals. This was the control hub for the drills.

He pulled his pickaxe. He raised it high.

"Stop!" Vex screamed. His voice was no longer smooth. It was distorted, glitching. "You'll destroy the grid! The city will go dark!"

"Better dark than dead," Cassian said.

He swung.

The panel exploded.

The lights in the office died. The hum of the ventilation stopped.

The city groaned. A deep, agonized sound of metal under stress.

In the darkness, Vex laughed. It was a dry, hollow sound.

"You fool," Vex said from the shadows. "You didn't cut the power. You cut the brakes."

The floor tilted.

Cassian slid across the polished mahogany. He slammed into the wall.

"The Spire is falling," Kait screamed.

Cassian looked out the window. The massive tower of the Spire was tilting. The bolts were shearing. The counterweights had failed.

"We have to jump," Elara said. She grabbed Cassian’s collar. "Now."

"Where?" Cassian asked.

"To the next level," she said. She pointed to the window. "We fly."

She kicked the window. The glass shattered. The wind howled. Below them, the city was a swirling vortex of lights and smoke.

She grabbed Kait with one hand and Cassian with the other.

"Hold on," she said.

She jumped.

They fell.

Cassian’s stomach lurched into his throat. The wind roared.

They didn't hit the bottom. Elara’s sword caught in the gantry of a lower level. She swung them, like a pendulum, momentum carrying them across the abyss.

They slammed into a metal grating. The Spire groaned above them, shedding debris like rain.

Cassian lay on his back, gasping. He looked up. The Spire hadn't fallen. It had listed. It was leaning against the cavern wall, crippled but stable.

Vex was still up there. And the Queen was here.

Elara stood up. She was bleeding from a cut on her forehead. She looked battered, but unbowed.

"We did not sever the head," she said. She looked up at the tilting tower. "But we have wounded the beast."

Cassian sat up. He checked his pockets. The *Ledger of Years* was safe. He checked the Chronometer. It was broken, the needle bent at an awkward angle.

"What now?" Kait asked. She was shaking, hugging her knees.

"Now," Cassian said. He looked at the Queen. "We find a new hole to hide in."

Elara looked at him. She smiled, a grim, terrifying expression.

"We do not hide," she said. "We dig."

Cassian looked at the ground beneath them. The metal was vibrating. A new sound. A deep, rhythmic thumping. Like a heartbeat.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

It wasn't coming from the city.

It was coming from below.

"The Mantle," Cassian whispered.

"The thing I buried," Elara said. Her smile faded. "It is waking up."

The ground cracked. A fissure opened up between them. Hot steam hissed out.

Cassian grabbed Kait and rolled back. Elara stood her ground, looking into the crack.

"We need to run," Cassian said.

"No," Elara said. She peered into the steam. "It's not coming up."

"Then what is it?"

"It is calling," she said. "Calling for the King."

She turned to Cassian. "You said your brother was lost."

"He was erased," Cassian said.

"Nothing is ever truly erased," Elara said. "Not in this city. It is just... compressed."

She pointed to the crack.

"He is down there."

Cassian stared into the dark. The Nausea flared, blinding him. But for a second, he saw it. A shape in the steam. A familiar silhouette.

Joren.

He wasn't a ghost. He was a resource. He was fuel.

"He's in the Chronite," Cassian realized. "He's part of the Vein."

"If we drill," Elara said. "We can bring him back."

"But we'll wake the other thing," Kait said.

Cassian looked at the crack. He looked at his hands. The tattooed bars on his arms were fading, but the scars on his palms were deep.

"We have a choice," Cassian said. "Save the city. Or save the past."

Elara rested her hand on his shoulder. Her touch was cold, but steady.

"We are excavators, Cassian," she said. "We dig. We decide what is worth keeping. And what is worth leaving buried."

Cassian stood up. He took the pickaxe from his belt. He tested the weight.

The city groaned above them. The beast stirred below.

He looked at the crack.

"Get the shovel," Cassian said.
