The maintenance shaft was a throat of greased steel. It smelled of ozone and sulfur.

Cassian hauled himself up, hooking his elbows over the lip of the hatch, and rolled onto the floor of the Spire. The air here was different. Thin. Sterile. It smelled of synthetic lavender and cold air.

He lay there for a second, his heart hammering against the marble like a trapped bird.

Elara dropped beside him. She landed in a crouch, silent, absorbing the impact with knees that didn't bend so much as compress. She was covered in gray slurry from the shaft, her rags streaked with the black oil of the deep strata.

She looked at her hands. She rubbed her fingers together, spreading the grease.

"Polish," she said. "We are in the polishing room."

Cassian pushed himself up. His joints popped. The Nausea receded in the clean air, replaced by a dull, throbbing pressure behind his eyes. He checked the hallway.

White walls. White ceiling. A line of copper molding traced the corner, pristine and bright. No rust. No soot.

"It's the Gallery," Cassian said. "The service entrance."

He moved to the door. He pressed his eye against the hinge-gap.

Light spilled out. Not the dim blue of the Pit, but the harsh, uncompromising white of the Obsidian Eye amplified through quartz lenses.

He saw the table. Long, dark wood, reflecting the ceiling. Silver service. Crystal goblets filled with amber liquid.

And the people.

They sat in high-backed chairs. Men and women in white uniforms, stiff and formal. They wore porcelain masks—half-faces of smooth white ceramic that hid their features, leaving only their mouths exposed to eat, or their eyes to watch.

At the head of the table sat Vex.

He was larger than the others. His uniform was trimmed with gold braid. His mask was full-face, a smooth oval of porcelain with no features at all, except for two dark lenses where eyes should be.

He wasn't eating. He sat with his hands folded on the table.

Cassian pulled back. He looked at Elara.

"He's here," Cassian whispered. "Vex."

Elara nodded. She stood up. She wiped the grease from her cheek, leaving a streak of soot that looked like war paint. She straightened her spine, and suddenly, she wasn't a scavenger from the tunnels. She looked like she owned the marble.

"We walk," she said.

"They'll kill us," Cassian said. "They have guards."

"They are bureaucrats," Elara said. "They follow rules. If we act like we belong, they will hesitate. It is the nature of the thief to fear the law, but the law never fears the thief."

"I'm not a thief," Cassian said. "I'm an excavator."

"Then act like a King," she said. "You are the Second Son of the House of Bells. Act like it."

She pushed the door open.

Cassian had no choice. He followed.

The room was vast. The floor was a mirror. Every step Cassian took felt like an intrusion, the sound of his boots cracking the silence like bones.

The conversation at the table stopped.

Fifty masked faces turned toward them. The lenses focused with the mechanical whir of aperture shutters.

Vex didn't move. He watched.

Elara stopped in the center of the room. She stood on the reflection of the table.

"Guests," she said. Her voice rang out, clear and commanding, cutting through the string quartet's hesitation. "You dine on the ashes of your grandchildren."

A murmur went through the crowd. A rustle of stiff fabric.

Vex stood up.

The sound of his chair scraping the floor was sharp, a gunshot in the quiet.

"Who are you?" Vex asked. His voice came from a synthesizer in his throat, a smooth, genderless baritone. "And how did you bypass the sentries?"

"I am the memory you tried to bury," Elara said. "I am the stone in your throat."

Vex tilted his head. The porcelain mask caught the light, blank and terrifying.

"A ghost," Vex said. "A hallucination from the Chronite fumes. Guards."

Two Sanitizers at the side doors stepped forward. They raised sonic rifles.

Cassian’s hand went to his pocket. He gripped the tuning fork. He didn't have a seismic charge. He didn't have a weapon. He had a lump of metal and a bluff.

"One step," Cassian said. His voice cracked, then steadied. "One step and I trigger the resonance."

He held up the tuning fork. It looked like a weapon if you didn't know better.

"The structural integrity of this level is compromised," Cassian lied. He injected the certainty of a geological surveyor into his tone. "I've calibrated the frequency to the load-bearing pillars. If I drop this, the Spire falls into the Pit."

Vex looked at Cassian. The dark lenses zoomed.

"A terrorist," Vex said. "And a delusional accomplice."

"I am Elara of the Line of Ouro," Elara said. "And I command you to cease the excavation."

Vex laughed. A dry, electronic chuckle.

"The Queen," Vex said. "We dug you up three days ago. You were a fossil. A statue. You are nothing but animated mud. A byproduct of the refining process."

"I am the law," Elara said.

"The law is what we say it is," Vex said. He spread his hands. "We eat the past. We digest it. It is the cycle. You are just food."

He turned his back on them. He dismissed them.

"Take them," Vex said. "Scrub the chambers. Recycle the biomass."

The guards leveled their rifles.

Cassian’s thumb tightened on the tuning fork.

*Wait.*

The floor shook.

Not a tremor. An impact. A heavy, booming thud that vibrated up through the soles of their boots and into the marrow of their shins.

The wine glasses on the table rattled. The silverware chimed against the porcelain.

The guards stumbled. Their aim wavered.

Vex grabbed the edge of the table to steady himself.

"The drill," Cassian said. "They hit the Basalt Threshold."

Another crash. Louder.

Dust fell from the ceiling. It wasn't gray soot. It was white. A fine, choking powder that drifted down like snow.

It landed on the table. On the white uniforms. On the lenses of the masks.

"The White Dust," Cassian whispered.

He looked at Vex.

Vex was looking at the dust on his pristine white sleeve. He brushed it away, but it clung. It stained.

"It's not possible," Vex said. "The threshold is solid. It is sealed."

"It's waking up," Cassian said. "The Queen isn't the only thing buried down there."

Elara looked up. She watched the dust fall. She caught a flake on her finger.

She sniffed it.

Her eyes went wide. The black pupils dilated, swallowing the irises.

"The Mantle," she said. "You have drilled too deep. You have woken the Sleeper."

The floor heaved.

A crack appeared in the marble floor. It ran from the door to the center of the room, splitting the reflection in two. It was a jagged, black vein, oozing vapor.

A sound rose from the crack.

Not a mechanical grind. Not a gear.

A roar. Low. Deep. Subsonic. It vibrated the ribs of everyone in the room.

It sounded like a beast exhaling after a thousand years of sleep.

Vex backed away. The composure was gone. He tapped his fingers against his porcelain cheek. *Clack-clack-clack.*

"Seal the chamber!" he shouted. "Engage the blast shields! It's a breach!"

The guards scrambled for the doors. They forgot Cassian and Elara. They feared the floor.

"The city is haunted, Cassian," Elara said. She spoke over the roar, her voice calm. "Not by ghosts. By the future."

Cassian grabbed her arm. "Move!"

They ran for the side exit as the floor began to buckle. The table tipped, spilling wine and silver onto the white marble.

The Spire screamed.

They burst into the service corridor. Cassian slammed the heavy iron door behind them.

The roar was muted here, but the vibration was worse. The walls were sweating. Condensation slicked the white tiles.

"Where?" Cassian asked. He was panting. The air in the corridor was hot, getting hotter.

"The Control Deck," Elara said. "Vex will try to stabilize the drill. He will try to bury it again. We must be there when he fails."

She started running.

Cassian followed.

The corridor was long. It sloped upward, spiraling around the central core of the Spire. The lights flickered, strobing against the wet walls.

Cassian’s watch spun. The hands were a blur.

"You feel it?" Elara asked. She didn't look back.

"The Nausea," Cassian said. "It's bad."

"No," she said. "The time. It is slippery. The friction is gone."

She was right. Cassian felt light-headed. He looked at his hand. The skin was translucent, fading in and out of focus. He could see the veins, the bone, and for a second, he saw through the bone to the wall behind it.

He shook his head. The solidity returned.

"Don't think about it," he muttered. "Just run."

They reached the end of the corridor. A heavy security door blocked the way. *HIGH AUDITOR ACCESS ONLY.*

"Stand back," Elara said.

She raised her hand. She didn't touch the panel. She just pointed her palm at the lock.

The metal groaned.

The lock didn't melt. It rusted. Instantly. The steel turned orange, flaked away into red powder, crumbling like a dry leaf.

The door swung open.

Inside, the room was a hemisphere of glass. The Command Center.

Banks of monitors lined the curved wall. They displayed telemetry, seismographs, thermal imaging. Most were red. Blinking.

In the center of the room was a chair. Vex was sitting in it.

He had moved fast. Or perhaps he had been here all along, a projection, a consciousness distributed through the grid.

He was strapped in. Thick cables ran from the armrests into ports in his neck.

He turned his head. The porcelain mask was cracked. A spiderweb fracture ran down the left cheek.

"You," Vex said. His voice was amplified, booming from the speakers in the ceiling. "You are the variable."

"We're the correction," Cassian said.

He looked at the main screen. The schematic of the city.

The drill shaft. A red line piercing the layers of the city like a needle through skin.

At the tip of the needle, the Basalt Threshold.

It was glowing.

"The frequency is unstable," Elara said. She walked to the console. She ignored Vex. She looked at the numbers. "He is feeding the resonance forward. He is trying to burn the past to stop the future."

"It is efficient," Vex said. "The only way to kill the infection is to cauterize the wound."

"You're burning the foundation," Cassian said. "You'll collapse the Mid-Levels. You'll kill everyone in the Slums."

"The biomass is expendable," Vex said. "The Spire must remain airborne."

Elara’s fingers flew over the keys. She didn't type. She struck them like a piano, playing a chord.

*Clack. Clack. Clack.*

The monitors shifted. The red line on the drill schematic turned green.

"What are you doing?" Vex demanded. He tried to move, but the harness held him.

"I am reversing the polarity," Elara said. "The resonance flows down. It always flows down. You are trying to push it up. It is... illogical."

"Stop!" Vex shouted. "The feedback loop will destroy the Spire!"

"Good," Elara said.

She hit the final key. A large, red switch marked *PURGE.*

She pulled it.

The room filled with a low hum.

The glass walls vibrated.

On the screen, the red line of the drill didn't stop. It split. It branched out, sending tendrils of green energy into the surrounding rock.

The vibration in the room stopped.

The silence was worse than the noise.

Vex went limp. The cables disconnected from his neck with a hiss of pressurized air. The mask fell away, shattering on the floor.

Underneath, there was nothing. No face. Just a blackened cavity of wet tissue and wire.

"He was hollow," Elara said. She looked at the body. "He emptied himself to fill the city."

Cassian looked at the monitors. The red lights were fading. The drill was idle.

"Is it over?" Cassian asked.

"The drill is stopped," Elara said. "But the hole remains."

She walked to the glass wall. She looked out over the city.

Below them, the Spire tapered down to the Mid-Levels. The lights were going out, district by district.

"They are afraid," she said.

"The Spire. They steal the light. They pipe it down. They don't make anything. They just polish the rot."

Cassian joined her at the glass. He looked at his reflection. He looked old. His hair was white at the temples. His skin was sallow.

"The Nausea," he said. "It's not going away."

"No," Elara said. "Because the wound is open. The future is leaking in."

She pointed to the horizon of the cavern. Where the rock walls met the dark sky.

There was a crack there. A fissure in the bedrock. A line of blue light.

Not the blue of Chronite. The blue of the sky.

The surface.

"The Mantle isn't a monster," Cassian said. "It's the sun."

"It is the Scorch," Elara corrected. "And it is coming down."

She turned away from the glass. She looked at Cassian.

"We have a choice," she said. "We can stay here and polish the rot until the floor falls. Or we can climb out."

"Out?" Cassian asked. "To the surface?"

"To the Scorch," she said. "To the world that killed us. It is the only way to close the door. We have to face the sun."

Cassian looked at the blue light. It hurt his eyes.

He thought of Joren. He thought of the dark. He thought of the silence of the deep tunnels.

He thought of the endless digging.

"I'm tired of the dark," Cassian said.

He adjusted the collar of his coat. He checked his watch.

The hands were still.

"Then we climb," Elara said.

She walked toward the service door.

Cassian watched her go.

The city groaned. A long, settling sigh.

He followed her into the hall.

The lights flickered once, then died.

The darkness rushed in, but for the first time, Cassian didn't mind. He knew the way out was up.
