The hallway stretched out into the gloom, a throat of rough-hewn stone swallowing the light from Otho’s room. The air tasted of ozone and old wet soot.

Cassian moved forward. The leather coat creaked, a loud sound in the quiet. He kept his left hand against the wall. The stone was cold, damp, slick with the condensation of the city’s breath.

Elara followed. Her footsteps were silent. Bare feet on stone. She moved like a shadow cast by a light that no longer existed.

They reached the intersection of the corridors. A junction box on the wall sparked, throwing erratic blue shadows against the rock.

"Wait," Elara said.

Cassian stopped. He didn't turn. He watched the dark ahead. The intersection fed into the main arterial tunnel that led to the Lifts.

"The resonance," she said. "It is louder here."

"It's the mains," Cassian said. "The ventilation pumps."

"It is not pumps," she said. "It is a scream. Wrapped in iron."

Cassian closed his eyes for a second. The needle behind his left eye pricked sharp. He rubbed the spot with his thumb. "It's just the city settling. The Spire is heavy. The weight compresses the gas in the lines."

He started moving again. Faster.

Elara kept pace. She didn't look at the flickering lights. She looked at the darkness between the lights.

They passed a row of tenement doors, all sealed. Some had red crosses painted on them—Sanitizer quarantine marks. The wood was rotting, bloated with the humidity of the deep levels. Through the gaps in the wood, the smell of unwashed bodies and boiled fungus drifted out.

Cassian kept his head down. He watched the ground. Debris. Broken glass. A rat skeleton, picked clean.

"You walk like a thief," Elara said.

"I walk like someone who doesn't want to be audited," Cassian said.

"You are afraid of the law."

"I'm afraid of the white masks," Cassian said. "They don't ask questions. They just scrub."

"Scrub?"

"Erasure," Cassian said. "They take you. They burn your clothes. They boil your bones for ink. No one remembers you existed."

Elara walked beside him now. Her rags fluttered. "I remember," she said. "I remember the men who buried me. I remember their faces. I remember the sound of the concrete mixing. I remember the heat of the lime."

Cassian glanced at her. Her face was blank. Not empty—blank. A slate waiting to be written on, or a slate that had been wiped clean too many times.

"They're likely dead," Cassian said. "That was three thousand years ago."

"Everything is dead," she said. "That is why we must go up."

They reached the end of the corridor. The heavy blast doors to the Grand Lift station were shut. A small, reinforced window was set into the steel at eye level.

Cassian stepped up to it. He peered through the thick, wire-meshed glass.

The station beyond was a cavern of rusted iron and dripping pipes. A massive gantry crane hung dormant from the ceiling. And there, in the center of the room, stood the Lift.

It wasn't moving.

"Damn it," Cassian whispered.

He pulled back. He looked at the control panel on the wall. The gauge needle was flatlined. Zero pressure.

"The power is cut," he said. "They've grounded the Lifts."

"Can we fix it?" Elara asked.

Cassian looked at the panel. The Sanitizer seal was stamped into the metal—a white circle, unbroken. "Not without a bypass key. And not without about an hour of work we don't have."

He looked at his watch. 12:02.

"Vex knows," Cassian said. "He knows the drill is failing. He's locked down the transit lines to stop anyone from interfering."

He slammed his palm against the wall. The metal rang, a dull, hollow sound.

"We climb," Elara said.

Cassian looked at her. "What?"

"The shaft," she said. She pointed to the ceiling. "The counter-weight tunnel. It goes up."

"That's a maintenance chute," Cassian said. "It's a straight vertical drop. Three miles of smooth steel. No ladder rungs. Just the grease track."

"We climb," she repeated.

Cassian looked up. A small circular hatch was set into the ceiling of the station, obscured by steam pipes. It was painted with yellow warning stripes. *DANGER: FALLING HAZARD.*

"We don't have gear," Cassian said. "No ropes. No pitons. Just our hands."

"You have the coat," Elara said. "It has grip."

Cassian looked at the leather sleeves. Thick, heavy oil-tan.

"And I have me," Elara said. She held up her hands. The fingers were long, tipped with black, cracked nails that looked more like obsidian than keratin. "I was a mason before I was a Queen. I know the stone. I know the hold."

Cassian looked at the hatch, then back at the dark tunnel they had come from. The distant grinding of the drills vibrated through the soles of his boots.

The needle spiked.

"Alright," Cassian said. He grabbed a rusted valve wheel sticking out of the wall and hauled himself up. He reached for the hatch lever.

It was stiff. Rusted shut. He put his shoulder into it.

Metal groaned. A shower of rust flakes fell into his hair.

With a screech that echoed down the hall, the hatch popped open. Cold air hissed out. The smell of hydraulic fluid and ancient dust.

Cassian pulled himself up.

He swung his legs into the dark throat of the shaft.

Elara followed. She moved with a fluid grace, hauling herself up beside him without using the rungs. She just gripped the edge and pulled.

Below them, the station floor waited.

Cassian reached up and grabbed the guide cable. It was thick, greasy, vibrating with the distant hum of the machinery above.

"Don't look down," he said.

"I do not fear the fall," Elara said. "I fear the landing."

She reached past him. Her hand clamped onto the cable above his. She hauled herself up, moving fast.

"Wait," Cassian said.

She didn't wait. She climbed.

Cassian swore under his breath. He gripped the cable. The grease coated his palms. He pulled.

Up.

The station lights receded. The darkness closed in.

The shaft was a cylinder of black steel. The only light came from the faint, dying phosphorescence of fungus growing in the grease joints.

Cassian’s arms burned. The weight of the coat pulled at his shoulders. He hooked his elbow around the cable, letting his breath hitch.

"How far?" he whispered. The sound vanished instantly in the vast vertical space.

"Far," Elara’s voice drifted down. "I can hear the wind."

Cassian looked up. He couldn't see her. Just the faint outline of her silhouette against the lighter gray of the hatch far above.

He climbed.

*Left hand. Right hand. Hook. Breathe.*

The rhythm took over. The Nausea receded, replaced by the physical pain of exertion. His muscles trembled. The leather of the coat creaked in time with his heartbeat.

*Thump. Creak. Thump. Creak.*

He focused on the grease. The black sludge coating the cable. It smelled of sulfur and rotten meat.

He thought of Joren.

Joren hanging from the scaffolding. Joren laughing, his boots kicking against the metal strut. *Look at me, Cass! I’m a bird!*

Joren falling. The sound of the snap. The silence after.

Cassian gripped the cable tighter. The metal bit into his gloves.

He climbed faster.

Time lost meaning. It was just the motion. Up. Pull. Up. Pull.

His arms were lead. His lungs burned with the cold, recycled air.

"Stop."

Elara’s voice came from above, sharp.

Cassian froze. He hooked his arm, locking his elbow. He looked up.

Elara was clinging to the side of the shaft, pressed flat against the wall.

"What?" Cassian mouthed the word. No sound.

"Listen," she whispered.

Cassian pressed his ear against the cold steel of the cable.

Through the vibration of the shaft, a new sound emerged.

*Clack. Clack. Clack.*

Metal on metal. Rhythmic. Sharp.

It wasn't a drill. It was a wheel. A cart.

"The inspection rail," Cassian whispered. "They run the drones along the mid-level maintenance track."

"Men?" Elara asked.

"Machines," Cassian said. "But they have sensors. Thermal."

"Can they see us?"

"If we're moving, yes," Cassian said. "If we hold still, we’re just background temperature."

The sound grew louder. A high-pitched whine of electric motors. The cable began to sing, a low resonant hum that vibrated in Cassian’s teeth.

"Hold," Elara said.

Cassian pressed himself flat against the wall. He found a rusted rivet to dig his boots into. He hugged the darkness.

A beam of light slashed through the shaft below him.

It swept back and forth, cutting through the gloom. It missed his boots by inches.

The drone cart zipped past on a parallel track. It was a sleek, white teardrop, silent except for the wheels. It moved with terrifying speed.

The wind of its passage whipped Cassian’s hair against his face.

It passed.

The light faded.

The whine dropped in pitch.

Cassian let out a breath he didn't know he was holding. His heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird.

"That was close," he whispered.

"They are hunting," Elara said. She didn't sound scared. She sounded hungry. "They are looking for the breach."

"They're looking for us," Cassian said.

"Then we must not be found," she said. "We must be faster than the light."

She resumed climbing.

Cassian watched her go. She moved like a spider, finding holds in the seamless steel that shouldn't exist.

He pushed off the wall. He grabbed the cable.

He climbed.

His shoulders screamed. His hands were raw inside the gloves. He could feel the blisters popping, the wet heat of blood mixing with the grease.

*One hour. Two hours.*

The pressure changed. His ears popped. The air grew thinner. Colder.

The smell changed. The sulfurous rot of the Slums faded, replaced by the sharp, chemical scent of ozone and synthetic lavender.

The Spire.

Cassian looked up. The darkness was thinning. A faint, diffuse glow filtered down from above.

"We are close," Elara called down.

Cassian looked at his watch. The crystal was cracked, the hands spinning lazily before catching.

*12:45.*

He froze.

The cable above him vibrated. violently.

Elara stopped.

"Cassian," she said.

He looked up.

Above her, the shaft opened into a lit chamber. But hanging in the opening, suspended by a thick chain, was a figure.

It was wrapped in white plastic. A cacoon. It hung like a pupa, blocking the exit.

"What is that?" Cassian whispered.

"Sanitizer," Elara said. "A sentry."

The figure twitched.

The plastic split.

A white mask dropped down. Porcelain. Expressionless. Behind the mask, a single red ocular sensor flared to life.

The thing hissed. It wasn't human. It was a prosthetic body, a puppet of ceramic and pneumatic hoses, suspended on winch lines. It dangled on a tether, blocking the hatch like a spider in a web.

"It sees us," Elara said.

She let go of the wall.

She dropped.

Cassian flinched, expecting her to scream, to fall past him into the abyss.

She didn't.

She caught the cable with her legs. She slid down the greased steel, friction smoking from her rags, and swung in a wide arc towards the puppet.

She had a rock in her hand. A chunk of granite she’d pried from the wall somewhere in the dark.

She swung at the end of her arc.

*Crack.*

The rock smashed into the red ocular sensor.

The puppet screeched—a sound of tearing metal. It thrashed, its tether tangling.

Elara kicked off the puppet’s chest. She launched herself back toward the wall, finding a grip ten feet above Cassian.

"Go!" she shouted.

Cassian didn't argue. He hauled himself up the last twenty feet.

The puppet was blind now, flailing wildly, its ceramic limbs clattering against the steel walls. It fired a bolt of sonic energy randomly.

The beam hit the cable next to Cassian’s head.

The steel cable sang, a deafening shriek. The metal superheated, glowing red for a split second.

Cassian scrambled over the lip of the hatch. He rolled onto the floor of the maintenance room.

It was white. Blindingly white. Tiles of polished ceramic. The air smelled of antiseptic and cold air.

He turned and reached down the hole.

Elara’s hand slapped into his.

He pulled.

She vaulted out of the shaft, landing in a crouch. She was covered in grease and dust. She looked like a demon in a temple.

Cassian kicked the hatch wheel. It spun. The heavy steel door slammed shut.

The muffled thuds of the puppet hitting the other side vibrated through the floor.

Cassian slumped against the wall. He gasped for air. The thin, sweet air of the Spire burned his lungs.

"We are in," he wheezed.

Elara stood up. She walked to the window set into the door.

She looked out.

"The glass trees," she whispered. "They are still here."

Cassian pushed himself up. He joined her at the window.

They were in a high-ceilinged corridor that looked out over the Atrium. It was a park, but wrong.

The trees were sculptures of twisted copper and blown glass. The leaves were shards of colored silicon, chiming in the stillness. The grass was a carpet of green velvet, perfectly trimmed.

And the light.

It came from the Obsidian Eye in the ceiling far above. A massive beam of focused sunlight, amplified and magnified, flooding the central chamber with a brilliance that hurt Cassian’s eyes after years of gloom.

"It is beautiful," Elara said. "And it is a lie."

"It’s the Spire," Cassian said. "They steal the light. They pipe it down. They don't make anything. They just polish the rot."

He checked the sonic detonator in his pocket. It was heavy. A dead weight.

"We need the Control Room," Cassian said. "Vex will be there. The drill telemetry is routed through the main hub."

He pointed down the corridor. "The turbolifts are that way. But they’ll be guarded."

"We walk," Elara said. "We do not sneak. We are the Queen. And you are the Keeper."

She straightened her rags. She wiped the grease from her face, leaving a streak of soot that looked like war paint.

She looked dangerous.

Cassian nodded. He adjusted the collar of the leather coat. He felt the weight of the years in his bones. He felt the Nausea receding, replaced by a cold clarity.

"Lead," he said.

Elara opened the door.

They stepped into the light.

The corridor was silent. The floor was marble. White, veined with gray. The walls were lined with portraits.

Cassian looked at them as they passed.

Men in white uniforms. Women in stiff, high-collared dresses. All holding the same expression. Bored. Superior.

"Vain," Elara spat. "All of them. They ate the food of the poor while the city starved."

She stopped in front of a portrait of a man with a beard like a wire brush.

"High Auditor Karc," she read the plaque. "He ordered the draining of the Mid-Levels reservoirs. To build the fountains."

She reached up and ripped the painting from the wall. The canvas tore.

Behind it was a safe. A biometric scanner.

Elara placed her hand on the scanner.

Nothing happened.

"Access denied," a mechanical voice said.

Cassian stepped forward. He looked at the scanner. It was an old model. Year 2500 tech.

He pulled a multi-tool from his belt. He pried the faceplate off.

"Give me a second," he muttered. He crossed two wires. He bypassed the capacitor.

The light turned green.

*Click.*

The safe door popped open.

Inside was a bottle. Crystal. Filled with amber liquid.

"Memory-Wine," Elara said. "They keep the vintage stock in the walls."

She took the bottle. She smashed it against the floor.

Glass shattered. The liquid pooled, smoking slightly.

"Come," she said.

They moved deeper into the Spire.

They encountered no one. It was strange. The Spire should have been bustling with functionaries, with Sanitizer officers, with the machinery of the state.

But the halls were empty.

"The drill," Cassian said. "The vibration. It’s shaking the whole structure. They’ve evacuated the upper echelons. They’re in the bunkers."

"Or they are at the feast," Elara said.

They reached the massive double doors of the Grand Gallery.

The doors were open.

Sound drifted out. Not the sound of machinery. The sound of music.

A string quartet. Something slow, melancholic. A waltz.

Cassian peeked around the edge of the door.

The gallery was a vast room with a floor so polished it reflected the ceiling. In the center of the room, under the beam of the Obsidian Eye, a long table was set.

White cloth. Silver service. Candelabras.

And people.

Dozens of them. Sanitizers. High Auditors. Censors.

They sat, eating. They wore their porcelain masks, but they had pushed them up onto their foreheads to eat. They revealed faces that were scarred, rotting, or chemically burned.

They ate gray meat. They drank wine that glowed faintly blue.

And at the head of the table, sitting in a high-backed chair upholstered in red velvet, was Vex.

He wasn't eating. He was watching.

His porcelain face was perfect. Smooth. White. No mouth, just a breathing grille. No eyes, just the dark sockets of the lenses.

He tapped his finger on the table.

*Clack. Clack. Clack.*

"The meat is tough today," one of the guests said. A woman with half a face.

"The livestock is stressed," Vex said. His voice was synthesized, smooth, devoid of grit. "The vibrations from the excavation distress the muscle fibers. It adds... flavor."

"The flavor of fear," the woman laughed.

Cassian pulled back. He leaned against the wall, his heart hammering.

"They’re dining," he whispered. "While the city collapses."

"They are celebrating the end," Elara said. "I recognize that tune. It is the *Requiem for the Sun*. They play it when they intend to burn something."

"We have to go around," Cassian said. "There’s a service corridor behind the gallery. It leads to the Control Deck."

"We cannot sneak past them," Elara said. "The floor is too open. They will see us."

"Then we create a distraction," Cassian said. He patted his pocket. The detonator.

"No," Elara said. She touched his arm. Her fingers were cold. "Not yet. If we break the machine now, the drill falls. The city collapses. We need the kill switch. We need to stop the drill *before* we break it."

"Then how?"

"We walk," Elara said.

"They'll kill us," Cassian said.

"No," Elara said. "They are civilized. They follow rules. If we act like we belong, they will hesitate."

She looked at Cassian. She straightened his coat. She wiped a smudge of dirt from his cheek.

"You are the Second Son of the House of Bells," she said. "You are of the Old Blood. Act like it."

Cassian stared at her. "I'm a scavenger from the Slums."

"Tonight," she said, "you are a King."

She turned.

She walked into the room.

Cassian froze. The needle behind his eye throbbed.

He took a breath.

He followed her.

They walked into the center of the room.

The music didn't stop. The violinist saw them, sawed a wrong note, screeched to a halt.

The conversation died.

It went silent like the air had been sucked out of the room.

Fifty faces turned. Half human, half porcelain. Eyes wide.

Vex stopped tapping his finger.

He turned his head. The lenses whirred as they focused.

Elara stopped in the center of the room. She stood on the reflection of the table.

"Guests," she said. Her voice rang out, clear and commanding. "You dine on the ashes of your grandchildren."

Vex stood up.

The sound of his chair scraping was like a gunshot.

"Who are you?" Vex asked. His voice was calm. "And how did you get past the sentries?"

"I am the memory you tried to bury," Elara said. "I am the stone in your throat."

Vex tilted his head. "A ghost," he said. "A hallucination from the Chronite fumes. Guards."

The guards at the door raised their sonic rifles.

Cassian stepped forward. He put his hand on the detonator in his pocket.

"One step," Cassian said. His voice cracked, then steadied. "One step and I trigger the seismic charge I planted in the load-bearing wall of this gallery."

It was a bluff. He had no charge planted. But the detonator in his hand looked real enough.

"The structural integrity of this level is compromised," Cassian lied. "If I drop this, the Spire falls into the Pit."

Vex looked at Cassian. He looked at the hand in the pocket.

"A terrorist," Vex said. "And a delusional accomplice."

"I am Elara of the Line of Ouro," Elara said. "And I command you to cease the excavation."

Vex laughed. A dry, electronic chuckle.

"The Queen," Vex said. "We dug you up three days ago. You were a fossil. A statue. You are nothing but animated mud."

"I am the law," Elara said.

"The law is what we say it is," Vex said. "We eat the past. We digest it. You are just food."

Vex raised his hand. He snapped his fingers.

"Take them."

The guards leveled their weapons.

Cassian’s hand tightened on the detonator.

*Wait.*

The floor shook.

Not a tremor. An impact.

A massive, booming crash from below.

The wine glasses on the table rattled. The crystal chandelier swayed.

The guards stumbled.

Vex grabbed the table to steady himself.

"The drill," Cassian said. "They hit the Basalt Threshold."

Another crash. Louder.

Dust fell from the ceiling. A fine, white powder.

It drifted down like snow.

It landed on the table. On the food. On the masks.

"The White Dust," Cassian whispered.

He looked at Vex.

Vex was looking at the dust on his pristine white sleeve.

"It's not possible," Vex said. "The threshold is solid."

"It's waking up," Cassian said. "The Queen isn't the only thing buried down there."

Elara looked up. She watched the dust fall. She caught a flake on her finger.

She sniffed it.

Her eyes went wide.

"The Mantle," she said. "You have drilled too deep. You have woken the Sleeper."

The floor heaved.

A crack appeared in the marble floor. It ran from the door to the center of the room, splitting the reflection in two.

A sound rose from the crack. Not a drill. A roar.

Like a beast exhaling after a thousand years of sleep.

Vex backed away. "Seal the chamber!" he shouted. "Seal the Gallery!"

The guards scrambled for the doors.

Cassian grabbed Elara’s arm. "Move!"

They ran for the side exit as the floor began to buckle beneath the table.

The Spire screamed.
