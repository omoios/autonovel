The light of the Spire was not warm. It was a clinical, piercing white that lacked a source, bleaching the color out of Cassian’s coat and turning the black ichor dripping from his nose into a slick, dark mirror.

Vex stood five paces away. The porcelain mask was smooth, featureless except for the two glass lenses and the vertical grill where a mouth should be. He held the white cane loosely in one gloved hand.

"Audit," Vex said. The word vibrated through the soles of Cassian’s boots. The floor was polished marble—real stone, not synthetic. It felt alien after the grit of the Mid-Levels. "You are dragging biological contaminants into a sterile environment, Chronicler. And a fossil. That is a violation of health code four."

Elara stepped forward. The air pressure in the room dropped. Cassian’s ears popped. The Queen’s skin was still cracking, flakes of golden shale falling from her shoulders like dandruff. She left dusty footprints on the white marble.

"I am not a fossil," Elara said. Her voice didn't just carry sound; it carried weight. The light fixtures in the ceiling hummed in sympathy. "I am the landlord. And you are squatting in my parlor."

Vex tilted his head. *Clack.* The porcelain mask tapped against his collar.

"Landlord," Vex repeated. The synthesizer in his throat flattened the tone, removing any inflection of curiosity. "The title deed on this structure expired three thousand years ago. This city is a corporation now. It belongs to the Board."

He gestured with the cane. The row of Sanitizers behind him—one, two, six of them—raised their sonic cannons. The barrels were shaped like tuning forks, hollow and dark.

"Seize them," Vex said. "Recycle the biomass. Preserve the sample."

The Sanitizers moved. They didn't march; they glided. Their boots were magnetic. They moved in perfect unison, a phalanx of white ceramic and black rubber.

Cassian grabbed Kait by the collar of her rags and shoved her backward toward the lift doors. "Get back!"

"I can fight!" Kait yelled, pulling a rusty wrench from her belt.

"Fight the wall, then," Cassian snarled. He turned to Otho. The old woman was wheezing, leaning against the lift frame. "Get her down. Hide in the shaft."

"And leave you?" Otho coughed, a wet, rattling sound. She fumbled in her shawl for a detonator. "I have gas charges. I can blow the couplings."

"No gas in here," Cassian said. "The scrubbers will eat it before it works. Go."

He didn't wait for them to move. He charged.

It was a stupid maneuver. A miner’s tactic. He didn't aim for Vex; he aimed for the Sanitizer on the left flank. The one with the chipped shoulder plate.

The needle behind his eye flared. A hot spike of white agony.

Cassian dropped to his knees, sliding on the marble. The Sanitizer’s cannon swiveled. Cassian punched the mechanism. Not the gun, the cooling vents.

*Crunch.*

His hand, already translucent and flickering, shattered the ceramic vent. The Sanitizer shuddered. Its aim went wide. The sonic blast hit a marble pillar instead of Cassian’s chest.

The stone didn't break. It liquefied. The vibration turned the rock into jelly. It slumped to the floor with a wet *thwack*.

"Physics," Cassian gasped, scrambling to his feet. His left hand was useless now, vibrating so hard it blurred. "Apply some."

Another Sanitizer fired. Cassian threw himself behind a console. The soundwave hit the console, and the metal casing split along the grain.

Vex hadn't moved. He just watched, tapping his cane on the floor. *Clack. Clack. Clack.*

"Inefficient," Vex said. "Your resistance is geologically unsound. You are trying to stop a glacier with a breath."

Elara moved. She didn't run; she strode. She walked directly into the line of fire.

"Mother, no!" Otho screamed.

A Sanitizer fired point-blank at Elara’s chest.

The soundwave hit her and stopped. It rippled outward like water hitting a rock in a stream. The air distorted around her, bending light, turning her outline into a jagged blur.

She didn't bleed. She didn't bruise. She just kept walking.

"Your toys are noisy," Elara said. She reached out. Her hand, made of granite and gold, clamped onto the Sanitizer’s helmet. *Crunch.*

She squeezed. The helmet imploded. The machine inside slumped.

She tossed the broken wreckage aside. It skittered across the marble and hit Vex’s boots.

Vex looked down at the debris. He looked up at Elara.

"Fascinating," Vex said. "High-density temporal shielding. You aren't just a fossil. You are a node."

Cassian used the distraction. He scrambled toward Otho and Kait, who were cowering near the lift mechanism. The lift doors were trying to close, but the Sanitizers had jammed the track with a magnetic lock.

"We need to unjam the gear," Cassian hissed. He grabbed a handful of the black ichor from his own nose and smeared it on the locking mechanism. It was raw Chronite. Concentrated time.

"What are you doing?" Kait whispered, her eyes wide.

"Rusting it," Cassian said. "Accelerating the decay."

He focused. He pushed the nausea down, channeled it into his hand. The metal of the lock groaned. It turned orange, then gray, then flaked away into rust.

The gears disengaged. The lift dropped, leaving them stranded on the Spire level.

"Good," Cassian said, panting. "Now we just have to get to the Obsidian Eye."

"The what?" Kait asked.

"The lens," Otho said, staring at the massive glass structure dominating the center of the hall. "The light."

Vex raised a hand. The remaining Sanitizers ceased fire. They stepped back, forming a perimeter.

"You wish to reach the Eye," Vex said. He sounded almost amused. "Why? To look at the sun? To burn out your retinas with the truth? It is dead up there. Nothing but ash and glass."

"Not dead," Elara said. She stood in the center of the room, the white light reflecting off her golden skin. "Waiting."

She turned to Cassian. Her eyes were solid black. No whites. Just pits.

"Chronicler," she said. "The conduit."

Cassian froze. "Your Majesty?"

"You feel it," she said. "The vibration. The city is screaming. The drills have breached the bedrock."

Cassian did feel it. A low-frequency hum that rattled his teeth. It wasn't just the Nausea. It was coming from the floor.

"The Bore," Cassian said. "They hit the Mantle."

"We hit the silence," Vex corrected. "And now we will mine it."

Vex tapped his cane. The floor beneath them— polished marble, the symbol of the Spire’s perfection—split.

A crack appeared. It ran from Vex’s feet to Cassian’s.

"Structural failure," Vex said calmly. "It seems the city is settling."

The floor groaned. A massive grinding sound, like tectonic plates shifting. The Spire wasn't just a building; it was a capstone on a pressurized bottle. And the bottle was breaking.

"We have to move," Otho shouted. "The whole level is going to drop!"

"Run," Cassian said.

They ran. Not toward Vex, but toward the archway that led to the Observation Deck. The Obsidian Eye.

Vex didn't chase them. He pointed his cane at the ceiling.

"Cut the power," he said.

The lights died.

The Spire plunged into darkness. The only light came from the blue veins of Chronite in the walls, pulsing like arteries. And from Elara. She glowed with a faint, golden luminescence, like a radium dial.

"Kait," Cassian whispered into the dark. "Stay close."

"I'm here," the girl said. She grabbed his coat. Her hand was shaking. "I can't see."

"Listen," Cassian said.

He listened. The sound of the Sanitizers was a soft, magnetic hum. The sound of the city dying was a roar.

They stumbled through the archway. The Observation Deck was a vast balcony overlooking the cylinder of the city. Below them, miles of vertical strata, lights flickering out as the power grids failed.

In the center of the deck stood the Obsidian Eye. A massive lens, twenty feet tall, made of polished black glass. It focused the light from the surface down the shaft.

Elara walked up to it. She placed her hand on the glass.

"It is cold," she said. "The fire is gone."

"Turn it on," Cassian said. He looked at the controls. A bronze lever, corroded with age. "Vex said the sun is dead. But he lies."

"He lies," Elara agreed. "But the sun is... faint."

Cassian gripped the lever. His hand was shaking. The Nausea was peaking. He could see his own skeleton through his skin. His radius and ulna were translucent ghosts.

"If I do this," Cassian said, "the Censors will know exactly where we are."

"They already know," Otho said. She was leaning against the railing, her face gray. "Do it, Cassian. Let them see."

Cassian pulled the lever.

It didn't move. It was rusted solid.

"Help me," Cassian grunted.

Kait jumped up. She braced her feet against the housing. Otho pushed. Even Elara put her shoulder to the glass.

*Groan.*

The metal shrieked. A spray of rust coated Cassian’s face.

*Snap.*

The lever gave way. The gears engaged. A clanking, grinding roar echoed through the deck.

Above them, the shutter in the ceiling began to open.

Vex stepped onto the balcony. His Sanitizers flanked him. The white light of his mask reflected in the black glass of the Eye.

"You are wasting time," Vex said. "And energy."

The shutter opened fully.

Light hit them.

It wasn't the warm yellow light of the stories. It wasn't the blue light of the Chronite.

It was a harsh, violet radiation. The light of a dying star.

Cassian covered his eyes. His skin prickled. He felt the radiation burning his dead cells.

Elara didn't cover her eyes. She looked up.

"The Scorch," she whispered. "It is still burning."

She turned to face Vex. The violet light cast long, monstrous shadows behind her.

"You sit in the dark and pretend the sky is dead," Elara shouted. Her voice boomed, amplified by the acoustics of the Spire. "You bury your heads in the rock to avoid the gaze of the sun!"

Vex took a step back. The white ceramic of his uniform began to discolor in the violet light. It yellowed, like old paper.

"The radiation levels are lethal," Vex said, his voice picking up speed. "Close the shutter. You are contaminating the gene pool."

"This is the truth!" Elara roared. She pointed a finger at Vex. "The world didn't end! We just hid!"

She slammed her hand against the glass of the Obsidian Eye.

The lens resonated. A high-pitched whine that built into a scream. The Chronite in the walls reacted. The blue veins flared bright white.

The beam of light that usually shot *down* the shaft—shot *up*.

It reflected off the Obsidian Eye. It blasted a hole through the ceiling of the cavern. A column of violet fire piercing the rock, climbing toward the surface.

"Stop it!" Vex yelled. He lost his composure. He lunged for Elara, his cane raised like a club.

Cassian stepped in the way. He didn't have a weapon. He had his hands.

He caught the cane.

The shockwave traveled up his arms. His radius snapped. But he held on.

"The audit," Cassian gritted out. "Is concluded."

He twisted the cane. Vex stumbled. The porcelain mask cracked.

A sound came from behind the mask. Not a synthesizer. A wet, wheezing gasp.

Vex ripped the cane free. He backed away, clutching his face.

"Kill them," Vex rasped. His real voice was wet, ruined. "Burn the deck."

The Sanitizers raised their cannons.

But the city was shaking harder now. The beam of light had destabilized the core. The floor tilted.

"The drills!" Otho screamed. "The drills are reversing!"

A deep rumble from below. The Chronite veins were overheating. The Spire was listing.

"The weight," Cassian said. He looked at Kait. "We have to jump."

"Jump where?" Kait yelled. She was holding onto the railing as the floor tilted to a forty-degree angle.

"The Mid-Levels," Cassian said. He pointed over the edge of the balcony. "The Market level. We can make it."

"It's a hundred feet down!" Otho said.

"It's better than burning," Cassian said.

He grabbed Kait. He looked at Elara.

"Jump!" Cassian yelled.

Elara looked at the violet sky. She looked at Vex, who was clutching his broken mask.

"I will not hide again," Elara said.

She didn't jump. She stood her ground as the Sanitizers fired.

The sonic blasts hit her, but she didn't fall. She raised her arms. The floor beneath the Sanitizers—reinforced concrete—shattered. They fell into the abyss of the shaft.

Elara grabbed Vex by the throat. She lifted him. He was heavy, armored, but to her, he was a feather.

"Look at it," Elara commanded. She forced him to look up at the violet beam. "Look at what you abandoned."

Vex clawed at her hand. The porcelain mask fell away.

Cassian saw the face beneath.

It wasn't human. It was a tumor of flesh, a raw, red mess of exposed muscle and nerve endings, stretched over a skull that was too large. No nose. No lips. Just teeth and gums.

"The light..." Vex gurgled. "It burns..."

"Good," Elara said.

She threw him.

Vex flew over the railing. He plummeted into the dark, screaming as he fell toward the Pit.

Elara turned to Cassian. The floor was collapsing. The Spire groaned, a great metal beast dying.

"Come with us!" Cassian yelled over the roar of the city.

Elara smiled. It was a terrifying expression. A queen smiling at her executioner.

"My place is here," she said. "I am the foundation. If I leave, the house falls."

She wasn't just talking metaphorically. As she stood there, her feet began to fuse with the floor. The granite of her skin merged with the marble.

"The Spire will hold," Elara said. "If I become the pillar."

"You'll die," Otho wept. "You'll be a statue again."

"I will be a roof," Elara said. "Go."

The floor gave way.

Cassian grabbed Kait and Otho. He threw them over the railing.

They screamed.

Cassian jumped after them.

The wind roared. The violet light flashed. He saw the layers of the city blurring past. The Market level. The Slums.

He hit the canvas awning of a spice stall in the Mid-Market.

*Snap.*

The canvas tore. They fell through, crashing into crates of dust-wheat and fermented fungus.

Cassian rolled. He hit the cobblestones. Pain lanced through his shoulder, his hip. His broken arm throbbed.

He lay there, staring up at the ceiling of the cavern.

High above, the Spire was glowing. A single, bright star of violet light. And then, the light stabilized. It stopped flickering. It held.

The shaking stopped.

The city didn't fall.

Cassian lay on the dirty floor of the Market, smelling cinnamon and mold. He looked at his hand. The translucence was fading. The Nausea was gone.

He was solid. He was heavy.

Kait crawled out of a crate of fungus. She was covered in gray dust. She looked at Cassian. Her lip trembled.

"We..." Kait started. "We fell."

"We landed," Cassian said. He sat up. Every bone in his body ached. He felt like he'd been dug up with a shovel.

Otho was lying nearby, clutching her chest. She coughed, but she sat up. She looked up at the Spire.

"She stayed," Otho whispered. "She stayed behind."

"She saved us," Cassian said.

He looked at his pocket watch. It was smashed. The hands were stopped at 12:00.

"It's over," Kait said.

Cassian looked at the people around them. The merchants. The shoppers. They were standing still, staring up at the Spire. The violet light from above was casting strange shadows on their faces.

"No," Cassian said. He stood up. He wiped the black ichor from his nose. It was just blood now. Red blood.

"It's not over," Cassian said. "We just opened the door."

He looked at the Spire one last time. The light was fading, turning back to the artificial white of the city. But for a moment, he had seen the sun.

"We need to move," Cassian said. "Before the Censors figure out who broke their floor."

He grabbed Kait’s hand. He helped Otho up.

"Where to?" Otho asked.

"The Archives," Cassian said. "If the Queen is gone, we need to find out what she left behind."

They walked through the market. The crowd parted for them. They looked like ghosts. Covered in white dust and red blood.

But they were alive.

And the city was ringing. A single, clear note that vibrated through the soles of their shoes, humming a song that hadn't been heard in three thousand years.
