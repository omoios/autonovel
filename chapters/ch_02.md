The ladder rungs were slick with condensation. Cold water dripped down Cassian’s collar, tracing the line of his spine.

He climbed. Above him, Kait’s boots kicked loose scree that rattled against his helmet.

*Clack. Clack.*

The sound was sharp against the low, grinding thrum of the city below. The Bore. Vex’s machine was chewing through the bedrock, and the vibrations were shaking the dust loose from the world.

"Hurry," Kait hissed from the dark. "The light is changing."

Cassian risked a look down. The fissure they had fled was twenty feet below, a jagged tear in the grey rock. The pale, watery luminescence was still spilling out of it, turning the black shale into the color of bruised skin.

The heartbeat sound was gone. In its place was the shriek of twisting metal.

The rock face groaned. A fissure opened in the wall beside Cassian’s head, spraying him with grit. He pressed his forehead against the cold iron of the ladder. The vibration rattled his teeth.

*Move.*

He hauled himself up, hand over hand. His gloves were fraying; the iron bit into his palms. The needle behind his left eye flared—a hot, wire-thin spike of pressure. He blinked, tasting copper. The Nausea. The deeper they went into the compressed time of the Pit, the more the present moment wanted to slip out of his grip.

He checked the Chronometer on his wrist. The needle was jittering against the red zone.

"Kait," he said. "Don't look at the light."

"I'm not," she said. She was lying. He could hear the breath hitch in her throat. She was fascinated by the woman in the rock.

They reached the landing—a rusted iron grate suspended over the shaft. Cassian swung his leg over, rolling onto the grating. Kait dropped beside him, landing like a cat. She was trembling, her eyes wide and fixed on the vertical tunnel they had just escaped.

"She blinked," Kait whispered. She pulled her knees to her chest, wrapping her oversized coat tight. "I saw her. She had obsidian eyes. She looked at you."

"It was a trick of the refraction," Cassian said. He sat up and checked his gear. Pickaxe. Chisel. Bag of samples. "Gas pockets in the Chronite vein cause hallucinations. You know the rules."

"I know what I saw." Kait glared at him, then looked back down the shaft. "Do you think she’s pretty?"

Cassian paused. He adjusted the strap on his lamp. "She’s a fossil, Kait. She’s been dead for three thousand years. Pretty doesn't enter the equation."

"You’re scared of her."

"I’m scared of the ceiling collapsing." Cassian stood up. The landing groaned under his weight. "We’re in Sector 4. The Rust Belt. If Vex’s drill breaks the Basalt Threshold, this entire sector drops into the magma."

He grabbed her shoulder, steering her toward the service corridor. The air here was hotter. It smelled of sulfur and burnt insulation.

"Move."

They jogged down the corridor, their boots ringing on the metal plating. The city’s architecture was a claustrophobic’s nightmare: pipes the size of pythons coiled around the walls, leaking steam that hissed like angry snakes. Every fifty feet, a speaker box crackled with the Sanitizer’s daily broadcast.

*"...efficiency is the virtue of the present. Report all irregularities. Audit is mandatory..."*

Cassian ignored it. He focused on the rhythm of his own breathing. *In. Out. Check the watch.*

*The needle jumped.*

He stopped.

"What?" Kait skidded to a halt.

"Listen."

The drone of the Bore was louder here. But underneath it, something else. A low-frequency pulse.

*Thrum.*

It wasn't coming from the depths. It was coming from the walls around them.

"The rock is singing," Kait said. She reached out to touch a pipe, then snatched her hand back. "It’s hot."

"Don't touch the infrastructure," Cassian said. He scanned the corridor. "The drill is resonating through the strata. It’s shaking the Chronite loose. If we’re standing on a vein when it liquefies, we fall through time."

"Like Joren?"

Cassian flinched. The name was a stone dropped in a well. *Joren.*

"Joren didn't fall through time," he said, his voice flat. "He was edited. There’s a difference."

"Did he scream?"

"No." Cassian started walking again, faster. "He didn't have time."

They reached the junction box: a massive hub of cables and fiber-optics that fed data to the Spire. Cassian knelt, prying the cover off with a crowbar. Sparks showered his gloves. He bypassed the lock, hotwiring the auxiliary line to his personal comm-unit.

"Who are you calling?" Kait asked. She was hopping from foot to foot, jittery with the adrenaline of the escape.

"Mother Otho."

"You think she’s still alive? She’s old, Cass. Like, really old. She probably forgot to wake up."

"She wakes up for the Queen." Cassian twisted the copper wire. The connection sparked. A burst of static roared from the speaker, then a voice.

*"...go... away..."*

"Otho." Cassian pressed his mouth to the grate. "It’s Cass. We have a situation. Sector 4. The Threshold."

*"...Cassian?* The voice was thin, rusted like old iron. *...you’re not supposed to be down there. The audit is in two hours..."*

"Audit is irrelevant. Vex accelerated the timeline. They’re drilling now."

A pause. The static popped. *...how far down?*"

"Basalt layer. We found the seal."

*...the seal?* The tone shifted. The weariness vanished, replaced by a sharp, cutting urgency. *...did you break it?**

"Not yet. But it’s active. The Chronite is... glowing. And there’s a voice."

*...a voice?* Otho coughed, a wet, rattling sound. *...don’t listen to it, boy. Whatever you do, don’t let it speak your name. If it knows your name, it can anchor to you. Get out. Get to the surface lift. Now.*

"We can’t," Cassian said. He watched the dust falling from the ceiling pipes. "The tremors are blocking the main lift. We have to take the Slurry Chute."

*...the Chute?* Otho sounded horrified. *...that’s suicide. That’s where they dump the biomass. You’ll be boiled...*

"Better than being buried." Cassian cut the line. He stood up.

"The Chute?" Kait made a face. "That smells like pee and dead people."

"It’s the only way up that doesn't have a Sanitizer checkpoint," Cassian said. "Unless you want to try explaining to Vex why we were digging in a restricted zone."

"I’d rather tell the Queen," Kait muttered. She kicked a bolt on the floor. "Can we take her with us?"

"We are not taking the fossil," Cassian said. He checked his watch. The needle was deep in the red. "We’re taking the memory. Come on."

They ran.

The Slurry Chute was a massive, vertical pipe that ran through the center of the city’s underbelly. It carried waste—organic and inorganic—down to the furnaces. The heat coming off the intake valve was enough to singe Cassian’s eyebrows.

He hacked the control panel. The wheel squealed as he turned it. A blast of humid, foul air hit them. It smelled of rot, sewage, and recycled death.

"Ladies first," Kait said.

Cassian grabbed her by the back of her coat and shoved her in. She screamed, but it turned into a laugh as the draft caught her.

Cassian jumped after her.

The inside of the chute was a slick, dark tube. They slid, controlling their descent with gloved hands against the friction-pads on the sides. Gravity fought them. The air pressure popped his ears.

*Down. Down.*

Then the updraft caught them. The massive fans at the bottom of the city, designed to suck smoke from the furnaces, roared to life. They were lifted, shooting upward like corks in a stream.

Cassian’s stomach lurched. The needle in his eye felt like it was drilling into his brain. He gritted his teeth, focusing on the circle of grey light above them.

They burst out onto a collection grate. Cassian rolled, absorbing the impact on his shoulder. He came up coughing in a cloud of white steam.

"Safe," Kait said. She was already up, shaking the slime off her coat. "That was fun."

Cassian pushed himself to his knees. He checked his surroundings. They were in the Mid-Levels. The Market.

The contrast was violent. After the silence and darkness of the Pit, the Market was a sensory assault. Neon signs hissed, powered by stolen Chronite. Merchants shouted over the din of haggling. The air smelled of frying grease, ozone, and unwashed bodies.

"Keep your head down," Cassian said. He pulled his hood up.

They moved through the crowd like ghosts. Cassian kept one hand on his knife, the other on Kait’s shoulder. The Market was a dangerous place on a good day. With the Bore active, the tension was palpable. People were shoving, stockpiling water, staring at the ceiling as the dust rained down.

"Chronite for sale!" a merchant bellowed, holding up a vial of black sludge. "Pure Year 900! Burn it and remember the sun!"

Cassian kept walking. He needed to get to the safehouse. Mother Otho’s cell was located behind the front of a tannery.

Then, the crowd froze.

The sound wasn't a noise. It was a pressure wave. A ripple that knocked hats off heads and shattered the cheap glass of the neon signs.

*CRACK.*

It came from below.

Cassian grabbed Kait, throwing her under a fruit cart. He covered her head with his body.

The ground shook. Not a tremor. An impact.

Above them, the heavy iron shutters of the market stalls rattled. A fine grey dust began to snow down, coating the fruit, the crowds, the cobblestones.

"The drill," Kait whispered, her eyes wide. "He broke through."

Cassian stood up. He looked at the people around them. They were confused, scared. But he felt something else.

He felt the *resonance*.

It started in his teeth. A vibration. Then his bones. The Chronite in his pocket—the sample he’d taken from the fissure—was glowing through the canvas. It was hot. Burning hot.

He pulled it out. The black oil was boiling.

"It's not just a drill," he said. "He breached the Threshold."

He looked up, toward the distant ceiling of the cavern, miles above. The Spire.

High Auditor Vex was up there, watching the readouts. Vex would know. He would know the seal was broken.

Cassian grabbed Kait’s hand. "Run."

They sprinted through the panic. The market was dissolving into chaos. Looters were smashing windows. The Sanitizer sirens began to wail—a deafening, mechanical shriek that drowned out the screams.

*Warning. Structural instability in Sector 4. Evacuate immediately.*

Cassian ignored the announcement. He ran for the tannery.

He kicked the door open. The smell of curing hides and chemicals was thick. He shoved aside a rack of leather aprons, revealing a heavy steel door in the back wall. He pounded on it. Three times. Two times. Three times.

The spyhole slid open. An eye looked out.

*...let him in...*

The door swung open. Mother Otho stood there. She looked terrible. Her skin was paper-thin, stretched tight over her skull. Her white hair was wild. But her eyes were clear.

She grabbed Cassian by the front of his shirt and hauled him inside, slamming the door and throwing the heavy bolts.

"You're late," she wheezed. She coughed, spitting black bile into a handkerchief.

"We had trouble," Cassian said. He leaned against the wall, gasping. "The seal. It's open."

Otho froze. She looked at Kait, then back at Cassian. She reached out and touched his face. Her hand was trembling, but her grip was iron.

"Did you see her?" she asked.

"The woman in the rock," Kait said, bouncing on her heels. "She blinked. Cassian says she's a fossil, but I think she's just sleepy."

Otho ignored the girl. She stared at Cassian. "Did she speak?"

"No," Cassian said. "But she laughed."

Otho let out a breath. It sounded like wind whistling through a tomb. She turned and shuffled toward the back room, her cane tapping on the stone floor.

"Then it's begun," she said. "The Past is waking up."

Cassian followed her. The safehouse was a single room, lit by a single green lamp-vine. In the center of the room was a table covered in maps—architectural schematics of the city’s strata.

Otho sat down heavily. She gestured to the chair opposite her.

"Show me," she said.

Cassian placed the glowing sample of Chronite on the table. The black oil pulsed with a pale, watery light. It cast strange shadows on the walls—shadows that looked like faces.

Otho stared at it. She didn't touch it.

"The Basalt Threshold," she whispered. "The Queen's prison."

"Vex is drilling straight through it," Cassian said. He leaned forward. "We have to stop him. If he extracts the Chronite from that layer, he doesn't just get energy. He releases the memory of the Interregnum. The Sanitation Era. All of it. It will flood the city."

"It will flood the city with madness," Otho corrected. "Memories are not stories, Cassian. They are experiences. If Vex burns that rock, three thousand years of trauma will hit the Spire all at once. The Censors will go insane. The populace will riot."

She looked up at him. Her eyes were wet.

"And we will be free," she whispered.

Cassian stared at her. "Free? Otho, the structural collapse will kill half the Mid-Levels."

"Sometimes a forest fire is necessary for new growth," she said. She sounded like a zealot. Like the Silentium.

"I'm not burning the city down," Cassian said. "I'm here to save the history, not weaponize it."

"History is a weapon," Otho snapped. She slammed her hand on the table. The impact knocked over a stack of papers. "Vex knows that. That is why he drills. He wants to weaponize the past to fuel his present. If we want to stop him, we have to be willing to break the machine."

She reached into her shawl and pulled out a small, jagged object. It looked like a piece of a gear, made of a metal Cassian didn't recognize. It shimmered, iridescent, like oil on water.

"I found this," she said. "In the ventilation shafts. It’s Pre-Scorch."

Cassian frowned. "That shouldn't exist. The Scorch burned everything."

"This didn't burn," Otho said. "It survived. It’s a key, Cassian. To the Obsidian Eye."

Cassian felt the blood drain from his face. "The lens in the Spire? That's a mile of solid glass and steel."

"It's a door," Otho said. "And this key opens it. If you can get to the Eye, you can reverse the polarity. You can stop the drill."

"And then what?"

"Then we wake her up." Otho pointed at the glowing Chronite on the table. "The Queen. Elara. She is the only one who can control the Chronite flow. She stopped them once. She can stop them again."

Cassian laughed. It was a dry, humorless sound. "Great plan, Otho. Let's go wake up a three-thousand-year-old corpse and ask her to save us. What makes you think she’ll want to? She was a tyrant. She buried herself to kill the city, not save it."

"She buried herself to save the future," Otho said, her voice dropping to a hiss. "I have read the journals, Cassian. The real ones. The ones the Censors didn't burn. She didn't start the Silence. She *was* the Silence. She absorbed the noise. She took the temporal displacement into her own body to stop the Nausea from spreading."

She leaned forward. Her face was gaunt, but her eyes burned with a fierce, fanatic light.

"She is a martyr. And she is the only weapon we have left."

Cassian looked at the sample. The light was fading. The pulse was slowing down.

He touched his left temple. The needle was gone. In its place was a cold numbness.

He stood up.

"I need air," he said.

"Cassian," Otho called out.

He stopped at the door. He didn't turn around.

"I'll think about it," he said.

"There is no time to think," Otho said. "The drill doesn't sleep. And neither does she."

Cassian walked out.

The hallway was cold. He found a bench and sat down, putting his head in his hands. His gloves were stained with black ichor. His hands were shaking.

*The Chronoshakes.*

He was sick. He was dying. He had been down in the deep too long. The Lag was setting in. He looked at his watch.

*10:00 PM.*

But his internal clock said it was 4:00 in the morning. He felt jet-lagged inside his own skin.

He closed his eyes. He listened to the city.

The distant roar of the Bore. The clatter of the trains. The hum of the ventilation fans.

And, beneath it all, a sound he hadn't noticed before.

A voice.

*Cassian.*

His eyes snapped open.

He wasn't in the hallway. He was back in the fissure. He was standing in front of the rock face.

The woman was there. She wasn't fossilized anymore. She was made of light and shadow, her edges blurring into the black rock.

She reached out a hand. Her fingers were long, tapered, made of obsidian.

*Dig,* she said.

Cassian gasped. He fell off the bench, hitting the floor hard.

Kait was standing in the doorway, watching him. She wasn't bouncing anymore. She was still. Very still.

"You heard her too," she said.

Cassian scrambled to his feet. He wiped the sweat from his forehead.

"It's the Nausea," he said. "It causes auditory hallucinations."

"She said your name," Kait said. She stepped into the room, closing the door behind her. She looked at the map on the table, then at the sample of glowing Chronite.

"She knows us," Kait said. "She’s been waiting."

She walked over to the table and picked up the sample. She held it up to the light.

"We have to go back," she said.

Cassian stared at her. "We just escaped. Vex is drilling. The ceiling is coming down."

"We have to go back," Kait repeated. She turned to him. Her eyes were glowing with a faint, blue light—the same light as the Chronite. "She told me where the key goes."

Cassian froze. "What key?"

"The one the old lady has." Kait pointed toward the back room. "The Pre-Scorch thing. It fits in the Bore. It fits in the machine."

Cassian felt a chill that had nothing to do with the temperature. "How do you know that?"

"She told me," Kait said simply. "She says if we put it in the machine, it will stop the drill. But we have to be there to turn it."

Cassian looked at the girl. He looked at the black ichor staining her hands where she held the glowing stone.

She was infected. The resonance was rewriting her timeline.

"Give me the stone," Cassian said.

Kait hugged it to her chest. "No. It’s warm. It feels like... like a heartbeat."

"Kait." He stepped forward. "That’s not a toy. That’s compressed time. If you hold it too long, you’ll start to age. Or you’ll disappear."

"I don't care," she said. "I want to see the Queen. I want to ask her for a crown."

Cassian grabbed her wrist. He tried to pry her fingers loose. She was stronger than she looked. Her grip was like iron.

"Let go," she hissed.

"Kait, drop it."

She looked at him. Her face changed. For a second, the overlay of another face flickered across hers—a woman’s face, sharp and regal.

*Yield to me,* the voice echoed in Cassian’s mind. Not Kait’s voice. *The vessel is small, but willing.*

Cassian let go of her hand. He stepped back, his heart hammering against his ribs.

He understood.

The Queen wasn't just a fossil. She was a consciousness. A pattern of information encoded in the Chronite. And she was looking for a way out.

She was using Kait.

"Okay," Cassian said slowly. He held up his hands, palms open. "Okay. We keep the stone. But we don't go back down there yet. We need to plan."

Kait relaxed. The blue light in her eyes faded. She looked like a normal, grubby street kid again.

"We have to go back," she said, pouting. "She says it’s time."

"Soon," Cassian lied.

He looked at the map on the table. The schematic of the Bore. The massive drill shaft descending miles into the earth.

If Otho was right, and the key could stop the machine... and if Kait was right, that the key needed to be placed inside the drill itself...

Then they had a suicide mission.

Cassian checked his watch. The needle was pinned against the stop.

He looked at Kait. She was petting the Chronite sample like a kitten.

"Get your coat," Cassian said. "We’re going to the Spire."

"The Spire?" Kait’s face lit up. "Really? Are we going to rob the Censors?"

"Something like that," Cassian said.

He grabbed the map from the table and folded it, shoving it into his pocket.

He wasn't doing this for the Queen. He wasn't doing it for Otho. He was doing it because the alternative was sitting here and waiting for the ceiling to crush them.

And because, deep down, in the part of him that was sick with Nausea, he wanted to know.

He wanted to know if his brother was still down there, trapped in the black rock, waiting for someone to dig him out.

If the Queen could wake the dead, maybe she could wake Joren.

"Come on," Cassian said.

He opened the door. The sirens were still screaming. The city was trembling.

They stepped out into the chaos.
