The city of Ouroboros did not land. It crashed.

Cassian stood in the open hatch of the Mid-Levels, his boots welded to the deck-plate by the sheer deceleration. The world had turned to white noise.  The sound was a physical thing, a hammer blow to the sternum that left him gasping.

They were down.

The air rushing past him didn't smell of sulfur or coal dust or the recycled rot of the Slums. It smelled sharp. Ozone. Burnt silica. 

Cassian gripped the railing. His knuckles were white. He waited for the nausea to take him. When you moved a city, you moved time. The shift should have liquified his insides. He should be tasting his childhood in the back of his throat.

The needle behind his left eye, the constant migraine of the Excavator, was gone.

"You puking yet?"

Cassian turned. Kait stood a few yards away on the deck. She looked small. The coat of rags she wore whipped in the wind, snapping like a flag. Her goggles were pushed up on her forehead, revealing eyes that were too wide, too bright. She was grinning. It was a feral, gap-toothed expression.

"No," Cassian said. His voice sounded thin. It didn't carry the gravel-weight of the deep strata.

"Disappointing," Kait said. She stepped closer to the edge, peering over the lip of the city-hull. "I wanted to see if you'd spew black. I got a copper coin on it."

"Save your coin." Cassian joined her at the railing.

 Steam hissed from the cooling joints, pluming white against the dark horizon.

And beyond the city?

The sky.

It wasn't a ceiling. It wasn't the Obsidian Eye or the smog-choked artificial dark of the Spire. It was blue. Unbelievably, violently blue. A dome of endless color arching over them. And the sun.

Cassian flinched. He raised a hand to block it. The light was hot. It wasn't the dry heat of a forge or the radioactive warmth of a Chronite vent. It was heavy. It pressed down on his shoulders.

"It's bright," Kait said. She didn't flinch. She stared at it, mesmerized. "Like a diamond lamp."

"Don't look too long," Cassian muttered. "You'll burn your retinas."

"Pretty way to go blind." She spun around, her boots clanging on the metal. "So. We're here. The Queen's fancy garden."

 No trees. No ruins. Just the dunes of shattered obsidian and the skeletons of ancient skyscrapers, thrusting up like broken ribs.

"It's a grave," Cassian said.

"Looks like a scrapyard to me," Kait said. She crouched down, tapping the metal deck. "Hey. Hey, Old Man. You feel that?"

Cassian looked at the metal. He didn't need to touch it. He could hear it. A low, resonant hum. The city was vibrating.

He was vibrating.

He felt a spark of himself ignite. Not a ghost. A recorder.

He looked at the dunes. He looked at the distant, shimmering heat haze. He looked at the birds circling, confused by the new metal mountain thrust into their domain.

 It was no longer a cylinder digging down. It was a fortress sitting on the surface.

The engines died.

The violet fire faded.

The silence of the surface rushed in. It was a heavy, oppressive silence. No drills. No gears. No alarms. Just the wind whistling over the sharp edges of the city.

Cassian hovered there, a consciousness suspended in the bright air.

He waited.

He watched the hatch open on the Mid-Levels.

Kait stood on the roof of the world.

She looked up. She shielded her eyes.

She saw the sun. She saw the dunes.

Then she looked down. She looked at the metal of the roof beneath her boots.

She crouched. She touched the iron.

The metal hummed back at her. A low, steady thrum. A heartbeat.

Cassian poured his memory into the touch.

He showed her the way to the Spire. He showed her the location of the water filters. He showed her how to bypass the security locks Vex had engaged. He gave her the map of the city, drawn in vibration.

Kait smiled. A gap-toothed, fierce smile.

"I hear you," she said to the empty air. "Loud and clear."

She stood up. She put her goggles on her forehead.

"Right," she said. "Let's get to work."

Cassian Varr, the Second Son of the House of Bells, the Excavator, the Witness, let the wind take him.

He dissolved into the atmosphere, a scattering of gold dust in the blue.

The transition was not a blink. It was a drag.

One moment, he was dust in the wind. The next, he was meat. He was bone. He was Cassian again, slumped against the cold iron of the deck-plate. He gasped, inhaling a lungful of sharp, clean air. It tasted like nothing he had ever known. No ash. No mold. Just cold.

"Whoa," Kait said. She was crouching over him, poking his cheek with a dirty fingernail. "You went away. Like a candle snuffing out."

Cassian slapped her hand away. He sat up. His muscles screamed. 

"I'm here," Cassian rasped. He checked his pockets. His chisel was there. His chronometer. The glass vial of Chronite oil. "Did I... say anything?"

"You didn't speak," Kait said. She stood up and offered him a hand. He ignored it, pushing himself to his feet. His knees popped. "You hummed. The metal hummed back. It was weird. I liked it."

Cassian looked around. They were on the outer hull of the Mid-Levels, a vast, flat expanse of reinforced steel that used to be the ceiling of the Industrial District. Now it was the floor of the world.

"We need to move," he said. "Vex will have sent Sanitizers to secure the perimeter."

"Let him come," Kait said. She pulled a wrench from her belt—a heavy iron thing she’d stolen from a maintenance drone. "I got his number right here."

"You have a wrench," Cassian said. "He has sonic cannons that can liquefy your bones. Move."

They ran. The wind tried to push them over, a constant, invisible hand shoving at their shoulders. Cassian kept his head down. He focused on the rhythm of his boots on the metal. Clang. Scuff. Clang.

The city was quiet. The population was hiding in their cells, terrified of the sun. The lifts were locked down. But the maintenance hatches were open. The rats always found a way.

Cassian found a vent intake near the base of the Spire's support strut. It was rattling.

"Here," he said. He gripped the grille. It was warm. The vibration was stronger here. He could feel the pulse of the water pumps deep inside the city.

He pulled. The metal groaned, resisting. He grunted, his shoulders straining. The Nausea might have been gone, but his strength was fading. He was thirty-four years old, feeling like fifty.

"Help," he bit out.

Kait rolled her eyes. She stepped past him, wedged her fingers into the gap, and kicked the frame with both heels. The metal shrieked. With a snap, the bolts gave way. The grille clattered to the deck.

"Show off," Cassian muttered.

"Get in, Grandpa," she said.

Cassian crawled into the duct. The air inside was stale, recycled, smelling of ozone and burnt rubber. It smelled like home. It smelled like the past.

He exhaled. The tension in his jaw loosened. He was back inside the beast.

"Where are we going?" Kait whispered. She crawled behind him, her movements quick and agile.

"The Spire," Cassian said. "Vex is there. He's trying to bypass the Queen's lockdown. We need to beat him to the Control Room."

"What about the Queen?" Kait asked. "The fossil lady?"

"We can't wake her yet," Cassian said. "If Vex is drilling, the resonance will kill her. We have to stop the drill first."

He stopped at a junction. The pipe split three ways.

"Which way?" Kait asked.

Cassian closed his eyes. He pressed his hand against the metal. He listened.

The city was telling him a story.

To the left: the slow, steady beat of the Slums. Low frequency. Muddy.

To the right: the frantic, high-pitched whine of the Market. Chaotic. Disharmonic.

Straight ahead: The Spire. A cold, sterile hum. Perfect pitch. No soul.

"Straight," Cassian said.

"You sure?" Kait asked. "Straight sounds boring."

"Straight is where the power is," Cassian said.

They crawled. The tunnel narrowed. Cassian’s shoulders scraped against the sides. He felt a sudden wave of panic—not his own. The city's.

The walls were closing in. The pressure was spiking.

"Cass," Kait said. Her voice was tight.

"I know."

The tunnel ahead was glowing. A faint violet light, pulsing.

"What is that?" Kait whispered.

"Sanitizer field," Cassian said. "Security lockdown."

He reached into his pocket and pulled out the vial of Chronite oil. It was black, viscous, swirling with gold dust.

"Cover your eyes," he said.

"What?"

"Cover your eyes!"

Kait buried her face in her arms.

Cassian uncorked the vial. He didn't drink it. He poured a single drop onto the screw-head of the vent cover blocking their path.

The drop hit the metal.

*Sound.*

A chord shattered the silence. A perfect, resonant C-major, amplified by the steel. The sound rippled outward, washing over the violet field. The light flickered. The field buzzed, fought back, and then died with a pathetic electronic wheeze.

The path was clear.

"Music," Kait said, peering through her fingers. "You used music to pick the lock."

"Resonance," Cassian said. He corked the vial. "Everything has a frequency, Kait. Even locks."

They dropped down into the Spire.

It was... quiet. The Obsidian Eye was dark. The marble floors were covered in a fine layer of dust. The Sanitizers had cleared the sector, pulling their troops back to the upper levels.

Cassian landed hard. He rolled, absorbing the impact. Kait landed beside him, silent as a cat.

They were in the Grand Atrium. Massive columns of bronze rose up to support a ceiling so high it was lost in shadow. Statues of the ancient Censors lined the walls, their faces blank, their hands raised in blessing or warning.

"Creepy," Kait whispered. "Look at them. They all look like Vex."

"They aren't Vex," Cassian said. "They were the ones who built the cage."

He moved to the central console. It was a monstrosity of brass and glass, the nerve center of the city's environmental controls. The screens were dark.

"Can you turn it on?" Kait asked. She was wandering the room, tapping on the statues.

Cassian examined the console. The ignition slot was shaped like a key. A specific, organic key.

"I need the High Auditor's signet," Cassian said. "Or a bio-sample from the bloodline."

Kait snorted. "Good luck asking Vex for a handout."

Cassian looked at the statue nearest to him. It was a woman, tall and severe, wearing a dress that looked like armor.

"The Queen," Cassian said. "Her bloodline is dormant. But her body is still down there."

"Use the oil," Kait said. She pointed at the vial in his hand. "Use the time-stuff."

"It's not that simple," Cassian said. "I can't just pour history into a machine. I need a specific memory. A specific moment."

"So?" Kait shrugged. "Pick one. You're full of them."

Cassian looked at the dark glass of the monitor. He saw his reflection. A gaunt man with hollow eyes. He looked like a ghost.

He thought of the Day of White Dust. He thought of his brother, Joren. He thought of the moment the drill breached the vein and the world turned white.

He uncorked the vial.

"Cass," Kait said. She stopped pacing. She looked at him. "Your nose."

He touched his upper lip. His fingers came away slick with black ichor.

"I'm fine," he said.

"You're leaking," she said. She stepped closer, reaching out.

"Don't touch me," Cassian snapped. "The resonance is unstable. If you touch me while I'm holding this, you'll get dragged in."

"I'm not scared of a little drag," Kait said. But she lowered her hand.

Cassian held the vial over the ignition slot. He closed his eyes. He focused on the memory. The heat of the explosion. The scream of the rock. The look on Joren's face as he faded.

He tipped the vial.

The black oil dripped onto the brass.

The console shrieked.

Sparks flew. The glass screens flared to life, displaying a chaotic cascade of numbers and diagrams. The air pressure in the room dropped. Cassian's ears popped.

"Whoa!" Kait jumped back.

 The lights in the Atrium flickered on, one by one, climbing up the columns like waking giants.

"System Online," a mechanical voice droned. "Audit Complete. Welcome, High Auditor."

Cassian slumped against the console. He wiped his nose. The bleeding stopped. The pain faded.

"We're in," he said.

"That was wicked," Kait breathed. "Do it again."

"If I do it again, my brain will turn to mush," Cassian said. He typed a command into the terminal. "I'm locking the Sanitizers out of the lower levels. They won't be able to take the lifts down."

"And the Queen?"

Cassian pulled up the schematic of the city. He zoomed in on the Pit. The Basalt Threshold.

It was glowing.

A red warning light pulsed on the screen.

"Unauthorized Excavation Detected," the voice said. "Sector: The Basalt Threshold. Operator: Sanitation Directorate."

"Vex," Cassian hissed. "He's already drilling."

"He's fast," Kait said.

"He's desperate," Cassian said. "He knows the city is dying. He's trying to bleed the stone dry."

Cassian’s fingers flew across the keys. He tried to engage the emergency brakes on the main drill.

"Access Denied," the voice said. "Biometric Lock Override. Authorized User: Vex."

"Damn it!"

Kait climbed up onto the console. She looked at the screen. "He's got the keys?"

"He owns the machine," Cassian said. "We can't stop the drill from here. We have to go down there. We have to jam the gears physically."

"Like a wrench in the works?" Kait asked, patting the heavy iron tool on her belt.

"Exactly," Cassian said. "But it's not just a drill, Kait. It's a sonic cannon. If we get close, the vibration will liquefy us before we can touch it."

"Then we need earplugs," she said.

"We need shielding," Cassian corrected. "We need to find the old damping gear. The Silentium equipment."

"The crazy mouth-sewers?" Kait asked. "They have stuff that can stop that?"

"They've been sabotaging the drills for centuries," Cassian said. "They know how to kill a machine."

He pushed away from the console. "We need to get to the Rust Belt. That's where they hide."

Kait hopped down from the console. She looked at the massive bronze doors of the Atrium. They were sealed.

"Lead the way, Excavator," she said.

Cassian walked toward the door. He stopped. He looked back at the statue of the Queen.

She seemed to be watching him.

He touched the cold bronze face.

"I'm coming," he whispered. "I haven't forgotten."

The heavy thud of boots echoed from the corridor outside. Heavy, rhythmic. Marching.

"Company," Kait hissed. She ducked behind a statue.

Cassian drew his chisel. He felt the weight of it. It was a comforting weight.

The doors didn't open. They were blown open.

A shockwave of purple energy shattered the bronze hinges. The doors slammed into the marble, skidding across the floor, sending up clouds of dust.

Sanitizers poured in. Six of them. White armor. Gas masks. Sonic rifles leveled at chest height.

They moved in perfect unison. They didn't speak. They didn't breathe. They were the machine's immune system.

"Secure the terminal," the lead Sanitizer said. His voice was amplified, metallic and cold. "Eliminate the biomass."

Cassian didn't hesitate. He threw the chisel.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a tuning fork.

He threw it at the marble floor between them and the Sanitizers.

The chisel struck the stone.

*Ping.*

A single, high-pitched note.

The Sanitizers stumbled. Their audio-receptors were sensitive. The sound threw off their equilibrium.

"Run!" Cassian shouted.

He grabbed Kait by the collar of her rags and hauled her toward the service exit behind the console.

"Hey!" she protested. "I could have taken them!"

"Not with six rifles," Cassian said. He kicked the service door open.

They tumbled into the dark of the maintenance shaft. The door slammed shut behind them, locking automatically.

Cassian leaned against the metal, gasping. His heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird.

"That was close," Kait said. She was grinning again. "Did you see their faces? Well, their masks. But I bet they were mad."

Cassian slid down the wall. He sat on the floor, head in his hands.

"We're running out of time," he said. "The Nausea is gone, but the city is still falling apart."

"So we fix it," Kait said. She sat down next to him. "We smash the drill. We wake the lady. We have tea."

Cassian looked at her. 

"We smash the drill," Cassian agreed. "And then we find a way to keep the lights on without burning the past."

He stood up. He offered her a hand. This time, she took it.

"Come on, Duster," he said. "Let's go steal some enemy equipment."

They moved into the darkness of the shaft, heading down toward the Rust Belt, heading toward the end of the world.
