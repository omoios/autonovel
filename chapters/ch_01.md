The air in the shaft tasted of wet copper and old dust.

Cassian knelt on the shale, his pick hovering over the black vein. The rock here was soft,Year 400 sediment, rotten with time. He struck. The chisel rang against the Chronite, a high-pitched *zing* that vibrated in his molars. A flake of black oil peeled away, glistening like obsidian glass.

It hummed.

The sound was low, subsonic, a thrumming pressure against his eardrums. Cassian ignored it. He pressed his thumb against the flake. It was warm. The heat soaked into his glove, traveled up his wrist, and settled behind his left eye. A sharp, needle-point prick of pain.

He flinched.

"Just a scratch," he whispered. His voice was swallowed by the tunnel.

 It couldn't decide what year it was. Cassian tapped the casing. The needle stuttered and settled on *Now*. 

He had to move.

Cassian wedged the chisel deeper into the vein. He needed to clear the sediment before the resonance shifted. If he missed the frequency, the Chronite would harden into useless granite, and the day's labor would be wasted. He couldn't afford waste. Not with the quota.

He struck again. *Clink. Clink. Clink.*

Rhythm was safety. The noise of the digging drowned out the other sounds. The groans of the city settling. The distant shriek of the drill-bits in the Spire, five miles above. And, underneath it all, the Voice.

*Dig.*

It wasn't a sound. It was a pressure spike in the blood. A memory that wasn't his.

*Cassian. The dark is cold.*

He gripped the pick until his knuckles turned white. The needle behind his eye twisted. "Quiet," he said to the empty tunnel.

He worked faster. Black slurry pooled around his knees. The smell hit him then—ozone and burnt hair. The scent of a funeral pyre. It was leaking from the rock. The Chronite was volatile today, bleeding echoes of the Sanitation Era.

He pulled a chunk free. It was the size of a pigeon's egg, heavy and oily. He held it up to the beam of his helmet lamp. Inside the black resin, a tiny bubble of trapped gas swirled. A moment. A scream. A kiss. It was impossible to tell which without a burner.

Cassian shoved the chunk into his canvas satchel. The bag clinked. He had enough for half a loaf of bread. Maybe a whole one if the market merchant was feeling generous or drunk.

He stood up. His knees popped. The gravity down here was heavier than in the Mid-Levels, pulling at his shoulders, dragging his spine down. He felt every year of his thirty-four years. Or was it fifty-two? The lines on his forearms—the black bars of his Yield—were fading. His body was rejecting the ink. That was bad. When the ink faded, the timeline was next.

He hiked up the tunnel, boots slipping on the slick mud. The passage narrowed, forcing him to turn sideways. He scraped his shoulder against the wall. The rock bit through his jacket.

"Move it," a voice hissed from behind.

Cassian flattened himself against the wall as a team of Excavators pushed past. They were younger, faster, their eyes wide and frantic. They didn't speak. They didn't look at him. In the Pit, eye contact was an invitation for a fight or a audit, and both were fatal.

He waited until their footsteps faded. Then he climbed.

The ladder was rusted iron, bolted into the vertical shaft. Each rung was cold, slick with condensation. He climbed hand over hand, his satchel banging against his hip. The air grew thinner as he ascended. The smell of sulfur faded, replaced by the acrid tang of coal smoke and unwashed bodies.

Level 14. The Rust Belt.

He pulled himself onto the platform. A catwalk made of grating extended over the abyss, suspended by chains that moaned in the wind. Below him, the Pit yawned open—a black mouth waiting to be fed.

Cassian unclipped his helmet. He wiped the sweat from his forehead. It came away gray. He was filthy. He always was.

"You're late," a voice said.

Cassian didn't jump. He didn't have the energy for it. He turned slowly.

Kait sat on a crate bolted to the railing. Her legs dangled over the drop. She was twelve, maybe thirteen, wrapped in a coat made of sewn-together rags. A massive pair of goggles rested on her forehead, magnifying her eyes to insectoid proportions. She chewed on a length of copper wire.

"I'm exactly on time," Cassian said.

"The bell rang three minutes ago," Kait said. "I counted."

"I was finishing the seam."

"You were dawdling. Mother Otho is pacing. She paces when she's nervous. When she paces, she breaks things. Last week she broke a mug. The week before, she broke a toe."

Cassian walked past her, checking the seal on his satchel. "Is the tunnel clear?"

"Clear as a whistle. No Sanitizers. No Dusters. Just the rats." Kait hopped off the crate. She landed silently, like a drop of water hitting stone. "I saw a Rock-Rat with three eyes. Big as a cat. It was eating a bolt."

"Good."

"They're hungry. The rats are hungry. Which means the drill is moving."

Cassian stopped. He looked at her. "What drill?"

"The Big One. The Bore." Kait pointed upward, toward the ceiling of the cavern, miles above. "I can feel the vibration in my teeth. Can't you?"

Cassian pressed his tongue against his back molars. A hum. Yes. He had thought it was just the Nausea. "It's always moving."

"Not like this." Kait stepped closer, lowering her voice. "It's speeding up. My rats say the gear-ratio is doubled."

"Your rats don't know gears."

"They know teeth. They know the sound of grinding bone." She tapped the side of her head. "They hear it through the vents. The Sanitizers are pushing the Bore. They're going for the Threshold."

Cassian felt the needle behind his eye twist again. He rubbed his left ear. A lie.

If they were hitting the Basalt Threshold, they were digging into the Queen's layer. They were digging into *her*.

"Go," he said. "Tell Otho I'm coming. And tell her to pack the burner. We're going to need to read the yield tonight."

Kait grinned. She bared her teeth, which were pointed and slightly yellow. "I like it when you burn things. It smells like history."

She scrambled up the service ladder, moving fast, a spider in the dark.

Cassian watched her go. Then he looked down into his satchel. The Chronite chunk pulsed in the gloom. He touched it again. The heat flared.

*Cassian. Help me.*

The voice wasn't in his ear. It was in the marrow of his arm bone.

"Shut up," he muttered.

He hoisted the bag and started the long climb to the surface.

***

The Mid-Levels were a riot of noise.

Cassian stepped off the lift and into the crush. The Grand Lift Station was a cavern of steam and shouting. Vendors hawked slurry-cakes from carts made of old oil drums. The smell here was distinctive—fried grease, wet wool, and the sour tang of too many bodies breathing too little air.

He kept his head down. He moved with the flow of the crowd, a gray ghost in a gray coat. The market was dangerous. Pickpockets worked the crowds with fingers lighter than air. Enforcers in white jackets stood on the corners, their faces covered by porcelain masks, scanning the crowd with sonic-dampeners.

Cassian adjusted his collar. He needed to be invisible.

He pushed through a gaggle of laborers hauling baskets of iron-moss. They cursed in a dialect he didn't recognize. He sidestepped a puddle of rainbow-hued wastewater leaking from a dye-vat.

"Years for sale!" a merchant bellowed. "Refined memory! Pure Year 900! See the Scorch with your own eyes!"

Cassian ignored the pitch. He didn't want to see the Scorch. He saw enough fire in his sleep.

He needed parts. His burner was leaking gas. He needed a valve, a compression spring, and a filter.

He found the stall he was looking for. It was tucked behind a pillar of riveted steel, smelling of ozone and burnt plastic. The sign above it read *SCRAP & SAVAGE*.

The woman behind the counter was welding a copper pipe. The flare of the torch illuminated her face—scarred, missing an eyebrow, with a tattoo of a gear on her neck.

"I need a seal," Cassian said. "For a Type-4 burner."

She didn't look up. The torch hissed. "Got no Type-4. Only Type-6. Military surplus."

"Type-6 is too hot. It'll vaporize the echo."

"Then don't burn it," she said. She cut the torch. The sudden silence was deafening. She looked at him, eyes like flint. "You're the Varr boy."

"I'm a man."

"You're a boy who digs too deep." She wiped her hands on a rag. "I heard about your brother."

Cassian stiffened. His hand went to his left ear. He stopped himself. "Don't."

"He got edited," she said. "My cousin saw it. He just... faded. Like a smudge on a window."

"I need the seal," Cassian said. His voice was flat.

She stared at him for a long moment. Then she reached under the counter and dropped a brass ring onto the grease-stained wood. It glinted dully.

"Type-4," she said. "I was saving it."

"How much?"

"Two bars."

"One."

"One and a half. And you tell me what you found."

Cassian paused. He touched the satchel at his hip. "Nothing. Just shale."

"Liar." She leaned forward. "The vibration is changing, Cassian. The whole city feels it. The gears are grinding. The drill is eating the quiet. You found something in the dark."

He placed a bar of compressed Chronite on the counter. It was black, dull, unrefined. Worth a day's wages.

She took it. She bit it. Her teeth crunched against the outer shell. "Sour," she said. "Old fear. Good vintage." She swept the bar into her drawer. "Take the seal. And be careful. The white masks are asking questions. They're looking for a leak."

"What leak?"

"The kind that screams."

Cassian pocketed the seal. He turned away, pulling his collar higher.

He moved faster now. The crowd felt thicker. The faces looked blurred, indistinct. He felt the prickle on the back of his neck—the sensation of being watched. He scanned the shadows above the stalls.

There.

A figure in white, standing on a mezzanine balcony. Porcelain mask. Long coat. A sonic-cannon resting on its shoulder.

A Sanitizer.

Cassian ducked behind a stack of crates. His heart hammered against his ribs. The needle behind his eye throbbed in time with his pulse. *Thrum-thrum. Thrum-thrum.*

He waited. He counted to one hundred.

When he looked again, the balcony was empty.

He exhaled. The air tasted like ash. He couldn't go back to the safe house the usual way. He had to take the service tunnels.

***

The safe house was located in the disutilized ventilation shafts of Sector 7. It was cramped, hot, and smelled of mold.

Cassian squeezed through the narrow access pipe and dropped onto the concrete floor. He landed in a crouch, absorbing the impact.

"You're late," a voice wheezed.

She was twenty-four.

"Traffic was heavy," Cassian said. He set his satchel on the workbench. "I got the seal."

Otho didn't look at the seal. She was looking at him. Her eyes were sharp, youthful, buried in the ruin of her face. "You look pale, Cassian. The shakes?"

"It's the depth."

Cassian stepped forward. He rolled up his left sleeve.

The tattoos were fading. The black bars that tracked his Yield were bleeding into his skin, turning from ink to bruise. The skin was translucent, showing the blue veins beneath.

Otho traced the lines with a fingernail. "You're drifting."

"I'm stable."

"You're fading. Your timeline is desynchronizing." She looked up at him. "How much time do you have left?"

"Hours. Days."

"Don't lie to your mother, boy."

Cassian pulled his sleeve down. "I have enough. I have to finish the seam."

Otho sighed. The sound whistled in her throat. "Kait said the drill is moving."

"She said the rats told her."

"The rats are liars, but she's right about the drill." Otho stood up, her joints cracking like pistol shots. She moved to the window—a crack in the ventilation shaft that looked out over the Pit. "The vibration is changing. The city is waking up."

She turned back to him. "Show me the yield."

Cassian opened the satchel. He took out the chunk of Chronite. It pulsed in the dim light of the room, casting long, flickering shadows.

Otho didn't touch it. She held her hand over it. She closed her eyes. "It's heavy," she whispered. "Heavy with grief."

"It's Year 400," Cassian said. "The Sanitation Era."

"No." Otho opened her eyes. "It's older than that. It's underneath the Sanitation. It's... the Silence."

Cassian froze. "That's impossible. The Silence is a myth. A blank spot."

"History is not a blank page, Cassian. It is a palimpsest. Someone wrote over the Silence, but the original text is still there." She pointed a trembling finger at the stone. "Burn it."

"If I burn it, and it's the Silence, it could attract the Auditors."

"If we don't burn it, we won't know what we're digging for." She coughed, a wet, hacking sound that brought up black phlegm. She spat it into a rag. "Do it."

Cassian moved to the burner. It was a crude device, a modified blowtorch hooked to a gas canister. He fitted the new seal Otho had given him. It clicked into place.

He placed the Chronite chunk on the asbestos plate.

He lit the flame.

The blue fire hissed. He directed the jet of heat at the stone.

For a moment, nothing happened. The stone soaked in the heat. Then, a hairline crack appeared.

*Sound.*

The room filled with the sound of screaming.

It wasn't loud. It was quiet. A thousand voices whispering at once. The shadows on the wall lengthened, stretching into impossible shapes. The air grew cold.

Cassian staggered back, clutching his head. The needle behind his eye became a knife.

*Not Joren. Not him.*

The vision took hold.

He saw a throne room. Not the Spire, but something older. Carved from living rock. A woman sat on the throne. She was tall, her skin pale as marble, her hair a cascade of black oil. She wore a crown of jagged obsidian.

She stood up. She pointed a finger at the sky.

"Stop," she said. Her voice was the sound of tectonic plates grinding. "We go no further."

Then, darkness. Concrete poured, crushing the room. Burying her alive.

The vision snapped.

The Chronite stone crumbled into gray dust. The flame on the burner died.

Cassian fell to his knees. He gasped for air. The room was spinning.

"Cassian!" Otho was at his side, gripping his shoulders. "Breathe. It's just an echo. It's not real."

He pushed her away. He crawled to the workbench. He grabbed his notebook. His hand was shaking so badly he could barely hold the pen.

"I saw her," he rasped. "I saw the Queen."

Otho stared at the pile of gray ash. "The Basalt Threshold," she whispered. "They found her."

"Not they," Cassian said. "Me."

 He had absorbed a fraction of the timeline.

He looked at Otho. Her face was a mask of terror.

"The drill," Cassian said. "Vex isn't just drilling for Chronite. He's drilling for her. He's going to crack the seal."

"If he cracks the seal..." Otho trailed off.

"He releases the past," Cassian finished. "And the past eats the present."

He stood up. The nausea receded, replaced by a cold, hard clarity. He knew what he had to do.

"I have to go back down," he said.

"You'll die," Otho said. "You're already half-ghost."

"I have to get to her before the drill does."

He grabbed his coat. He checked his pick. He checked his satchel.

"Cassian," Otho said. Her voice was soft. "You can't save everyone. Some things are meant to stay buried."

"I don't want to save everyone," he said. He touched the pocket watch in his vest pocket. It ticked against his palm. "I just want to save the history."

He climbed into the ventilation pipe.

"Wait," Otho called out. "Take Kait. She knows the rat-runs. If the Sanitizers are in the tunnels, you'll need eyes."

Cassian paused. He looked back at the old woman. She looked small in the big chair.

"Send her to the lift," he said. "I'll wait."

He disappeared into the dark.

***

The descent was faster this time. He didn't stop at the Rust Belt. He went all the way to the bottom.

The Pit.

The air here was hot. It smelled of sulfur and blood. The darkness was absolute, broken only by the green glow of lamp-vines clinging to the ceiling.

Kait was waiting for him at the foot of the ladder. She was bouncing on the balls of her feet, eager.

"Did you see it?" she whispered. "Did you see the Queen?"

"Quiet," Cassian said.

He moved to the face of the excavation. It was a jagged hole in the rock, leading downward into the earth.

The sound was different here. The hum was louder. A discordant shriek.

*Dig. Dig. The dark is cold.*

The voice was back. It wasn't Joren. It was something else. Something older.

Cassian held up his hand. "Stop."

He pressed his palm against the rock face. The stone was vibrating.

"What is it?" Kait asked. "The drill?"

"No," Cassian said. "The drill is above us. This is... this is a response."

He grabbed his pick. He struck the rock.

*Crack.*

A fissure appeared. Light spilled out. Not the green light of the lamp-vines, nor the electric light of the Spire. It was a pale, watery light. The color of a forgotten dawn.

"Whoa," Kait said.

Cassian leaned closer. He peered into the crack.

He saw a face.

It was a woman, fossilized in the black rock. Her eyes were open. They were made of obsidian. She was staring at him.

She blinked.

Cassian scrambled back, dropping his pick. The clatter echoed in the silence.

"Did you see that?" Kait hissed. "Did you see her move?"

"It's a trick of the light," Cassian said. His heart was hammering against his ribs like a trapped bird.

"It wasn't the light," Kait said. She stepped forward, fearless. "She's waking up."

Cassian looked at the fissure. The light was pulsing. A rhythm.

*Thrum. Thrum.*

It matched the needle behind his eye.

He realized then what the sound was. It wasn't a drill. It was a heartbeat.

The city wasn't a machine. It was a body.

And they were the parasites.

"We have to go," Cassian said. He grabbed Kait’s shoulder.

"We just found her!"

"We found the seal," Cassian said. "And if we don't move, the pressure will crush us."

He pointed up.

Above them, the ceiling of the cavern groaned. A shower of dust rained down on them. The rock was shifting. The sediment was settling.

Vex had started the drill.

The Bore was coming down.

"Run," Cassian said.

They turned and sprinted for the ladder.

Behind them, in the depths of the fissure, the woman in the rock opened her mouth. She didn't scream. She laughed.

The sound was the shattering of glass.
