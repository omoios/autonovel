The dark was not empty. It was heavy. It pressed against his eyelids like the weight of a mile of basalt.

*Dig.*

The command vibrated in the marrow of his frozen arm.

*Dig.*

The needle drove into his left eye. A sharp, hot wire of pain.

Cassian forced his fingers to twitch. Nothing. He was fused. The gray sediment had calcified around him, locking him into the geology. He was a brick in the wall of the tunnel, a fossil of his own making.

*Dig.*

Something struck the stone casing over his shoulder. A muffled clang.

"Harder!" Kait’s voice. Muffled. As if she were shouting from the bottom of a well.

*Clang.*

The stone shell cracked. A fissure of light—purple, bruised light—bled through the darkness.

Cassian gasped. The air rushed in. The sediment on his chest fractured. He heaved, and the gray shell fell away like dry mud, shattering on the floor.

 He coughed, a cloud of gray dust puffing from his lips.

"You're cold," Kait said. She stood over him, wielding a pry-bar like a sword. Her eyes were wide, white-rimmed. "You're cold as the Pit."

"Help me," Cassian rasped. His voice was grinding stones. "Up."

He reached out. She grabbed his wrist. Her grip was slippery with sweat, fever-hot against his chill. She heaved. Cassian pushed with his legs. They stumbled upright.

The room tilted. The purple light from the broken Threshold swirled around them, dizzying. The air tasted of ozone and ancient blood.

"Where?" Cassian asked. He scanned the chamber.

The tunnel was gone. In its place stood a wound in reality. The purple light pulsed, rhythmic, like a heartbeat. And in the center of the light, floating in the dust, was a face.

*The audit is incomplete,* the voice said in Cassian's teeth. *The debt is unpaid.*

The face surged forward, a tidal wave of starlight.

"Move!" Cassian shoved Kait toward the fissure in the ceiling.

He scrambled to the bundle on the floor. Elara. He grabbed her under the arms. She was stiff, heavy, dead weight.

"Grab her legs," Cassian barked.

Kait jumped down from the fissure, grabbed the Queen’s ankles. "She's stiff. Like a frozen chicken."

"Drag."

"The tunnel," Cassian said, pointing to the crack above. "Up. Now."

Kait jumped, caught the edge, and vanished. Cassian tied a rope around Elara’s chest, knotting it fast. He tugged the rope three times.

Kait pulled. Elara’s body slid upward, scraping against the rock.

Cassian waited. The purple light was feet away. He could see shapes in the fog. Faces. Hundreds of them. The silent citizens of the deep, mouths open in silent screams.

He jumped. He caught the rope. He hauled himself up, hand over hand, the gray residue on his skin flaking off, drifting down into the purple fog.

He rolled over the lip of the fissure, gasping.

"Cut it," he wheezed.

Kait slashed the rope. They lay there, panting, in the dark of the upper tunnel.

Below them, in the chamber, the purple light dimmed. The face of stars faded. The silence returned.

Cassian sat up. He checked his Chronometer. The gauge was pinned in the red. *Critical Drift.*

"We have to go," he said. He stood, swaying. "Now."

***

The ascent was a blur of pain and vibration.

The city was waking up. The drills.

*Crr-ump. Crr-ump.*

They moved in a stumbling rhythm. Step. Drag. Step. Drag. The air grew hotter, the sulfur smell replaced by the acrid tang of ozone.

"Status," Cassian muttered. He needed to hear a voice.

"Arms tired," Kait grunted. "She's getting heavier."

"Good. That means you're still here."

They reached the junction of the Rust Belt. The tunnel widened into a graveyard of industry—old pipes, rusted gears, broken lamps.

A figure stepped out from behind a pile of discarded iron-moss.

Cassian dropped Elara’s legs and drew his pistol.

The figure raised its hands. It was tall, thin, wrapped in white bandages. A porcelain mask covered its face.

"Easy, Excavator," the figure said. The voice was synthesized, metallic.

"Sanitizer," Cassian spat.

"Observer," the figure corrected. It tapped the side of its head. "My eyes are elsewhere. I am only the hands."

Cassian kept the pistol aimed at the center mass. "Step away."

"You have something," the Observer said. "It's humming. The whole sector is resonating."

"Get back," Kait said. She hefted the pry-bar.

The Observer didn't move. "High Auditor Vex knows. The gauges spiked in the control room. He's sending the Sweepers."

"Sweepers?" Kait looked at Cassian.

"Cleansing units," Cassian said. "Gas and fire."

"Give us the anomaly," the Observer said, extending a gloved hand. "It will be reclaimed."

"Reclaimed?" Cassian laughed. A dry, hacking sound. "You'd burn her."

"I would sanitize the temporal anomaly. The laws of Ouroboros are clear. History is fuel. If it burns, it serves."

"History," Cassian said, stepping forward, "is not fuel."

He pulled the trigger.

*Click.*

He pulled again. *Click. Click.*

Cassian threw the empty pistol. It struck the Observer’s mask with a clang.

"Kait," Cassian yelled. "Run!"

Kait didn't run. She swung the pry-bar.

She connected with the Observer's knee. A sickening crunch of metal and bone. The figure went down.

"Go!" Cassian grabbed Elara’s legs again.

They scrambled past the fallen Observer. Behind them, a siren began to wail. A low, mournful sound that vibrated in the fillings of their teeth.

***

The Grand Lift station was a chaotic cathedral of noise.

Steam hissed from broken valves. The massive gears of the pulley system groaned overhead. People—miners, merchants, dusters—were stampeding toward the platforms.

"Alert! Alert!" A mechanical voice boomed over the PA. "Sector 9 Containment Breach. All non-essential personnel evacuate immediately."

Cassian and Kait pushed through the crowd.

"Move!" Cassian shouted. "Make way!"

A man looked at Elara’s limp form. "Plague," he screamed. "It's the Rot!"

The crowd surged away, creating a bubble of panic.

"Good," Cassian muttered. "Use it."

They reached the heavy iron grate of the lower lift. The gate was down. The operator was gone.

"Hold this," Cassian said, dropping Elara’s legs. He grabbed the crank handle. It was rusted shut.

Kait threw her weight against the gate. "It's stuck!"

Cassian braced his feet against the frame. He pushed. The muscles in his neck corded. The Nausea flared, blurring his vision.

"Help me," he gritted out.

Kait abandoned the gate and grabbed the crank handle with him. Together, they threw their weight against it.

The gears screamed. The gate lifted an inch. Then two.

Something hissed behind them.

Cassian looked back.

Three white shapes were gliding down the tunnel. Porcelain masks. Flowing white robes. The Sweepers. They carried canisters that vented green gas.

"Incinerate," one said. The voice was flat, devoid of inflection.

"Hurry!" Kait screamed.

The gate rose high enough. They rolled Elara under and dove after her.

Cassian slammed the lock lever. The gate crashed down.

*Thud.*

A second later, something struck the other side of the iron. A cloud of green gas hissed through the cracks, sizzling as it touched the floor.

"Up," Cassian said. He hit the activation rune.

The lift shuddered. The cable sang. The platform jerked upward.

They rose out of the Slums. Past the Rust Belt. Toward the Mid-Levels.

Cassian slumped against the wall. He looked at Elara. She was still gray. Still dead.

He reached out. He touched her neck.

A pulse. Weak. Thready. But there.

"She's alive," he whispered.

Kait stared. "I thought we were carrying a corpse."

"We were," Cassian said. "We're carrying a paradox. The deeper we go, the more the past asserts itself. She’s... re-aligning with the present."

"Is that good?"

Cassian looked at the gas canisters rolling on the floor of the lift below them. "It depends on if the city kills us first."

***

The lift groaned to a halt at the Market Level.

The noise was deafening. Haggling, shouting, the clatter of steam-pistons. The smell of roasting grease and unwashed bodies.

Cassian and Kait dragged Elara out of the lift and into an alleyway behind a noodle stall.

"We need transport," Cassian said. "We can't use the main roads. The Censors will be watching the sensors."

"I know a guy," Kait said. "Toby. He drives a scav-rig. He owes me a favor."

"A favor?"

"I didn't steal his catalytic converter once."

Cassian leaned against the brick wall, wiping sweat from his eyes. "Go. Get him. I'll watch her."

Kait hesitated. "You gonna be okay? You look like death."

"I feel like death. Go."

Kait vanished into the crowd.

Cassian slid down the wall to sit beside Elara. He watched her chest rise and fall. A small, miracle of mechanics and time.

He reached into his coat and pulled out the Key. The stone artifact he had used to open the Threshold. It was cold. Dead.

He turned it over in his hands. Just a rock now.

"You did it," he whispered to the unconscious Queen. "You woke up."

He closed his eyes for a second.

*The city is a machine that eats people.*

He opened his eyes.

Elara was looking at him.

Her eyes were black. No whites. Just infinite, obsidian pupils.

Cassian scrambled back, reaching for a weapon he didn't have.

"Silence," she said.

Her voice didn't come from her throat. It came from the walls. From the bricks. From the air itself.

"Where?" she demanded. "Where is the sun?"

Cassian stared. "The sun is gone. It's been three thousand years."

"Three thousand," she repeated. "The Interregnum failed."

She looked at him. Her gaze was heavy. It felt like a physical weight.

"You," she said. "You are the digger."

"Cassian Varr," he managed. "Excavator."

"You pulled me from the stone."

"Yes."

"You broke the seal."

"Yes."

"This," she said, gesturing to the city, "is a ruin."

"It's Ouroboros," Cassian said. "It's alive."

"It is a parasite," she corrected. "Feeding on the corpse of the world."

She turned back to him. The obsidian eyes narrowed.

"Take me to the Spire," she commanded. "I wish to speak to the Architect."

Cassian swallowed. "There is no Architect. There's only the High Auditor. And he wants to burn you."

Elara smiled. It was a terrifying expression. It showed teeth that were slightly too sharp.

"Let him try," she said.
