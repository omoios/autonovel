The dark was not empty. It was heavy. It pressed against his eyelids like the weight of a mile of basalt.

Cassian tried to inhale. His chest didn't move. The stone had no give. He was entombed.

*Dig.*

The voice vibrated in the marrow of his femur. It was a sound felt, not heard—a low-frequency grind that bypassed the ear drums and rattled the teeth.

Cassian forced his fingers to twitch. Nothing. He was fused. The gray sediment had calcified around him, locking him into the geology. He was a fossil. A layer of sediment to be compressed under the next boot.

*Dig deep, brother.*

The needle drove into his left eye. A sharp, hot wire of pain. Cassian gasped, and the stone shell cracked.

Air rushed in. It tasted of sulfur and ozone. He rolled onto his side, hacking up gray sludge. His lungs burned. The air scraped his throat raw.

"Cassian!"

The voice was external. High. frantic.

He cracked an eye. The world was a blur of purple haze and brown dust. A shape moved against the light. Small. Wielding a pry-bar like a sword.

Kait.

She slammed the iron bar against the gray crust that had encased him. *Clang. Clang.* The sound rang like a bell in the silence.

"You're ash!" she screamed, swinging again. "You're all ash! Don't you go ash on me!"

Cassian grabbed her ankle. His hand was pale, translucent, the veins blue and sluggish, but it was flesh.

Kait jumped back, dropping the bar. It clattered onto the basalt floor. "You're cold," she whispered. "You're cold as the Pit."

"Help me," Cassian croaked. His voice was grinding stones. "Up."

He reached out. She hesitated, then grabbed his wrist. Her grip was slippery with sweat. She heaved. Cassian pushed with his legs. The gray shell shattered around him, falling away like dry mud.

He stood. The room tilted. The purple light from the broken Threshold swirled around them, dizzying.

"Where?" Cassian asked. He looked around.

The tunnel was gone. The basalt slab was gone. In its place stood a wound in reality. The purple light pulsed, rhythmic, like a heartbeat. And in the center of the light, floating in the air, was a face.

It wasn't Elara. It was something older. A woman's face made of swirling nebulae, her eyes full of cold stars.

Cassian’s stomach turned. The Nausea hit him—a wave of vertigo that doubled him over. He gripped his knees.

"Who're you?" Kait demanded. She picked up the pry-bar again, holding it out like a shield. She wasn't looking at the face. She was looking past it.

Cassian followed her gaze.

Lying on the floor, where the purple light touched the stone, was a body. It was small. Curled into a fetal ball. Covered in gray dust.

Cassian limped closer. His left knee clicked, the joint seizing up.

He knelt. He brushed the dust from the figure’s shoulder.

It was Elara. Not a fossil. Not a statue. A woman. Her skin was pale, her hair a tangle of black moss. She wore a shift of rotting velvet.

"She's solid," Cassian whispered. He pressed two fingers to her throat. Nothing. "She's dead."

"She ain't," Kait said, backing away. "Look at the light. It's eating her."

The purple haze was flowing *into* Elara. It seeped into her pores, staining her skin with veins of violent violet.

The face in the air turned. The star-eyes locked onto Cassian.

*The audit is incomplete,* the voice said in his teeth. *The debt is unpaid.*

The face surged forward.

"Move!" Cassian shoved Kait aside.

The purple light struck him. It didn't burn. It froze. The temperature in the tunnel plummeted. Cassian’s breath fogged in the air. Frost formed on his eyelashes.

He scrambled backward, dragging Elara’s body with him. She was heavy. Dead weight.

"Grab her legs," Cassian barked.

Kait didn't argue. She grabbed the Queen’s ankles. "She's stiff. Like a frozen chicken."

"Drag."

They hauled the body across the uneven basalt. The purple light pursued them, a slow-moving tide of luminescent fog. Where it touched the walls, the rock turned to dust.

"The tunnel," Cassian said. He pointed to the fissure they had made in the ceiling. "Up. Now."

Kait jumped. She caught the edge of the fissure, her boots scrabbling against the smooth stone. She hauled herself up, disappearing into the dark.

Cassian looked at Elara. The Queen's face was calm. Too calm. He grabbed her under the arms. The Nausea spiked—a needle behind his eye. He grit his teeth and heaved, lifting her dead weight.

"Kait! Rope!"

A dirty nylon rope snaked down from the fissure. Cassian tied it around Elara’s chest, knotting it under her armpits. He tugged the rope three times.

Kait pulled. Elara’s body slid upward, scraping against the rock.

Cassian waited. The purple light was feet away now. He could see shapes in the fog. Faces. Hundreds of them. The silent citizens of the deep.

He jumped. He caught the rope. He hauled himself up, hand over hand, his muscles screaming. The gray residue on his skin flaked off, drifting down into the purple fog.

He pulled himself over the lip of the fissure, rolling onto the floor of the upper tunnel.

"Cut it," Cassian wheezed.

Kait slashed the rope with a knife from her boot. They lay there, panting.

Below them, in the dark chamber, the purple light began to dim. The face of stars faded. The silence returned.

Cassian sat up. He checked his Chronometer. The gauge was pinned in the red. *Critical Drift.*

"We have to go," he said. He stood, swaying. "Now."

"What about her?" Kait pointed to the body.

"We take her."

***

The ascent was a blur of pain and vibration.

The city was waking up. The drills.

*Crr-ump. Crr-ump.*

The sound came from everywhere. The massive bore of the Sanitizers was chewing through the bedrock miles above, sending shockwaves down the shaft.

Cassian carried Elara’s torso. Kait carried the legs. They moved in a stumbling rhythm. Step. Drag. Step. Drag.

The air grew hotter. The sulfur smell was replaced by the acrid tang of ozone and burnt oil. They were entering the Rust Belt.

"Status," Cassian muttered. He needed to hear a voice. Any voice.

"Arms tired," Kait grunted. "My knees are knocking."

"Good. That means you're still here."

"I saw her," Kait said suddenly. "In the light. Before she turned solid. I saw her face."

Cassian didn't answer. He adjusted his grip. Elara’s velvet dress was disintegrating in his hands, leaving greasy black residue.

"She looked like my mom," Kait said softly.

"Don't."

"Just saying. She had the same nose."

"I said don't." Cassian’s voice cracked. "Don't trust the eyes. The Nausea makes you see things that aren't there. It shows you what you want."

"I didn't want to see my mom," Kait snapped. "She fell. I saw her hit the fans. I don't want to see that."

The vibration in his hands slowed. The needle in his eye dulled to a throb.

"Let's move."

They reached the junction of the Rust Belt. The tunnel widened. Here, the detritus of the city accumulated. Old pipes, rusted gears, broken lamps. A graveyard of industry.

A figure stepped out from behind a pile of discarded iron-moss.

Cassian dropped Elara’s legs and drew his pistol.

The figure raised its hands. It was tall, thin, wrapped in bandages.

"Easy, Excavator," the figure said. The voice was synthesized, metallic.

"Sanitizer," Cassian spat.

"Observer," the figure corrected. It tapped the side of its head. "My eyes are elsewhere. I am only the hands."

Cassian kept the pistol aimed at the center mass. "Step away."

"You have something," the Observer said. "It's humming. The whole sector is resonating."

"Get back," Kait said. She hefted the pry-bar.

The Observer didn't move. "High Auditor Vex knows. The gauges spiked in the control room. He's sending the Sweepers."

"Cleansing units," Cassian said. "Gas and fire."

"I would sanitize the temporal anomaly. The anomaly is causing structural stress. The laws of Ouroboros are clear. History is fuel. If it burns, it serves."

He pulled the trigger.

Nothing happened.

Cassian pulled again. *Click. Click.*

She connected with the Observer's knee. There was a sickening crunch of metal and bone. The figure went down.

"Go!" Cassian grabbed Elara’s legs again.

They scrambled past the fallen Observer. Behind them, a siren began to wail. A low, mournful sound that vibrated in the fillings of their teeth.

"The Lift," Cassian panted. "We have to make the Grand Lift."

The Grand Lift station was a chaotic cathedral of noise.

Kait threw her weight against the gate. "It's stuck!"

*Thud.*
