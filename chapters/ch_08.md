Cassian crouched behind the rusted housing of the ventilation unit. The air here tasted of sulfur and wet iron. He pressed his back against the cold metal, his chest heaving. Every breath dragged a jagged piece of air down his throat.

He looked at his shoulder.

The fabric of his coat was burnt away. The skin underneath was black. Not charred, but *voided*. Black ichor wept from the wound, thick and viscous like crude oil. 

He pulled a strip of canvas from his belt—scavenged sailcloth—and bit down on the end. He wrapped it around the shoulder, over the burn. He pulled it tight. The pain was a white spike driven through his vision. He grunted, the sound muffled by the canvas in his teeth.

He tied it off. Sweat stung his eyes.

The bundle in his hand felt heavy. The Key. It felt like holding a compressed star.

*Thump. Thump.*

He needed to move. The searchlights from the Spire were sweeping the darkness above, cutting through the smog like scalpels. If they found him on the catwalk, he was a target in a shooting gallery. If they didn't find him, the Chronite poisoning would finish the job.

 He grabbed a stanchion to steady himself.

"Focus," he muttered. "Ground."

He moved.

He stayed in the shadows of the gantry. 

He navigated the maze of suspended walkways. The rats scurried in the dark, their eyes glowing with a faint, sickly blue light. They chittered, teeth clicking on the metal.

Cassian kept his hand over the Key. The resonance coming off it was getting stronger. It was reacting to the depth. They were getting closer to the Basalt Threshold.

A sharp, precise piercing sensation hit his left eye. The needle.

He gasped, stumbling against a railing.

*The Under-note.*

He reached the Smelter stacks. Massive, brick chimneys rising like the fingers of a dead giant. A figure stood in the shadow of the fourth stack.

"Elara," he whispered.

The figure stepped forward. It wasn't Elara.

It was a child. She held a grappling hook in one hand and a dead rock-rat by the tail in the other.

Cassian lowered the gun. "Kait."

"Oi, Old Man." Kait tossed the rat into the darkness. "You're late. And you're leaking."

She pointed at his shoulder.

"It's nothing," Cassian said.

"Looks like something. Looks like the void-bit got you." She hopped up onto a crate, swinging her legs. "Where's the tall lady? The one with the scary voice?"

Elara stepped out from behind the stack. She was covered in dust, her white hair matted with grime. She held a rifle loosely in her hands.

"I am here," Elara said. Her voice was rough, stripped of its regal cadence by the dust. "Cassian. You have it."

Cassian nodded. He unwrapped the bundle from his coat.

The Key lay in his palm.

The air around them seemed to darken. The light from the distant Smog-fog bent toward the stone.

Kait leaned forward, eyes wide. "That's it? That's the Queen's Key? Looks like a brick."

"It is the frequency," Elara said. She reached out, then stopped. Her hand hovered over the stone. "It feels... like home. Like the Silence."

Cassian wrapped the stone again. "We can't stay here. The Sanitizers are sweeping the upper levels. They'll be in the Belt within the hour."

"We have a problem," Elara said. She checked the action on her rifle. "The descent shaft is blocked. Vex has deployed the 'Hydraulic Stamps.' They're crushing the lower tunnels to stop us."

Cassian closed his eyes. The needle in his eye twisted. "He's trying to bury us."

"He's trying to bury the Threshold," Elara corrected. "If he crushes the access tunnels, we can't get to the Queen. We can't wake her."

Cassian opened his eyes. He looked at the abyss below them. The Rust Belt hung precariously over the Pit. 

"We jump," Cassian said.

Kait laughed. A short, barking sound. "You've got the Nausea bad, Old Man. We jump, we go splat. That's gravity."

"Not if we have a counterweight," Cassian said.

He looked at a massive crane gantry nearby. It held a suspended shipping container filled with scrap metal. It was dangling directly over a ventilation shaft that led to the Deep Strata.

"The waste chute," Elara said, following his gaze. "It drops straight to the Slurry processing plant. Below the Threshold."

"Exactly," Cassian said. "We cut the cable on the container. The weight drops. The counter-balance shoots up."

"We're not the counter-balance," Kait said. "We're the payload."

"We ride the container down," Cassian said. "Into the chute."

"The chute is a half-mile of vertical iron," Elara said. "It has fans. Grinders."

"Then we hold on," Cassian said. "Because Vex is coming. And if we stay here, he captures us. If we go down, we might survive the fall."

The resonance spiked.

*Thump.*

A sound like a heartbeat echoed from the stone.

Cassian gripped the edge of the crate. "Kait. Can you splice the winch?"

Kait grinned. She hopped off the crate and pulled a pair of wire-cutters from her belt. "Can a rock-rat eat glass? Watch me."

She sprinted toward the crane housing.

Elara moved closer to Cassian. She reached out and touched his good arm. Her fingers were cold.

"You are fading," she said softly.

"I'm stable," Cassian lied.

"You are leaking time," she said. "I can see it. Your outline... it shimmers. Like heat haze."

"I'll hold," Cassian said. "I have to."

He looked up. The searchlights were sweeping lower. The Spire was a crown of thorns above them.

"We need to move," Elara said. "Now."

Kait’s voice echoed from the crane housing. "Hey! The cable is rusted to hell! I need a hammer! Or a big rock!"

Cassian drew his pistol. "I'll be right there."

He took a step, then stopped.

The vibration in the ground changed.

It wasn't the groan of the city. It wasn't the hum of the machinery.

It was a beat.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

It came from below. From the Pit.

Kait scrambled back down from the gantry. "Old Man! Did you hear that?"

"Hear what?" Elara asked.

"The drum," Cassian said. His face went pale. "The Queen is waking up."

"She's not awake yet," Cassian said, his voice tight. "She's dreaming. And we are in her dream."

The searchlights from above suddenly snapped toward them. Three beams converged on the Smelter stacks.

"Contact!" Elara shouted. She shoved Cassian behind a rusted boiler unit.

*CRACK.*

A bullet struck the metal near Cassian’s head. Spark showered his face.

"They found us," Kait yelled.

"Cut the cable!" Cassian screamed. "Kait, cut it!"

Kait scrambled up the side of the crane housing, dodging the debris falling from above. She reached the main winch drum. The cable was thick as her wrist, wound tight around the spool.

She jammed the wire-cutters into the frayed strand and leveraged her weight against the handles.

"Come on," she grunted. "Snap!"

*CRACK.*

Another bullet hit the crane arm.

"Hold the line!" Elara shouted. She leaned out from cover and fired three shots upward toward the lights. Muzzle flashes lit the gloom.

The cable groaned.

*SNAP.*

The sound was like a cannon shot. 

"Move!" Cassian yelled.

Cassian grabbed Elara and threw her toward the crane housing. He jumped after her.

They landed on the sloping deck of the gantry just as the catwalk sheared off. It plummeted into the darkness, taking the searchlights with it.

The container, now free, plummeted down the main ventilation shaft.

*BOOM.*

The shockwave blasted upward. A gust of hot, foul air hit them. The smell of the Pit. Sulfur. Decay. Old blood.

The counter-weight on the other side of the crane shot up.

The gantry they were standing on lurched.

"Grab on!" Cassian yelled.

He grabbed a railing. Elara grabbed his belt. Kait was already clinging to the winch drum like a barnacle.

The gantry was descending. The counter-weight had pulled the release, and now the entire crane mechanism was sliding down the guide rails, riding the shockwave of the falling container.

They were dropping into the shaft.

The darkness swallowed them.

The wind roared. The speed was terrifying. The walls of the shaft were a blur of rivets and iron.

Cassian closed his eyes. The needle in his eye was gone, replaced by a sensation of falling forever. The Nausea took him. He felt his timeline stretching. He felt his body aging, seconds peeling away like skin.

He saw Joren’s face in the dark.

*Dig.*

He gripped the railing until his knuckles turned white.

"Brace!" Elara screamed.

The crane mechanism hit the bottom of the shaft.

The impact was catastrophic.

Metal bent. Bolts sheared. The world spun.

Cassian was thrown from the railing. He flew through the air, tumbling in the dark. He hit a pile of soft, rotting sludge.

*Sludge.*

The smell was overwhelming. He rolled onto his side, coughing up black bile.

He lay there. The silence was absolute.

He checked his body. Arms. Legs. Head. All attached, though everything hurt.

He sat up. The dark was total. He couldn't see his hand in front of his face.

"Elara?" he croaked.

"Here," a voice came from the darkness. Pain-filled, but alive. "Kait?"

A tiny groan. "My goggles. They're cracked."

Cassian fumbled in his pocket. He found a lucifer match. He struck it against the zipper of his coat.

 The container they had ridden down on was smashed flat against the sludge floor.

The walls were not stone.

They were bone.

Massive, white ribs curved overhead, disappearing into the gloom. The floor was made of compacted ash and gray dust.

"Where are we?" Elara whispered. She was holding her arm, which hung at an odd angle.

"The Ossuary," Cassian said. "The deepest layer. Before the bedrock."

He looked around. The light of the match flickered. The shadows danced.

In the center of the room, rising from the ash, was a throne.

It was carved from a single piece of black basalt. And sitting on it was a skeleton.

The skeleton was human. It wore a robe of decayed silk. On its head sat a crown of jagged iron.

Cassian walked toward it. The Nausea roared in his blood. His hands shook. The vibration was coming from the throne.

"Not the Queen," Cassian said. "The King."

"Who?" Kait asked, limping over.

"The First King," Cassian said. "The one who started the digging. He built the city on top of his grave."

Elara stepped closer. "No. That's a myth."

"The myths are true," Cassian said. "Look."

He pointed to the floor beneath the throne. There was a crack in the basalt. A faint, purple light was seeping up through the crack.

The Key in Cassian’s pocket began to burn. He pulled it out. The stone was glowing, reacting to the purple light.

"We have to go deeper," Cassian said. "The Threshold is beneath this room."

"Kait," Cassian said. "Can you fit?"

Kait looked at the crack, then at Cassian. She wiped blood from her nose. "You want me to crawl in there? It looks like the mouth of a dead thing."

"That's exactly what it is," Cassian said. "We need to open it. Use the pry-bar from my pack."

Kait took the iron bar from Cassian’s kit. She jammed it into the crack. She pushed.

The stone groaned.

"Help me!" she yelled.

Elara and Cassian moved to her side. They put their shoulders to the stone.

Cassian’s bad shoulder screamed. The ichor flowed faster. He felt his strength draining, literally leaking out of him.

"On three," Cassian wheezed. "One. Two. Three."

They heaved.

The crack widened. With a sound like breaking ribs, the basalt slab shattered.

A blast of purple air hissed out.

And then, a sound.

*Hello.*

The voice wasn't in their ears. It was in their teeth. It vibrated their bones.

Cassian fell back, clutching his head.

"Cassian?" Elara caught him.

He looked up. The purple light was swirling. It was forming a shape. A woman’s face. Floating in the dust.

She was beautiful. And terrifying. Her eyes were full of stars.

"Who disturbs the sleep of the King?" the face asked.

Cassian struggled to his knees. He held up the Key.

"I am Cassian Varr," he said, his voice shaking. "Excavator. Chronicler. I come to audit the past."

The face smiled. The teeth were sharp.

"The past," the voice said, "does not wish to be audited. It wishes to be fed."

The purple light surged forward. It struck Cassian in the chest.

He didn't feel pain. He felt *cold*. A freezing so deep it stopped his heart.

He looked at Kait. The girl was holding the pry-bar, her eyes wide with horror.

Cassian tried to speak. He tried to say *Run*.

But the grayness reached his throat.

He coughed. A cloud of gray dust puffed out.

He was becoming sediment. He was becoming history.

The last thing he saw was the Key in his hand. It wasn't glowing anymore. It was dead. A piece of cold stone.

Then, the darkness took him.

And in the darkness, a hand reached out.

It was Joren.

*Dig, brother,* Joren said. *Dig deep.*

Cassian’s fingers twitched.

He was buried. But he was not gone.

The earth shifted.

Somewhere far above, Vex watched a gauge spike.

"We have contact," Vex whispered.

He pressed a button on his console.

The drills began to turn.

The city began to scream.
