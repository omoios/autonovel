The impact drove the air from his lungs. Cassian slammed into the sludge, a wet, heavy impact that folded him around the pain. He skidded across the floor of the chamber, tumbling through the dark until he hit a wall. Not metal. Stone. Cold, unmoving stone.

He lay there. The world spun. His shoulder was a white-hot void where the sonic bolt had clipped him, the skin already weeping black ichor.

"Kait?"

A cough from the gloom. Wet, rattling. "I'm bent. But I'm here."

A match struck. The flare of sulfur blinded him for a second. In the sudden, harsh light, Cassian saw the container they had ridden down. It was twisted, a metal carcass wrapped around the base of a massive stone pillar.

And then he saw the walls.

Cassian pushed himself up. His legs shook. He reached out, his hand brushing the wall.

"Get the light up," he rasped.

Kait scrambled over, shielding the match with her grime-stained hands. She raised it high.

The light caught the wall.

Cassian pulled his hand back as if burned.

"Blood," he whispered.

The walls were not stone. They were bone. Massive, ivory ribs curved overhead, vaulting the ceiling like the hull of a ghost ship. Between the ribs, the space was packed with gray ash, layers of it, compressed into a solid matrix.

"It's a grave," Kait said, her voice small.

"The Ossuary," Cassian said. "The deepest layer. The city dumps the dead here when the upper levels are full."

He looked at his hands. The black ichor from his shoulder had smeared across his palm. It was the same color as the shadows here.

Elara groaned. She was pulling herself out of the debris near the wrecked container. She cradled her arm, her face pale in the matchlight.

"We are deep," Elara said. "I can feel the weight. The stone... it sings."

She looked toward the far end of the chamber.

Cassian followed her gaze.

The chamber was vast. In the center, rising from the ash, stood a throne. It was carved from a single, jagged block of black basalt. And on it sat a shape.

Cassian moved closer, ignoring the scream of his shoulder. Kait followed, the match trembling.

It was a skeleton. Human, but vast, the bones blackened by age. It wore a robe of decayed silk that turned to dust at the edges. On its skull sat a crown of iron, twisted like roots.

"The First King," Elara whispered. She knelt, ignoring the pain in her arm. "The one who turned the first shovel. We found him."

"Myth," Cassian said. "He's a myth."

"He is the foundation," Elara corrected. "The city sits on his shoulders."

Cassian approached the throne. The air pressure here was immense. His ears popped. The Nausea hit him then—a needle of pure temporal pressure driving into his left eye.

*Dig.*

The voice wasn't a sound. It was a vibration in his teeth.

He staggered. Kait caught him.

"You're leaking," she said, pointing at his coat.

The black ichor was soaking through the canvas bandage. It dripped onto the floor, sizzling where it hit the ash.

"The Key," Cassian gasped. "Need the Key."

He fumbled in his pocket. His fingers were numb, clumsy. He pulled the stone out.

The Key was dark. Dead.

"Nothing," Cassian said. He shook the stone. "Why isn't it working?"

"Put it on the throne," Elara said. She stood, swaying. "The resonance. It needs the King."

Cassian stepped up to the dais. He looked at the skull. The iron crown was rusted, a band of red around the bone.

He placed the Key on the skeleton's knee.

For a second, nothing happened.

Then, the floor groaned.

The ash around the base of the throne shifted. A crack appeared, running from the foot of the dais to the wall. A hiss of pressurized air escaped from the stone.

*Dig.*

The command vibrated in Cassian's marrow.

He grabbed the edge of the throne to steady himself. The stone was vibrating.

"Back up," Cassian yelled. "Something is opening."

Kait scrambled back, dragging Elara with her.

The floor collapsed.

The throne didn't fall. It sank. The dais lowered into the darkness like a lift descending into a mine shaft.

"Get on!" Cassian shouted.

He jumped onto the base of the throne just as it dropped below floor level. Kait and Elara scrambled after him, landing hard on the rough stone.

They descended.

The walls of the shaft were smooth, polished obsidian. The air grew colder. The smell of sulfur faded, replaced by something else. Ozone. The scent of a storm that never broke.

The Nausea intensified. Cassian felt his skin crawling. He looked at his hand. The black ichor was moving, flowing up his wrist like a living thing.

"Cassian," Elara said. She was staring at his face. "Your eyes."

"What?"

"They're changing color."

The platform jerked to a halt.

They were in a round room. No doors. No windows. Just the circular platform and the walls.

And in the center of the room, hovering in the air, was a light.

It was purple. A bruised, violent violet. It swirled like smoke, but it had weight. The dust motes in the air didn't float; they were locked in a grid around the light, suspended by a magnetic field.

Cassian stepped off the dais. He couldn't help himself. The light pulled at him.

"Cassian, stop," Kait said. She hid behind Elara, her eyes wide.

"It's the Threshold," Cassian said. "The barrier."

He walked toward the purple light. The Key in his pocket burned against his thigh.

He stopped three feet from it.

The light coalesced. It took a shape. Not a face. A figure. A silhouette made of purple dust.

It was him.

The figure was Cassian. But younger. No white hair in the temple. No burns on the shoulder.

"Brother," the figure said.

The voice didn't come from the figure. It came from inside Cassian's head.

"Joren?" Cassian whispered.

The purple figure smiled. The mouth opened too wide.

"I am the past," the figure said. "I am the sediment."

The light surged forward.

It didn't hit him. It passed through him.

Cassian gasped. He felt cold. Not the cold of the air, but a freezing of his marrow. He looked down at his hands.

The skin was turning gray.

"It's draining him!" Elara shouted. She raised the rifle, aiming at the light.

"Don't!" Cassian yelled, but the word was a rasp.

He tried to move, but his legs were heavy. Stiff.

He looked at his boots. The leather was cracking. Flaking. It was turning into stone. The gray sediment was creeping up his shins.

*Dig.*

Joren's voice. But different. Not a memory. A command.

"I can't," Cassian gritted out. He tried to lift his foot. It wouldn't budge. He was anchored to the floor. "I'm stuck."

The purple figure drifted closer. It reached out a hand made of swirling dust.

"You ran," the figure said. "In the Spire. You ran. You left me in the dark."

Guilt. It was a physical weight. It pressed him down, compressing his spine.

"I didn't," Cassian choked out. The gray was at his waist now. "I tried."

"You ran," the figure repeated. "Now you are still."

Elara fired.

The sonic bolt passed through the purple figure like it was smoke. It hit the far wall, chipping the obsidian.

"Physics don't apply!" Elara shouted. She slung the rifle and grabbed Kait. "We have to pull him out!"

"No!" Kait yelled. "Look at him!"

Cassian was half gone. His torso was fused to the gray stone that was spreading across the floor. His right arm was raised, clutching the Key, but the fingers were turning to gravel.

He was becoming sediment. He was becoming history.

The Nausea spiked—a blinding headache that felt like his brain was being pulled through his eye sockets.

*Dig.*

The voice was louder.

"Dig?" Cassian whispered.

The gray stone reached his throat. He couldn't breathe. The air was solid.

"You are a coward," the purple figure hissed. "You dig for the truth, but you bury the lie."

The lie.

Cassian looked at the figure. He looked at the purple swirling shape of his brother.

"You're not Joren," Cassian wheezed. His voice was a grinding of stones.

"I am what you made me," the figure said.

"No," Cassian said. "Joren is gone. This is... this is just pressure. Geology."

He focused on the pain in his shoulder. The black ichor. It wasn't a wound. It was a leak.

He looked at the Key. It was dead in his hand.

"Dig," the figure commanded.

"No," Cassian said.

He didn't dig. He pushed.

He didn't push against the stone. He pushed *into* it.

He visualized the resonance. The frequency of the Basalt Layer. He hummed. A low, rumbling note in the back of his throat.

The gray stone trembled.

"What are you doing?" the figure screamed. The purple light flared, chaotic.

"I am auditing the past," Cassian said.

He slammed the Key against the stone of his own chest.

*CRACK.*

The sound was like a tectonic plate shifting.

The gray shell fissured.

Cassian screamed. He ripped his arm free. The stone shattered. He fell forward, crashing onto his hands and knees.

The purple figure shrieked. It swirled violently, then imploded, sucked into the crack in the floor.

The pressure vanished.

Cassian lay on the cold obsidian. He was shaking. He looked at his hands. The gray was gone. His skin was pale, scarred, but flesh.

He coughed. A cloud of gray dust puffed from his lips.

"Cassian!" Kait ran to him. She skid to her knees, grabbing his coat. "You're cold. You're cold as the Pit."

He looked up. The purple light was gone. The room was dark, save for the faint glow of the Key in his hand.

It was glowing. A soft, steady blue pulse.

"Elara," Cassian rasped. He pushed himself up. Kait helped him, taking his weight.

Elara was staring at the floor where the figure had been. "It was a guardian," she said. "A memory echo."

"It was a trap," Cassian said. He wiped the black ichor from his mouth. "Vex set this. Or the city did."

He looked at the Key. The blue pulse was syncing with a hum from the wall behind them.

"The door," Cassian said. "It's open."

He pointed to the far side of the room. A section of the obsidian wall had slid away, revealing a tunnel. It wasn't a ventilation shaft. It was a corridor. Lined with gold.

"The Queen," Elara breathed. "We are in the inner sanctum."

Cassian took a step. His legs held. He was weak, drained, but he was moving.

"We keep moving," he said. "The drills are turning. We don't have much time."

Kait looked back at the spot where the gray stone had trapped him. She shivered.

"Old Man," she said softly. "You turned into rock. I saw you."

"I know," Cassian said. He didn't look back. He stared into the golden tunnel. "It happens when you hold on too tight."

He walked forward.
