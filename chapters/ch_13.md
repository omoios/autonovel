The stairwell was a throat swallowing them whole. Cassian gripped the railing. It was cold iron, greasy with centuries of unidentified residue. His left knee refused to lock. It clicked, a dry, skeletal sound that echoed in the vertical shaft.

Behind him, Elara moved. She made no sound. The stone steps accepted her weight as if she were smoke.

"You are leaking," she said.

Cassian touched his nose. His glove came away black. Chronite ichor. It wasn't just blood; it was time, sloughing off him.

"It's the resonance," he said. His voice cracked. "The cannon. It scrambled my internal clock."

"You are desynchronizing," Elara said. "I can see the entropy. You are fading at the edges."

Cassian looked down. The stairwell spiraled into a black vortex. No lights. The emergency lamps had died years ago. Only the faint, sickly green bioluminescence of lamp-vines clinging to the upper arches gave any depth.

"We have to reach the Elevator," Cassian said. He slung the sonic cannon higher on his shoulder. The weapon was heavy, a dead weight of tuned steel and capacitors. "Vex is at the bottom. He's trying to crack the Foundation."

"And if he succeeds?"

"He wakes the Mantle," Cassian said. "We all become dust."

He took a step down. Pain lanced through his hip—a sharp, hot flare. He gritted his teeth.

"How far?" Elara asked.

"Three thousand steps," Cassian said. "We used to jog this for sport. Before the Smog."

They descended.

The air grew warmer. That was the wrong direction. Heat came from the deep earth, from the pressure of the city’s weight pressing down on the bedrock. As they climbed down, the temperature should have dropped.

"It's getting hot," Cassian muttered. He wiped sweat from his forehead. It was gray, stained with the dust of the Spire.

"The friction," Elara said. "The city is grinding against itself. The gears are stripping."

Cassian listened. Above them, a low groan vibrated through the stone. It sounded like a beast dying in a trap. The Spire was settling.

They passed Level 12. The signs were illegible, scratched away by scavengers. The door was welded shut, the metal bulging outward as if something inside had tried to push through.

"Don't look," Cassian whispered.

He could feel them. The echoes. The layers of time were compressed here like sedimentary rock. Every step was a year. Every landing a decade. His head throbbed. The needle behind his left eye twisted.

*Focus on the beat. One, two. One, two.*

He missed a step. His boot skidded on loose gravel. He pitched forward.

Elara caught him.

Her grip was iron. She was small, a woman made of porcelain and shadow, but her strength was undeniable. She hauled him upright.

"Stabilize," she commanded.

"My legs," Cassian gasped. "They feel... distant."

"Look at me," Elara said.

Cassian focused. Her eyes were not human. They were black, obsidian spheres that reflected the green light of the vines. They held no pity.

"You are excavating your own timeline," she said. "You are digging too fast. If you continue to bleed, you will erase yourself."

"I have to stop Vex," Cassian said. He pulled away from her. "My brother is down there. Somewhere in the strata."

"He is gone," Elara said. "Consumed."

"No," Cassian snapped. "He's an echo. I can hear him when the drill stops. He's calling for help."

He started down again, faster. If he stopped, he would feel the weight of the three thousand years pressing on his spine.

They reached Level 50. The Mid-Levels. The smell hit him first.

Sulfur. Rotting cabbage. Unwashed bodies.

Cassian stopped. The stairs ended here. The landing was a wide platform, cracked and overgrown with rust-fungi. A service door stood ajar, revealing the slums beyond.

"Wait," he said.

He crept to the door. He peered into the gloom.

The Mid-Levels were shifting. The buildings were tilting, sliding toward the central shaft. The geometry was wrong. Perspectives warped.

"Gravity is failing," Elara said. She stood beside him. "The anchors are loose."

Then he saw them.

People.

They were huddled in the center of the market square, a hundred of them. They weren't running. They were kneeling. Their faces were turned upward, toward the ceiling of the cavern, miles above.

"What are they doing?" Cassian whispered.

"Succumbing," Elara said.

One of the figures stood up. It was a woman, wearing the rags of a merchant. She opened her mouth.

No sound came out.

But Cassian felt it. A pressure wave. A vibration in the marrow of his bones.

"They are broadcasting," he said. "They're broadcasting their silence."

"They are not silent," Elara corrected. "They are screaming in a frequency we cannot hear. They are trying to hold the city together with their voices."

A sudden crack split the air.

A fissure opened in the floor of the market. It wasn't a clean break. It was jagged, tearing through the stalls. A stall selling glass lamps fell into the dark. The sound of breaking glass was like artillery fire.

The people didn't move. They knelt. They screamed their silent scream.

"We have to go," Cassian said. "The structural integrity of this sector is compromised."

"We cannot leave them," Elara said.

"We can't save them," Cassian said. He grabbed her arm. "Look at the cracks. They're spreading to the stairwell. If we stay, we fall."

He pulled her back toward the stairs.

As they stepped onto the landing, the floor gave a violent shudder. The doorframe twisted. Metal shrieked.

"Run," Cassian said.

They scrambled down the next flight. The stairs were vibrating violently now. The dust in the air turned into a choking fog.

Behind them, the sound of the market collapsing. A roar of masonry and flesh. The Silentium, swallowed by the Pit.

Cassian didn't look back.

They ran for ten minutes. His lungs burned. The air was thin here, recycled too many times.

He collapsed on a landing. Level 75. The Slums.

He leaned against the wall, gasping. He checked his chronometer.

The glass was cracked. The needle was spinning wildly, counterclockwise.

"Bad," he wheezed. "It's bad."

"You are fading," Elara said. She crouched beside him. "Your hand."

Cassian looked at his left hand. It was translucent. He could see the brickwork of the wall through his own skin.

"Touch me," he said.

Elara placed her hand over his. Her skin was solid, warm.

"Anchor yourself," she said. "Remember the weight. The stone. The present."

Cassian squeezed his eyes shut. He tried to remember the sound of the drills. The taste of the slurry. The grit of the coal dust.

*Not the past. The now.*

The solidity returned. Slowly. Painfully. The opacity of his skin crawled back like a rash.

"Vex," Cassian gasped. "He's accelerating the decay. He's burning the timeline to keep the lights on."

"He is burning the future to heat the past," Elara said. "A fool's equation."

Cassian pushed himself up. "We're close. The Elevator is at the bottom of the Slums. The Rust Belt."

They moved slower now. Cassian dragged his leg. The sonic cannon was a lead anchor.

The surroundings changed. The architecture became haphazard. Shacks built on top of shacks. Suspended by rusted chains from the ceiling above.

The Rust Belt.

Here, the heat was oppressive. The air shimmered.

They reached the platform.

The Grand Elevator stood before them.

It was a monstrosity of brass and iron. A massive piston that went straight down into the Mantle. It was designed to haul Slurry up from the mines.

Now, it was silent.

The cage was at the top. The gates were open.

"He locked it out," Cassian said. He hobbled to the control panel. It was dead. No lights. No power. "He cut the feed."

"Can you repair it?"

"Not from here," Cassian said. "The motor is at the bottom. The cables are slack."

He looked over the railing.

The shaft went down into absolute darkness. He couldn't see the bottom. But he could hear it.

A rhythmic thrumming. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.*

Like a heartbeat.

"He's down there," Cassian said. "I can hear the drill."

He looked at the cables. Thick, greased ropes of hemp and steel, hanging slack into the dark.

"We climb," Cassian said.

Elara looked at the cables. She looked at Cassian's shaking hands.

"You will fall," she said.

"Then catch me," Cassian said.

He gripped the cable. It was slick with oil and grime. He swung his legs over the edge.

He slid.

The friction burned his gloves. He dropped ten feet, then twenty. The cable swayed violently.

He hit the brake—a simple iron clamp he fastened around the line. He jerked to a stop.

"Come on!" he shouted up.

Elara stepped off the edge. She didn't slide. She descended. She simply let go, drifting down like a feather, controlling her fall with an outstretched hand against the cable.

She landed next to him, light as a bird.

"Show off," Cassian muttered.

They descended.

The darkness was total. Cassian closed his eyes and relied on the vibration in his hands. The cable was singing. A low, mournful note.

*G-sharp. The sound of the city dying.*

"How much further?" Elara asked. Her voice was right next to his ear, but it sounded like it was coming from a mile away.

"We pass the Threshold soon," Cassian said. "The bedrock. The Queen's Layer."

He felt the change in the cable. The texture grew rougher. The air grew colder. The smell of sulfur vanished, replaced by something else.

Ozone. And dried lavender.

"The Basalt," Elara whispered. "My prison."

Cassian opened his eyes. He couldn't see anything, but he could feel it. The pressure. The immense, crushing weight of three thousand years of stone pressing against a thin layer of black glass.

"We're here," he said.

He reached out with his free hand. His fingers brushed the wall.

It was smooth. Cold. Glass.

"The Threshold," he said. "Vex is drilling through it."

He touched the black surface.

*Flash.*

A sound. Not a sound. A memory.

A woman screaming. A chisel striking stone. The taste of copper in the mouth.

Cassian gasped. He pulled his hand back.

"You felt it," Elara said.

"It's... fresh," Cassian wheezed. "The memory. It's not compressed. It's active."

"He is close," she said. "The drill is biting into my tomb."

Suddenly, the cable snapped.

Not broke. Snapped.

A sharp *PING* sound, followed by the violent lurch of gravity.

Cassian fell.

He slammed into the clamp. The metal bit into his ribs. He hung by the single iron hook, spinning in the dark.

"Cassian!" Elara called.

He looked up. She was hanging from a cross-brace, her fingers dug into the stone. She hadn't fallen. She was anchored.

"The cable!" Cassian shouted. "It's cut!"

He looked down.

Below him, faint and distant, a single point of orange light.

And the sound of grinding. Massive, planetary grinding.

"He cut us loose," Cassian said. "He's dropping the city."

He looked at the wall. He was hanging against the black glass of the Threshold.

"Elara," he yelled. "I have an idea."

He unhooked the clamp.

He fell.

He didn't fall far. He slammed into the glass wall. His boots found purchase on a slight protrusion. He was clinging to the side of the shaft like a fly.

"The wall!" he shouted. "It's not uniform! There are fissures!"

He drew his pickaxe. A small one, for detail work.

He swung.

*CRACK.*

The black glass shattered.

A beam of white light pierced the darkness. Blinding. Pure.

Cassian shielded his eyes.

Inside the fissure, he saw it.

Not a vein of Chronite. A room.

A circular room, preserved in amber. A table. A chair. A woman, sitting, her head in her hands.

And a man standing over her. Holding a chisel.

"Vex," Cassian whispered.

No. Not Vex.

The man wore the mask of a Sanitizer, but the clothes were ancient. A uniform from the Interregnum.

"It's not Vex," Cassian said. "It's a memory. It's the assassination."

Elara dropped down beside him. She landed softly on the narrow ledge.

She looked into the fissure.

She saw the man.

"Father," she said.

The figure in the room turned. It looked right at them. Its eyes were white, blind with Chronite madness.

It raised the chisel.

"Get back!" Cassian shoved Elara.

The figure struck the glass.

The wall didn't break. It rippled.

The shockwave hit Cassian like a physical blow. He was thrown backward, off the ledge.

He plummeted.

He scrabbled for the cable. His fingers brushed the greased hemp. He missed.

He spun into the dark.

A hand caught his wrist.

Elara. She was hanging from the fissure, her arm extended, her grip crushing his bones.

"I have you," she said.

Her face was pale. Sweat beaded on her forehead. She was straining.

"He's here," Cassian said. "The echo. It's protecting the Threshold."

"It is not an echo," Elara grunted. She hauled him up, inch by inch. "It is a loop. The moment of my death, replaying forever."

Cassian climbed over the ledge. They collapsed together on the small outcropping of broken glass.

"We can't go through," Cassian said. "The memory is hostile. It's defending the layer."

"There is another way," Elara said. She pointed to the dark below them. "The Bore. The drill shaft."

"If we go there, we walk right into the cutting head," Cassian said.

"If we stay here, we become part of the wall," Elara said. "I will not be a museum exhibit again."

Cassian looked at the orange light below. It was growing brighter. The heat was rising.

"Okay," he said. "The Bore."

He checked the sonic cannon. "We have one charge left."

"Use it on the door," Elara said.

"What door?"

"The one at the bottom," she said. "The door Vex came through."

Cassian nodded.

He slung the cannon over his shoulder. He looked for a way down.

"The service ladder," he said. "On the pillar."

He pointed to a rusted iron ladder bolted into the central column of the shaft.

"Go," he said.

Elara climbed. She moved with terrifying speed.

Cassian followed. Every rung was a test of his grip. His hands were bleeding. The ichor was mixing with the rust.

They descended for an hour.

The air became hot. Unbearably hot.

Cassian's skin felt tight. Dry.

"The Mantle," he coughed. "We're too close."

The orange light below resolved into a shape.

A massive, spinning cone of steel. The Drill Head.

It was silent. The sound he heard was not the grind of metal on stone. It was the sound of the stone screaming as it was forced apart.

And at the base of the drill, a small, white box.

An airlock.

"The Elevator," Cassian said. "He bypassed the safety stops."

They reached the bottom of the ladder.

They stood on a catwalk surrounding the massive bore hole. The hole went straight down, glowing with the heat of the earth.

In the center of the catwalk, standing before the airlock, was a figure in white.

He wore a porcelain mask. He held a tuning fork in his hand.

"High Auditor," Cassian said.

Vex didn't turn. He tapped the fork against the rail.

*CLANG.*

"You are late, Mr. Varr," Vex said. His voice was amplified, metallic. "The audit is concluding."

"Stop the drill," Cassian said. He unslung the cannon. He leveled it at Vex's back. "Or I scatter you across the strata."

Vex turned slowly.

His mask was perfect. Smooth. White. Only two eye-slits, glowing with blue light.

"Do you know how loud it is?" Vex asked. "The silence? I have tried to sanitize it. But the dirt keeps coming back."

He took a step toward Cassian.

"You brought the fossil," Vex said, looking at Elara. "The primary contaminant."

"I am the Queen," Elara said. "And you are trespassing."

Vex laughed. It was a dry, clicking sound. "I am the city. I am the sanitation. I am the future."

He raised his hand. He snapped his fingers.

The Sanitizers emerged from the shadows of the catwalk.

Six of them. White masks. Sonic rifles.

They surrounded Cassian and Elara.

"Kill them," Vex said. "And sanitize the fossil. I want her memory compressed into a standard hour. We need the fuel."

Cassian tightened his finger on the trigger of the cannon.

"One charge," he whispered to Elara. "It's a cone of sound. It'll knock them back, but it'll liquefy my insides if I'm too close."

"I can shield you," Elara said.

"No," Cassian said. "You can't shield yourself."

He looked at her.

"You remember," he said. "You remember the sun."

"I do," she said.

"Then shine," Cassian said.

He pulled the trigger.

The cannon didn't fire a bullet. It fired a wave.

A distortion in the air. A ripple of pressure.

The Sanitizers were lifted off their feet. They flew backward, smashing into the railing. Their armor crumpled like paper.

Vex stood his ground. He raised a hand, and a shimmering blue barrier appeared in front of him. The sound wave hit the barrier and shattered. The shockwave blew out the lights of the catwalk.

Darkness swallowed them.

Cassian dropped the cannon. It was empty. Useless metal.

He fell to his knees. His ears were bleeding. The Nausea washed over him like a tsunami.

He was slipping. He could feel the years peeling away.

He saw a field of grass. Blue sky. A woman holding a baby.

*Joren.*

"Cassian!"

Elara's voice cut through the hallucination.

He blinked. He was on the catwalk. The heat was baking him dry.

Vex was walking toward them. His shield was gone, but he was unharmed.

"Primitive," Vex said. "You use a hammer when you require a scalpel."

He stopped in front of Elara.

"You look tired, Your Highness," Vex said. "Let me help you. Let me put you back in the dark where you belong."

Elara stood up straight. She drew herself to her full height. She looked Vex in the eye.

"I have seen the sun," she said. "I have seen the green grass. You have only seen the inside of a mask."

Vex stopped.

"I am saving them," Vex hissed. "I am giving them light!"

"You are burning them," Elara said.

She reached out. She touched Vex's porcelain mask.

"You are afraid," she said. "You are a child in the dark, screaming for a mother who is not there."

Vex flinched.

He raised his sonic pistol. He pressed it against her forehead.

"Do not," Vex said. "Do not touch the porcelain."

Cassian saw the opening.

He saw the exposed wires on Vex's neck. The power coupling for his vocal synthesizer.

Cassian didn't have a weapon. He had his hands.

He lunged.

He tackled Vex.

They crashed onto the steel grating. The pistol skittered away.

Vex was strong. Inhumanly strong. The Chronite injections had turned his muscles into steel cables. He threw Cassian off.

Cassian rolled. He came up with a rock. A chunk of loose basalt.

Vex scrambled to his feet. He drew a knife. A glass blade.

"You are dust," Vex said.

He charged.

Cassian didn't swing the rock. He dropped it.

He ducked.

He used Vex's momentum. He grabbed the Auditor by the waist and pivoted, throwing him toward the open airlock.

Vex stumbled. He caught the frame.

He turned, his mask contorted with rage.

"I will burn this city to ash!" Vex screamed.

Elara stepped forward.

She placed her hand on the airlock door.

"No," she said. "You will not."

She looked at Cassian.

"Close it," she said.

Cassian looked at the controls. A big red lever.

"He'll be trapped," Cassian said.

"He will be with his work," Elara said.

Vex lunged for the door.

Cassian pulled the lever.

The heavy steel door slammed shut.

The lock engaged with a hiss of pressurized hydraulics.

Vex was on the other side.

Inside the airlock, the alarms began to blare. The drill was spinning up.

"He's in the shaft," Cassian said. "He's in the Bore."

"He wanted to go to the Mantle," Elara said. "Let him."

The floor shook.

The drill began to turn. The vibration rattled Cassian's teeth.

"We have to go," Cassian said. "The bore is destabilizing."

He looked at Elara. "Can you stop the drill?"

"No," she said. "But I can direct it."

She walked to the control panel. Her hands moved over the levers with practiced ease. She had ruled this city once. She knew its machines.

She pushed the throttle to maximum.

"What are you doing?" Cassian yelled.

"We are digging," she said.

The drill roared. The massive cone of steel bit into the earth.

The catwalk shook violently. Rivets popped.

Cassian grabbed the railing. "This is suicide!"

"We are not digging down," Elara shouted over the roar. "We are digging sideways!"

She wrenched the steering yoke.

The entire massive shaft of the drill pivoted. The steel groaned.

The bit slammed into the side of the shaft, away from the Foundation. It tore through the bedrock of the Mid-Levels.

A hole opened up in the wall. A tunnel.

"Go!" Elara pointed.

Cassian didn't argue. He ran for the breach.

He jumped.

He landed on sloping earth. The tunnel was raw, fresh. The smell of raw clay filled his nose.

He turned back. Elara was still at the controls.

"Come on!" Cassian screamed.

The drill was screaming. The bearings were overheating. The machine was tearing itself apart.

Elara ran.

She leaped through the breach just as the drill head seized.

Behind them, a catastrophic explosion of steam and shattered steel.

The tunnel collapsed, sealing the Bore. Sealing Vex in the dark with his machine.

Silence returned.

Cassian lay on his back. He was covered in gray mud. He was exhausted. His body was heavy. So heavy.

He looked at his hand.

The needle on his chronometer had stopped.

He held it up to the light of Elara's eyes.

"Did we win?" he asked.

Elara knelt beside him. She was covered in mud too. She looked like a warrior now. Not a statue.

"We stopped the fall," she said. "The city is settling. It will not crash."

"And Vex?"

"He has what he wanted," Elara said. "He is deep in the earth. He is fuel."

Cassian coughed. He tasted blood. Not ichor. Real blood. Red blood.

"The resonance," he whispered. "It's gone."

Elara touched his face. Her hand was cool.

"You are back," she said. "You are in the present."

Cassian looked around. The tunnel was quiet. Roots were already starting to grow in the fresh dirt.

"I don't hear Joren," Cassian said.

"Good," Elara said.

Cassian closed his eyes. He listened to the silence. It wasn't empty. It was full. The sound of the wind in the tunnels. The sound of water dripping.

"Where do we go?" he asked.

"Up," Elara said. "We go up. We tell them the sun is real."

Cassian nodded.

He pushed himself up. He took a step. Then another.

His leg held.

He walked toward the dark, and for the first time in his life, he wasn't afraid of what he couldn't see.
